Fonterra Shareholders' Fund (FSF.NZ)

Unit Trust — Economic Rights over Fonterra Co-operative Group Limited | NZX Main Board

This is not a normal equity

FSF.NZ is a UNIT TRUST holding "Economic Rights" over Fonterra Co-operative Group shares. One Fund unit entitles the holder to the same dividends and capital returns as one Fonterra farmer-share, but carries NO voting rights and NO control over the underlying business.

Fonterra is a CO-OPERATIVE run by and for its ~8,000 farmer-owners to maximise the Farmgate Milk Price they are paid for their milk — not to maximise group profit for Fund unit holders. The Farmgate Milk Price ($10.16/kgMS, ~$15.3bn in FY2025) is Fonterra's dominant cost line and it flows straight to the farmer-suppliers who control the co-operative's board. This is a structural conflict of interest and it is the central investment risk in this instrument — a high milk price is good for the people who vote, and bad for the people who read this dashboard.

The financials shown below are Fonterra Co-operative Group's consolidated accounts (~1.607 billion shares on the group's economic base), not the Fund's own trust-level accounts (107.4 million units on issue). The Fund itself has no employees, no operations, and no assets other than the Economic Rights pool.

Data quality note: FY2024, FY2025, H1-2025 and H1-2026 have NO Revenue, balance-sheet or cash-flow figures in the chart data below. Those filings are the Fund's own trust-level annual/interim reports, which do not restate Fonterra's full group financial statements — only NetIncome, EPS, OperatingIncome and DividendPerShare are disclosed (sourced from the Chair's Report narrative) and are shown here.                 Dividend Discount Model (DDM) Valuation

                Model approach: this is a Dividend Discount Model, not a free-cash-flow DCF

                This valuation is a DIVIDEND DISCOUNT MODEL, not a free-cash-flow DCF. Because Fonterra is a co-operative that routes its dominant cash outflow to farmer-suppliers via the Farmgate Milk Price, group free cash flow is not available to unit holders — the only cash a unit holder receives is the dividend and occasional capital returns. Dividends are projected off management's FY2026 EPS guidance (50-65c) and the 60-80% payout policy, discounted at a 13.75% cost of equity (9.0% NZ base + 2.25% no-vote/governance + 1.25% Fund-structure/wind-up risk + 1.25% dairy-commodity cyclicality). Net debt is NOT subtracted — dividends are already post-interest. The NZ$2.00/unit capital return paid 14 April 2026 is a COMPLETED past event and is excluded from intrinsic value; the current $7.05 price is already ex-distribution. Result: base IV $4.22 (-40%), and even the bull case ($6.22) sits below the current price. At $7.05 the market pays ~12.3x FY2026 guided EPS, above the highest P/E of the last decade (10.4x).
