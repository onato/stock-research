Channel Infrastructure NZ Limited (CHI.NZ)

Marsden Point Fuel Import Terminal — Contracted Infrastructure / Yield Asset

Structural break, not decline: Channel Infrastructure was Refining NZ, an oil refinery, through April 2022. It then permanently closed the refinery and converted Marsden Point into a fuel import terminal. Revenue fell from $359m (FY2018) to ~$140m (FY2023-25) by design — refining margin/commodity risk was deliberately shed in exchange for high-margin, take-or-pay, PPI-indexed contracted infrastructure income (EBITDA margin rose from ~15-20% in the refining era to ~67% in the terminal era). Every chart below spanning FY2018-FY2025 shades the refining era distinctly from the terminal era. Figures before FY2022 describe a business that no longer exists and full-history growth rates (revenue, EPS, equity CAGRs) are not meaningful — treat FY2023-FY2025 as the only comparable run of "current business" data.

                DCF Valuation

                Valuation Philosophy: Infrastructure/Yield Asset, Not a Growth Compounder

                Channel Infrastructure is valued here as a regulated-like infrastructure / toll-road-style yield asset — a natural-monopoly fuel import terminal (sole deep-water jetties, sole pipeline to Auckland) earning largely fixed, take-or-pay, PPI-indexed fees from three investment-grade fuel majors under Terminal Services Agreements to 2032. The base case (NZ$2.04, -39.7%) deliberately excludes the Marsden Point Energy Precinct redevelopment and Biorefinery consortium (FID expected 2026, PwC-estimated ~$3.3bn GDP impact) as unmodelled optionality — a free call option the company has not yet controlled, financed or timed. The bull case (NZ$3.62, +7.0%), which assumes meaningful precinct/biorefinery progress, sits close to the current $3.38 price. The sanity check passed: the base case's implied multiples (1.07x P/B, 12.5x EV/EBITDA) sit at or below CHI's own FY2023-25 traded average, i.e. this DCF is, if anything, more conservative than the market's own recent multiples — the gap to the current price reflects the market pricing growth optionality, not a modelling defect. Investors who believe the biorefinery FID and precinct build-out will materialise should weight toward the bull case; investors sceptical of third-party-dependent, policy-contingent mega-projects should treat the ~$2.00-2.20 base/weighted range as the more defensible anchor.
