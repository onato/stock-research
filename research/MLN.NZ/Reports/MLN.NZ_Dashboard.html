NAV-Based Valuation (Not a Standard FCF DCF)

Model approach: Marlin Global is a closed-end listed investment company (LIC), not an operating business — it simply holds a portfolio of ~26-29 international growth equities and passes through dividends and capital gains. A standard discounted-cash-flow model built on operating free cash flow, SBC add-backs and a Gordon-growth terminal value is not meaningful here (Marlin has no debt, no SBC, no capex, and reported cash flow is portfolio turnover, not owner earnings). Instead this section uses a NAV total-return compounding model: NAV per share is projected forward using a total-return assumption (split between NAV growth and the formulaic ~8%-of-NAV dividend payout), and a scenario exit discount/premium to NAV is applied to derive intrinsic value — since P/NAV, not a P/E or EV/EBITDA multiple, is the metric that actually clears the market for LICs.

Read the entry price as "this is not a 15%-IRR opportunity at any normal price," not as an achievable near-term target. At today's actual price (~8.9% discount to NAV), the prospective return is close to Marlin's own ~7% base-case total return.
