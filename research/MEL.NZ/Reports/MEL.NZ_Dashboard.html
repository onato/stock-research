                DCF Valuation

                ⚠
                Sanity check TRIPPED — base case DCF values MEL well below the market price. This is a genuine finding, not a bug.

                The bottom-up owner-FCF DCF below produces a base intrinsic value of $2.85 against a market price of $5.52 (-48.3%). Meridian has persistently traded at a rich 13-21x EV/EBITDAF (10-year median 18.2x) renewable-scarcity / quasi-sovereign infrastructure premium that a bottom-up discounted cash flow does not reproduce once realistic maintenance AND growth capex, normalized D&A, and a disclosure-grounded pipeline return are all charged. The base IV implies an exit multiple of just ~11.8x EV/EBITDAF — i.e. the model is asking the market to de-rate to BELOW its historical 10-year floor of 13.2x.

                This model was corrected during construction: an earlier iteration credited the committed renewable pipeline (Ruakākā, Te Rahui, Mt Munro, Te Rere Hau) at a $40/MWh net margin, implying only a 4.3% return on the $1.44bn of committed capex — below the model's own 7.5% WACC, which would mean Meridian's board is knowingly sanctioning value-destroying FID decisions. It was re-derived to a $70/MWh base margin from Meridian's OWN audited FY2025 Level-3 fair-value disclosure of a $59-139/MWh long-run wholesale price band. The fix moved base IV only $2.68 → $2.85 (+6%) because the committed pipeline is a small share of total mid-cycle EBITDAF.

                Investment framing: MEL is best understood as an income + modest-growth infrastructure holding — a ~3.8% largely-imputed yield backed by an irreplaceable hydro asset base — NOT a DCF-driven multi-bagger. A buyer at $5.52 is paying for asset scarcity, ESG/renewable positioning and dividend durability, not for cash flows a bottom-up model can fully justify.
