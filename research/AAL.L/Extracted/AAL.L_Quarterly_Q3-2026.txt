20 October 2026
Anglo American plc
Production Report for the third quarter ended 30 September 2026
Duncan Wanblad, CEO of Anglo American, said: "Operational performance was resilient across the business, continuing
our momentum from the first half of the year despite extreme winter weather in Chile. We are on track to meet our 2026
guidance, supported by the planned improvement in copper ore grades in the fourth quarter. In Copper, Quellaveco
production increased from the second quarter, benefiting from the expected improvement in the ore grades, while record
snowfall and related unplanned maintenance later in the third quarter affected production in Chile. In Premium Iron Ore,
Minas-Rio benefited from higher plant availability, while Kumba's tie-in of its first ultra-high-dense-media-separation
(UHDMS) module reduced production as planned, albeit with sales largely unaffected as stockpiles were drawn down.
We continue to manage controllable costs tightly, particularly in the context of pressures from energy input cost volatility
and higher ocean freight rates impacting spot iron ore price realisations. However, strong by-product credits and cost
performance see Copper Peru unit cost guidance improved to c.50 c/lb (previously c.65 c/lb).
"We continue to make progress on our portfolio optimisation. The sale of our Steelmaking Coal business in Australia to
Dhilmar for up to $3.875 billion in cash is on track to complete by the first quarter of 2027, while we continue to work
through the European Commission's regulatory approval process for the agreed sale of our Nickel business. At De Beers,
we continue to work towards a transaction in a challenging market, having taken decisive action to protect cash and
preserve value.
"Our merger with Teck to form a copper-focused global metals and minerals champion continues to progress and
integration planning is now at an advanced stage. We continue to engage constructively with China's anti-trust regulator,
with this being the final outstanding regulatory milestone required for completion. Expected completion timing by March
2027 remains unchanged."

• Copper production decreased by 5% to 174,900 tonnes, primarily driven by unprecedented weather events in Chile,
  and processing lower-grade stockpile ore at Collahuasi.
• Premium iron ore production decreased by 4% to 13.8 million tonnes, due to commencing the tie-in of the first UHDMS
  module at Kumba, partially offset by higher plant availability at Minas-Rio, reflecting the planned shutdown in the
  comparative period.
• Manganese ore production increased by 6% to 1,031,400 tonnes, reflecting increased operating levels in Australia and
  improved productivity and operating improvements in South Africa.
• Rough diamond production decreased by 11% to 6.8 million carats, primarily driven by planned plant maintenance at
  Orapa and a slowdown at Venetia ahead of the previously announced production pause.
• Steelmaking coal production increased by 53% to 2.9 million tonnes, primarily driven by the ramp-up of longwall
  production at Moranbah North.
• Nickel production increased by 3% to 10,400 tonnes, reflecting timing of maintenance at Barro Alto.
• Production and unit cost guidance remains unchanged for 2026, except for lower Copper Peru unit costs of c.50 c/lb
  (previously c.65 c/lb) and De Beers production of 25–28 million carats (previously 21-26 million carats), with unit costs
  revised accordingly to c.$70/carat (previously c.$80/carat).

Copper production in the third quarter of 174,900 tonnes was 5% lower than the comparative period, reflecting lower
production from Chile and Peru.

Chile – Copper production of 94,200 tonnes was 6% lower than the comparative period, reflecting extreme winter
weather together with planned lower ore grades at Collahuasi and El Soldado, partially offset by higher throughput and
recovery at Los Bronces from the restart of the second plant.
Production from Los Bronces of 42,500 tonnes was 2% higher than the comparative period. However, throughput and
mining rates were more than 10% lower quarter-on-quarter following record snowfall and related challenges with plant
maintenance towards the end of the third quarter.
At Collahuasi, Anglo American’s attributable share of copper production decreased by 11% to 42,000 tonnes.
Throughput remained slightly below the comparative period, mainly reflecting the impact of severe weather conditions
experienced in the Tarapacá Region which have continued into the fourth quarter and the continued processing of
lower-grade stockpile ore and its related impact on recovery.
Collahuasi has now re-entered the higher-grade phases of the Rosario pit, supporting an increase in planned copper
production rates through the remainder of the year and into 2027. Throughput was supported by increased water
availability compared to the prior period, despite the ruling in May 2026 from the Second Environmental Tribunal which
has seen the Environmental Authorization partially set aside for the desalination plant. The operation will continue to
utilise water supply from existing alternative water sources. Collahuasi continues to work in coordination with the relevant
authorities and stakeholders to restart the desalination plant.
Production from El Soldado decreased by 12% to 9,700 tonnes due to lower throughput and planned lower ore grade
(0.80% vs 0.84%) as the mine transitions between phases. Despite experiencing record rainfall and severe weather
conditions in July, the operation effectively managed the impacts through proactive mitigation measures, minimising
operational disruptions.
The average YTD realised price for Copper Chile was 628 c/lb as compared to the average LME price of 609 c/lb,
reflecting provisional pricing adjustments.

Peru – Quellaveco continued to deliver stable mining and processing performance, with consistent throughput and
improved recoveries (85.6% vs 83.8%). Production of 80,700 tonnes was 3% lower than the comparative period,
reflecting the anticipated lower ore grades (0.73% vs 0.76%). In line with the expected grade profile, production remains
weighted to the second half of 2026, with the full year grade expected to be similar to 2025.
We continue to progress studies to optimise the planned transition from the current higher-grade supergene ore to the
hypogene ore. This is expected to maximise value by smoothing out the production profile to provide more stable
production rates in the first half of the 2030s.
The YTD average realised price for Copper Peru was 617 c/lb as compared to the average LME price of 609 c/lb,
benefiting from provisional pricing adjustments.

2026 Guidance
Production guidance for 2026 is unchanged at 700,000-760,000 tonnes (Chile 390,000-420,000 tonnes; Peru
310,000-340,000 tonnes). Copper Chile production continues to be weighted to the second half of 2026 and is subject
to water availability. Copper Peru production continues to be weighted to the second half of 2026, owing to the expected
grade profile.
Unit cost guidance for 2026 is revised lower to c.139 c/lb(1) (previously c.145 c/lb). Copper Peru unit cost improved to
c.50 c/lb(1) (previously c.65 c/lb), reflecting strong by-product realisations and controllable cost management. Copper
Chile unit cost remains unchanged at c.210 c/lb(1) with by-product realisations offset by the impact of challenging
weather conditions.

Premium iron ore production of 13.8 million tonnes was 4% lower than the comparative period due to lower production
from Kumba, partially offset by Minas-Rio.

Kumba – Total production decreased by 19% to 7.5 million tonnes, primarily driven by a 22% decrease in Sishen's
production to 4.9 million tonnes following the planned shutdown of the dense-media-separation (DMS) plant to
accommodate the main tie-in of the UHDMS plant. Kolomela's production decreased by 12% to 2.6 million tonnes, due to
low levels of plant feedstock.
Total sales increased by 3% to 9.7 million tonnes(1) supported by the planned drawdown of finished stock.
Total finished stock decreased to 5.1 million tonnes(1), compared to Q2 2026 (7.0 million tonnes). Stock at the mines was
2.9 million tonnes (Q2 2026: 4.8 million tonnes), with stock at the port flat at 2.2 million tonnes.
For the YTD, Kumba's iron (Fe) content averaged 63.6% (2025 YTD: 64.0%), while the average lump:fines ratio was 67:33
(2025 YTD: 66:34).
The YTD average realised price of $86/tonne(1) (FOB South Africa, wet basis) was 9% higher than the Fastmarkets 62%
Fe benchmark price of $79/tonne (FOB South Africa, adjusted for freight and moisture), primarily reflecting the benefit of
premiums for high Fe content and the rising premium for our lump product.

Minas-Rio – Production increased by 24% to 6.3 million tonnes, driven by higher plant availability following the planned
shutdown to complete the pipeline inspection campaign in the prior year.
The YTD average realised price of $77/tonne (FOB Brazil, wet basis) was 1% higher than the Fastmarkets 65% Fe
benchmark price of $76/tonne (FOB Brazil, adjusted for freight and moisture), benefiting from the premium for grade
(~67%) Fe content, partially offset by the impact of redirected sales away from the conflict area in the Middle East and
provisionally priced sales volumes.
Rising freight rates ($38/tonne as at 30 September, Platts Iron Ore Freight Brazil-China index) continue to negatively
impact realised prices.

2026 Guidance
Production guidance for 2026 is unchanged at 55–59 million tonnes (Kumba 31–33 million tonnes; Minas-Rio 24–26
million tonnes). Kumba production remains weighted to the first half of 2026 reflecting the tie-in of the UHDMS project in
the second half of the year, with sales not expected to be impacted owing to the planned drawdown of finished stock.
Kumba guidance is subject to third-party rail and port availability and performance.
Unit cost guidance for 2026 is unchanged at c.$41/tonne(2) (Kumba c.$45/tonne(2); Minas-Rio c.$36/tonne(2)).

Operational Performance
Rough diamond production was 11% lower at 6.8 million carats, primarily driven by planned plant maintenance at Orapa
in Botswana and a slowdown at Venetia in South Africa ahead of a planned two-year production pause.
In Botswana, production decreased to 4.8 million carats, as a result of the planned plant maintenance at Orapa. Planned
plant maintenance at Jwaneng in Q4 2026 is expected to substantially decrease production levels from Q3 2026 rates.
In Namibia, production remained stable at 0.5 million carats.
In South Africa, production decreased to 0.4 million carats, as Venetia ramped down production and processing ahead
of its production pause. While activities to support the production pause will continue into early 2027, no further carat
production is expected in 2026.
In Canada, production increased to 1.1 million carats, due to the planned processing of higher-grade ore at Gahcho Kué.
As of 16 October 2026, De Beers has assumed full ownership of the Gahcho Kué mine following the enforcement of
security over Mountain Province Diamonds' 49% interest in the joint operation. As a result, De Beers will in future report
100% of Gahcho Kué mine production and sales rather than 51%. Whilst this does not impact global supply, this will result
in an increase in De Beers' reported attributable production.

Trading Performance
Rough diamond trading conditions showed signs of stability during the third quarter, reflecting a more balanced supply-
demand landscape and improved consumer demand across the US and India. Synthetic lab-grown diamonds continued
to affect demand for lower-value natural diamonds, adding pressure in more price-sensitive categories.
The YTD consolidated average realised price declined by 37% to $97/carat, as a result of an 18% decrease in the
average rough price index and the return to a more balanced sales mix (a higher proportion of higher value goods were
sold in the comparative period).
Rough diamond sales in Q3 2026 totalled 9.8 million carats (8.7 million carats on a consolidated basis)(1) from two Sights,
generating consolidated rough diamond sales revenue of $731 million. This compares with two Sights in Q3 2025 of 5.7
million carats (4.6 million carats on a consolidated basis)(1), generating $700 million of consolidated rough diamond sales
revenue.

2026 Guidance
Production(2) guidance for 2026 is revised to 25–28 million carats (100% basis) (previously 21-26 million carats),
reflecting De Beers assuming full ownership of the Gahcho Kué mine. De Beers continues to monitor rough diamond
trading conditions in order to align output with prevailing demand.
Unit cost guidance for 2026 is revised to c.$70/carat(3) (previously c.$80/carat), reflecting the increased contribution
from Gahcho Kué, the impact of the Venetia production pause and ongoing cost control across all operations.
