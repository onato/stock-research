Business Model

    Live Catalyst: Strategic Review

    KMD is conducting a formal, full-scale strategic/portfolio review of its brand portfolio, explicitly assessing divestment of "non-core assets." One divestment (the Thailand wetsuit manufacturing facility) has already been announced. The review is targeted to conclude alongside the FY2026 full-year results on 23-September-2026 — a real, near-term binary catalyst.

    The sum-of-parts tension is the whole thesis: Rip Curl alone cost ~A$350m (~NZ$378m) to acquire in 2019, versus a current whole-group enterprise value of only ~NZ$158m for Kathmandu, Rip Curl AND Oboz combined. Bull case: a portfolio review/divestment realises that gap. Bear case: the brands genuinely earn their depressed multiples and no divestment eventuates.

                Sanity Check

                NOT TRIPPED. Base-case implied P/B (0.180x) is essentially identical to the market's current P/B (0.168x) — both reflect deep, consistent distress-pricing of Rip Curl/Oboz goodwill and brand intangibles rather than a modelling error. Base-case implied EV/FY25-statutory-EBITDA (3.29x) sits at the top of, but within, the 6-year historical range (2.13x–3.33x) — reasonable given FY2025 EBITDA was itself the cycle trough. Implied exit multiples in all three scenarios (bear 0.68x, base 1.31x, bull 2.48x) are all BELOW the historical range, confirming terminal value is NOT assuming multiple expansion.

                The market is pricing KMD.NZ consistent with continued distress / minimal recovery. The DCF's base case lands almost exactly where the market already sits (+6.7% upside) — i.e. the base case is NOT a bull thesis, it largely validates the market's current distressed pricing. The real spread in this name is bull-vs-bear (+241% vs -89%), driven overwhelmingly by whether the "Next Level" turnaround succeeds and/or the strategic review crystallises Rip Curl's residual sum-of-parts value.
