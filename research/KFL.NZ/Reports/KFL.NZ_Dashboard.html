                NAV Erosion — The Core Story

                NAV per share has fallen from $1.57 (FY2019) to $1.20 (FY2026) while shares on issue have risen 81%. Total net assets grew only because new shares were issued via warrants and the dividend reinvestment plan — not because existing holders' capital compounded.

                NAV per Share vs Shares on Issue

                This divergence — falling NAV per share while the share count climbs — IS the dilution / return-of-capital story. Existing shareholders' per-share claim on the fund has shrunk every year new shares were issued below intrinsic NAV growth.

                Is the Distribution Covered?

                The ~9% headline yield is funded first from the fixed 2%-of-NAV-per-quarter policy, not from what the portfolio actually earns. When dividends paid exceed income received and net profit, the balance comes out of shareholders' own capital.

                Dividends Paid vs Income Received vs Net Profit/(Loss)

                FY2026: dividends paid $38.1m vs dividend + interest income of just $10.5m and a net LOSS of $13.6m — the distribution was not remotely covered by either income or total return.

                Total Income (incl. Mark-to-Market) & Net Income by Year

                "Total Income" is NOT operating revenue — it is dividends/interest received plus realised and unrealised changes in the fair value of the portfolio, which swings the P&L from profit to loss year to year. Never plotted on a log scale because it goes negative (FY2022, FY2023, FY2026).
