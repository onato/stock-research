Non-IFRS financial measures
We use both IFRS and non-IFRS financial measures to assess our performance. Non-IFRS financial measures do not have any standardized meaning
prescribed by IFRS and are unlikely to be comparable to any similar measures presented by other companies. See Section 15 – Non-IFRS financial measures
for the definition and reconciliation to the most comparable IFRS measures. Management analyzes performance based on underwriting ratios such as
combined, expense, loss and claims ratios, MCT, and debt-to-capital, as well as other non-IFRS financial measures, namely DPW, Underlying current year
loss ratio, Underwriting income, NOI, NOIPS, OROE, ROE, AROE, Non-operating results, AEPS, Cash flow available for investment activities, and Market-
based yield. These measures and other insurance-related terms used in this MD&A are defined in the glossary available in the “Investors” section of our
website at www.intactfc.com, and in our Annual Report for the year ended December 31, 2016.

     A large loss is defined as a single claim larger than $0.25 million but smaller than the CAT threshold of $7.5 million.
     A non-catastrophe weather event (“non-CAT weather event”) is a group of claims which is considered significant but that is smaller than the
      CAT threshold of $7.5 million, related to a single weather event.

      acquisitions and are therefore presented on a proforma basis.

Section 1 – About Intact Financial Corporation




Who we are:
   Largest provider of P&C insurance in Canada, with over $8 billion in annual DPW and an approximate market share of 17%.
   We distribute insurance under the Intact Insurance brand through a wide network of brokers, including our wholly-owned
    subsidiary BrokerLink, and directly to consumers through belairdirect.
   Trusted by more than five million individuals and businesses who are insured through our multi-channel distribution strategy.
   Proven industry consolidator with a track record of 15 successful acquisitions since 1988.
   Largest private sector provider of P&C insurance in British Columbia, Alberta, Ontario, Québec, Nova Scotia and
    Newfoundland & Labrador.
   Canada’s largest provider of commercial insurance, with an approximate market share of 14% and a leading provider for
    specialized coverages such as Surety, Long Haul Trucking, Farm and sharing economy solutions.
   More than 12,000 employees from coast to coast.

Section 7 – Recent developments
7.1      OneBeacon, a unique pure-play specialty lines insurer
On May 2, 2017, we announced that we have entered into a definitive agreement and plan of merger pursuant to which we have agreed to
acquire OneBeacon, a leading U.S. specialty insurer. The transaction was unanimously approved by the Boards of Directors of both
companies and was approved by OneBeacon's shareholders on July 18, 2017. Approval under the Hart-Scott-Rodino Antitrust
Improvements Act has been received, along with approval from the Bermuda Monetary Authority. All other regulatory applications have
been filed and are in various stages of approval. The transaction is expected to close in Q3 or early Q4-2017, subject to satisfaction of
customary closing conditions, including receipt of regulatory approvals.

Intact's Acquisition of OneBeacon will create a leading North American specialty insurance business, with over $2 billion in annual
premiums. It will combine Intact's leading commercial lines track record and deep data, claims and digital expertise with OneBeacon's high
caliber team and specialty lines capabilities. We expect that this Acquisition will bolster our Canadian business with new products and
cross-border capabilities, and better position us to compete with international insurers. Furthermore, it will provide an additional growth
pipeline in the U.S. and enable IFC to leverage our consolidation expertise in a fragmented specialty lines market. The transaction is
expected to be neutral to NOIPS in the first year following the closing and generate mid-single digit NOIPS accretion within 24 months.

We have secured the conditional purchase of reinsurance pursuant to which a major reinsurer will assume 80% of any negative reserve
development in excess of US$74 million with respect to OneBeacon's claims liabilities as at December 31, 2016. The maximum am ount
recoverable under the insurance agreement is US$200 million and is subject to some exclusions and limitations.

7.2      OneBeacon acquisition financing
OneBeacon shareholders will receive US$18.10 per common share in cash. This represents an aggregate cash consideration of
approximately US$1.7 billion. The Acquisition plus related transaction expenses will be financed using a combination of $754 million of
common equity financing in the form of subscription receipts, approximately $1.0 billion of financing comprised of debt, medium-term notes
and preferred shares, and the remaining balance from excess capital. Of the $1.0 billion of financing, $425 million of medium-term notes
and $150 million of perpetual preferred shares have been issued as at June 30, 2017. Refer to Section 12.1 – Financing and capital
structure for more details.

We will maintain our strong capital position with an estimated MCT above 200% on closing and expect our debt-to-total capital ratio to
return below the target level of 20% within 24 months following the closing of the transaction.

7.3      Currency hedging
As at May 2, 2017 (date of the announcement), we have hedged the purchase price exposure to fluctuations in the CAD/USD exchange
rate by entering into currency derivatives to effectively lock the CAD equivalent purchase price at $2.3 billion (“purchase price hedge”).
These derivatives qualify as cash flow hedges and are marked-to-market through OCI. Given the strengthening of the CAD vs USD during
the quarter, we reported net unrealized losses of $121 million in OCI in Q2-2017.

In addition, on the date of announcement, in order to reduce our book value exposure to the USD post-closing, we entered into
US$600 million foreign currency forward contracts whereby we sell USD for CAD (“book value hedge”). These derivatives represent
economic hedges and are marked-to-market through net income until closing. After closing, we intend to continue to hedge a portion of our
USD book value exposure using derivatives that will be designated as hedges of a net investment in a foreign operation, with gains and
losses recognized in OCI. Given the strengthening of the CAD vs USD during the quarter, we reported net unrealized gains of $41 million in
net investment gains in Q2-2017.

These currency hedges and acquisition related costs negatively impacted BVPS by $0.71 in Q2-2017. See Section 18.5 – Book value per
share.

8.2          Weather conditions

                                                               At a glance


                  Most of Canada outside of the Prairies experienced higher than normal precipitation levels in Q2-2017 which,
                   combined with the late season snow melt, led to overflowing lakes and rivers in several regions. In eastern
                   Canada, April ranked the third rainiest April since 1900. Rivers in Québec and Ontario reached record heights and
                   flows – Lake Ontario water levels increased by over twice the normal amount, and the St. Lawrence river also
                   reached a record level. Temperatures were much cooler than last year in Western Canada, and largely consistent
                   with historical averages in the east.
                  Water and wind claims in Québec and Ontario contributed to the majority of the $105 million net pre-tax CAT
      2017
                   losses in the second quarter, which primarily impacted the personal property line of business.

                  In the first half of 2017, precipitation was above average for much of the country, as late winter snow storms and
                   freezing rain were followed by an unusually rainy April and May. Winter temperatures varied across the country,
                   but cooler than average temperatures in March resulted in a late snow melt, also contributing to overflowing lakes
                   and rivers.
                  The year-to-date CAT losses of $193 million on a pre-tax basis were well above our expectations, and were
                   primarily due to late winter windstorms and spring water events. Several large scale snow events in Western
                   Canada also resulted in higher claims frequency in personal auto and property during the first quarter.


                  In eastern Canada, temperatures on average were close to normal, thanks to a weakening El Niño, but with

We believe our optimal capital structure is one where the debt-to-capital ratio is up to 20%. We generally use financing instruments,

acquire OneBeacon, a leading U.S. specialty insurer. We intend to finance the Acquisition and related transaction expenses using a
combination of $754 million of equity financing, approximately $1.0 billion of financing comprised of debt, medium-term notes and
preferred shares, and the remaining balance from excess capital.

   On May 11, 2017, we completed our previously announced bought deal offering of 4.5 million subscription receipts
    underwritten by a syndicate of underwriters, resulting in gross proceeds of $414 million. Additionally, we completed the private
    placement to three Canadian institutional investors of an aggregate of 3.7 million subscription receipts for aggregate gross
    proceeds of $340 million. Each subscription receipt will entitle the holder to receive one common share of IFC upon closing of
    the Acquisition.
   On May 24, 2017, we completed a Series 5 offering of Perpetual preferred shares (the “Series 5 Preferred Shares”) by issuing
    and selling 6,000,000 Series 5 Perpetual Preferred Shares, at a price of $25.00 per share, for aggregate gross proceeds
    of $150 million. If the Acquisition is not completed, the net proceeds will be used for general corporate purposes. The Series 5
    Preferred Shares will yield 5.20% per annum and are not subject to a rate reset.
   On June 7, 2017, we completed an offering of $425 million principal amount of Series 7 unsecured medium-term notes
    (“the Notes”). The Notes bear interest at a fixed annual rate of 2.85% until maturity on June 7, 2027, payable in semi-annual
    instalments commencing on December 7, 2017. If we do not complete the Acquisition on or prior to March 31, 2018 or the
    acquisition agreement is terminated at any time prior to the special mandatory triggering date, then we will be required to
    redeem the Notes at a redemption price equal to 101% of the aggregate principal amount of the Notes, plus accrued and
    unpaid interest, if any, up to, but excluding, the date of redemption.

Section 15 – Non-IFRS financial measures
Non-IFRS financial measures do not have standardized meanings prescribed by IFRS and may not be comparable to similar
measures used by other companies in our industry. These non-IFRS financial measures are used by management and financial
analysts to assess our performance. Further, they provide users with an enhanced understanding of our results and related trends
and increase transparency and clarity into the core results of the business.

      DPW represents the total amount of premiums for new and renewal policies billed (written) during the reporting period,
       excluding industry pools and normalized for the effect of multi-year policies. This measure matches DPW to the year in which
       coverage is provided, whereas under IFRS, the full value of multi-year policies is recognized in the year the policy is written.

      Underlying current year loss ratio represents our current year claims ratio excluding CAT losses, reinstatement premiums,
       and PYD. Catastrophe events are not predictable, and as such, excluding them provides clearer insight into our analysis of
       current year performance.

      NOI, NOIPS and OROE exclude the impact of net investment gains (losses), the positive (negative) effect of MYA on
       underwriting, the difference between expected return and discount rate on pension assets, the amortization of intangible assets
       recognized in business combinations, integration and restructuring costs, change in fair value of contingent consideration, as
       well as other costs that we do not believe to be reflective of our operating performance. Investment gains and losses as well as
       the effect of MYA on underwriting arise mostly from changes in market conditions, which can be volatile to earnings. We also
       exclude the difference between expected return and discount rate on pension assets, as we believe the gap in these measures
       is not reflective of our internal investment management expertise and management of our pension investment asset portfolio.

      ROE excludes the dividends declared on preferred shares.

      AEPS and AROE exclude the impact on net income of amortization of intangible assets recognized in business combinations,
       integration and restructuring costs, change in fair value of contingent consideration, as well as acquisition-related derivative
       gains and losses, all on an after tax basis. We believe that these excluded items are not appropriate in assessing our
       underlying performance.

      Cash flow available for investment activities includes net cash flows from cash and cash equivalents and the investment
       portfolio. See Section 12.3 – Understanding our cash flows for a reconciliation of this non-IFRS financial measure.

      Market-based yield represents the annualized total pre-tax investment income (before expenses), divided by the mid-month
       average fair value of net equity and fixed-income securities held during the reporting period (average net investments). This
       calculation provides users with a consistent measure of our relative investment performance.
