New Zealand Rural Land Company Limited (NZX: NZL)

Externally managed NZ farmland landlord · triple-net CPI-linked leases · Dec FY end · reporting in NZD

The central question is the NAV discount. NZL's NAV per share was $1.6094 at FY2025 year-end against a market price around $0.935 — a persistent 41.9% discount that has never closed below 0.557x book or above 0.740x book in 5.5 years listed. Layered on top: in July 2026 Kiwi Crunch Farms, the operator of NZL's Hawke's Bay orchard leases (~5.8% of NZRLI LP rent), entered receivership owing ~$300k in rent arrears, and the Board has suspended FY26 AFFO guidance as a result. A further ~7.3% of rent sits with related Wade Glass-linked Central Otago entities whose wider group has been cascading into liquidation — so roughly 13% of NZL's rent roll is currently under a credit cloud.

DCF Valuation

Model approach: a naive NAV-based model (NAV/share $1.6094 with no discount) would print an intrinsic value of $1.61 and +72% upside — but NZL has never traded above 0.74x book in its 5.5-year listed history (P/B range 0.557–0.740, mean 0.63; see the historical table above). Every exit price-to-book multiple in this model was therefore hard-constrained to that observed band rather than assuming discount closure. This is not a standard owner-FCF DCF: it discounts NZL's attributable AFFO/dividend stream at a cost of equity (AFFO is already post-interest, so there is no WACC and no net-debt bridge), with terminal value = exit P/B × projected NAV per share. The conclusion: NZL is an income-and-modest-growth vehicle trading near fair value at a structurally persistent discount, not a discount-closure multi-bagger.
