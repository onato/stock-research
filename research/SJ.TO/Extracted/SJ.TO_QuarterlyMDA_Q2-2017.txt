OUR BUSINESS

Stella-Jones Inc. is a leading producer and marketer of pressure treated wood products. The Company supplies
North America’s railroad operators with railway ties and timbers, and the continent’s electrical utilities and
telecommunication companies with utility poles. Stella-Jones also manufactures and distributes residential
lumber and accessories to retailers for outdoor applications, as well as industrial products which include marine
and foundation pilings, construction timbers, wood for bridges and coal tar based products. The Company’s
common shares are listed on the Toronto Stock Exchange (TSX: SJ).

As at June 30, 2017, the Company operated thirty-seven treating plants, sixteen pole peeling facilities and a coal
tar distillery. These facilities are located in five Canadian provinces and nineteen American states and are
complemented by an extensive distribution network across North America. As at June 30, 2017, the Company’s
workforce numbered approximately 1,895 employees.

OPERATING RESULTS

Sales
Sales for the quarter ended June 30, 2017 reached $594.2 million, up $31.2 million, or 5.5%, over last year’s
sales of $563.1 million for the same period. Acquisitions contributed sales of approximately $16.0 million,
while the conversion effect from fluctuations in the value of the Canadian dollar, Stella-Jones’ reporting
currency, versus the U.S. dollar, had a positive impact of $16.9 million on the value of U.S. dollar denominated
sales when compared with last year’s second quarter. Excluding these factors, sales decreased marginally by
$1.8 million, or 0.3%, as lower railway tie sales were partially offset by higher utility pole sales, as detailed
below.

For the six-month period ended June 30, 2017, sales amounted to $991.2 million, versus $984.0 million for the
corresponding period a year earlier. Acquisitions contributed sales of $38.8 million, while the currency
conversion effect had a positive impact of $5.6 million on the value of U.S. dollar denominated sales. Excluding
these factors, sales decreased approximately $37.3 million, or 3.8%.

Sales by product category

Railway ties
Railway tie sales for the second quarter of 2017 amounted to $214.2 million, representing a decrease of $2.1
million from sales of $216.3 million in the second quarter of 2016. Excluding the currency conversion effect,
railway tie sales declined approximately $10.4 million, or 4.8%, as lower pricing was partially offset by
increased volume due to the early delivery of certain tie orders. Railway tie sales accounted for 36.0% of the
Company’s second-quarter sales.

For the first six months of 2017, railway tie sales totalled $372.7 million, versus $416.6 million in the first six
months of 2016. Excluding the currency conversion effect, railway tie sales decreased approximately $46.3
million, or 11.1%.



                                                                                                               3
Utility poles
Utility pole sales reached $167.5 million in the second quarter of 2017, up 17.3% from sales of $142.8 million
in the corresponding period in 2016. Acquisitions contributed sales of approximately $15.7 million, while the
currency conversion effect increased the value of U.S. dollar denominated sales by about $5.4 million when
compared with the same period last year. Excluding these factors, utility pole sales increased approximately $3.7
million, or 2.6%, reflecting organic sales growth in the southeastern United States. Utility pole sales accounted
for 28.2% of the Company’s total sales in the second quarter of 2017.

In the six-month period ended June 30, 2017, sales of utility poles amounted to $318.5 million, compared with
$274.6 million a year earlier. Excluding the contribution from acquisitions and the currency conversion effect,
sales increased approximately $4.7 million, or 1.7%.

Residential lumber
Sales in the residential lumber category remained relatively stable, reaching $153.2 million in the second quarter
of 2017, versus $152.1 million a year earlier. Higher selling prices due to increased untreated lumber costs were
offset by lower volumes as a result of less favourable weather in Canada during the second quarter of 2017
compared to the same period last year. Residential lumber accounted for 25.8% of Stella-Jones' sales in the
second quarter of 2017.

For the six-month period ended June 30, 2017, residential lumber sales totalled $191.8 million, compared with
$194.0 million for the corresponding period in 2016.

Industrial products
Industrial product sales reached $27.1 million in the second quarter of 2017, essentially stable in comparison
with $27.0 million in the second quarter of 2016. Excluding the contribution from acquisitions and the currency
conversion effect, sales decreased 5.0% mainly due to lower sales of marine pilings in Canada and lower sales
of rail-related products. Industrial products represented 4.6% of the Company’s sales in the three-month period
ended June 30, 2017.

For the first half of 2017, industrial product sales amounted to $49.0 million, versus $53.8 million in the first
half of 2016.

Logs and lumber
For the second quarter of 2017, logs and lumber sales totalled $32.2 million, versus $24.8 million in the second
quarter of 2016. This variation reflects the timing of lumber purchase and resale activities as well as the timing
of timber harvesting. Logs and lumber sales represented 5.4% of the Company’s sales in the three-month period
ended June 30, 2017.

In the six-month period ended June 30, 2017, logs and lumber sales stood at $59.1 million, up from $45.0
million in the six-month period ended June 30, 2016.

Sales by destination

Sales in the United States amounted to $396.1 million, representing 66.7% of Stella-Jones’ total sales in the
second quarter of 2017, versus $361.8 million in the corresponding period of 2016. This year-over-year increase
of 9.5% is mainly due to higher sales in the utility pole and residential lumber product categories, the
contribution from acquisitions and the effect of local currency translation on U.S.-dollar denominated sales.
These factors were partially offset by lower railway tie sales. For the first six months of 2017, sales in the
United States stood at $701.7 million, up from $691.7 million in the corresponding period last year.

Sales in Canada in the second quarter of 2017 reached $198.1 million, representing 33.3% of sales, compared
with $201.3 million in the second quarter of 2016. This 1.6% year-over-year decrease reflects lower sales in the
utility pole and residential lumber product categories, partially offset by higher railway tie and logs and lumber

sales, as well as the contribution from acquisitions. For the first six months of 2017, sales in Canada totalled

Cost of sales
Cost of sales, including depreciation of property, plant and equipment, as well as amortization of intangible
assets, was $495.2 million, or 83.3% of sales, for the three-month period ended June 30, 2017. This compares
with $453.5 million, or 80.5% of sales, in the three-month period ended June 30, 2016. The increase in absolute
dollars essentially reflects the contribution from acquisitions and the effect of currency translation. The increase
as a percentage of sales reflects lower selling prices for railway ties and a less favourable geographical mix in
the utility pole category. Depreciation and amortization charges reached $8.6 million for the three-month period
ended June 30, 2017, versus $6.7 million in the corresponding period of 2016. As a result, gross profit reached
$99.0 million, or 16.7% of sales, in the second quarter of 2017, compared with $109.6 million, or 19.5% of
sales, in the second quarter of 2016.

For the six-month period ended June 30, 2017, cost of sales amounted to $828.3 million, or 83.6% of sales,
versus $795.6 million, or 80.9% of sales, in the six-month period ended June 30, 2016. Depreciation and
amortization reached $16.8 million in the first half of 2017, versus $13.8 million in the corresponding period of
2016. As a result, gross profit stood at $162.8 million, or 16.4% of sales, in the first six months of 2017,
compared with $188.4 million, or 19.1% of sales, in the first six months of 2016.

Selling and administrative
Selling and administrative expenses for the second quarter of 2017 were $26.2 million, compared with expenses
of $26.4 million in the second quarter of 2016. This variation is mainly related to a reduction in profit sharing
expenses, partially offset by the effect of currency translation. Last year’s second quarter selling and
administrative expenses included acquisition costs of $1.6 million. As a percentage of sales, selling and
administrative expenses represented 4.4% of sales in the second quarter of 2017, down from 4.7% of sales in the
second quarter of 2016.

For the six-month period ended June 30, 2017, selling and administrative expenses amounted to $49.1 million,
or 4.9% of sales, compared with $50.5 million, or 5.1% of sales, in the six-month period ended June 30, 2016.

Other losses (gains), net
Stella-Jones’ other net gains of $1.7 million for the three-month period ended June 30, 2017, essentially
consisted of a $2.5 million reversal of a provision for site remediation and a foreign exchange gain of $835,000.
These factors were partially offset by a $772,000 loss related to the mark-to-market effect of diesel and
petroleum derivative commodity contracts and by a $535,000 expense on freight and distribution accruals. Last
year’s other net gains of $94,000 essentially consisted of a foreign exchange gain partially offset by a provision
for site remediation.

In the first six months of 2017, other net gains totalled $1.5 million, mainly as a result of the aforementioned
factors, while other net losses amounted to $145,000 in the first six months of 2016.

The Company’s exposure to foreign exchange gains or losses from currency fluctuations is related to its sales
and purchases in U.S. dollars by its Canadian-based operations and to U.S. dollar denominated long-term debt
held by its Canadian company. Stella-Jones U.S. Holding Corporation, the Company’s wholly-owned U.S.
subsidiary, is a foreign operation that has a different functional currency from that of the Company and foreign
exchange gains and losses on translating its financial statements are deferred in shareholders’ equity. The
Company monitors its transactions in U.S. dollars generated by Canadian-based operations. Its basic hedging
activity for economic purposes consists of entering into foreign exchange forward contracts for the sale of U.S.
dollars and purchasing certain goods and services in U.S. dollars. The Company will also consider foreign
exchange forward contracts for the purchase of U.S. dollars for significant purchases of goods and services that
are not covered by natural hedges.

Income before income taxes and income tax expense

2017, versus income before income taxes of $78.5 million, or 13.9% of sales, in the second quarter of 2016. The

of 28.5%. In the second quarter of 2016, the income tax expense stood at $23.9 million, equivalent to an
effective tax rate of 30.4%.

For the first half of 2017, income before income taxes stood at $104.5 million, or 10.5% of sales, while the
provision for income taxes amounted to $29.7 million, representing an effective tax rate of 28.4%. In the first
half of 2016, income before income taxes was $128.3 million, or 13.0% of sales, while the provision for income
taxes amounted to $38.6 million, representing an effective tax rate of 30.1%.

For the three- and six-month periods ended June 30, 2017, the year-over-year decrease in income before income
taxes results from the decrease in gross profit. The lower effective tax rate for the three- and six-month periods
ended June 30, 2017 is attributable to a more favourable allocation of taxable income within the Company’s
different tax jurisdictions.

Net income

versus net income of $54.7 million, or $0.79 per diluted share, in the three-month period ended June 30, 2016.

For the six-month period ended June 30, 2017, net income totalled $74.8 million, or $1.08 per diluted share,
compared with $89.7 million, or $1.30 per share, in the six-month period ended June 30, 2016.

STATEMENT OF FINANCIAL POSITION

As a majority of the Company’s assets and liabilities are denominated in U.S. dollars, exchange rate variations
may significantly affect their value. As such, the depreciation of the U.S. dollar relative to the Canadian dollar
as at June 30, 2017, compared to December 31, 2016 (see “Foreign Exchange” on page 2”), results in a lower
value of assets and liabilities denominated in U.S. dollars, when expressed in Canadian dollars.

Assets
As at June 30, 2017, total assets reached $1.95 billion, versus $1.96 billion as at December 31, 2016. The lower
total assets balance mostly reflects a decrease in inventory, partially offset by the seasonal increase in accounts
receivable, as explained below.

The value of accounts receivable was $282.4 million as at June 30, 2017, compared with $160.8 million as at
December 31, 2016. The increase is attributable to higher sales in the second quarter of 2017, when compared to
the fourth quarter of 2016, partially offset by the effect of local currency translation on U.S.-based accounts
receivable.

Inventories stood at $754.0 million as at June 30, 2017, down from $854.7 million as at December 31, 2016.
This decrease reflects solid sales in the latter part of the second quarter of 2017, lower untreated railway tie
prices and the effect of local currency translation on U.S. inventories.

Because of the long periods required to air-season wood, which can occasionally exceed nine months before a
sale is concluded, inventories are a significant component of working capital. In addition, important raw
material and finished goods inventory are required at certain times of the year to support the residential lumber
product category. However, solid relationships and long-term contracts with customers enable the Company to
better ascertain inventory requirements. The Company believes that its cash flow from operations and available
credit facility are adequate to meet its working capital requirements for the foreseeable future.

The value of property, plant and equipment stood at $464.8 million as at June 30, 2017, compared with $467.0
million as at December 31, 2016. This decrease during the first six months of 2017 is mainly related to the
depreciation of property, plant and equipment totalling $9.0 million and the effect of local currency translation
on U.S.-based property, plant and equipment. These factors were partially offset by purchases of property, plant
and equipment totalling $20.7 million during the same period.

The value of intangible assets and goodwill reached $135.2 million and $275.8 million, respectively, as at June
30, 2017. Intangible assets include customer relationships, the discounted value of the non-compete agreements,
a creosote registration, cutting rights, standing timber and a favourable lease agreement. As at December 31,
2016, intangible assets and goodwill were $146.3 million and $285.6 million, respectively. The decrease in the
value of intangible assets stems from an amortization charge of $7.8 million in the first six months of 2017 and
the effect of local currency translation on U.S.-based intangible assets. The decrease in goodwill is also
explained by the effect of local currency translation on U.S. dollar denominated goodwill.

Liabilities
As at June 30, 2017, Stella-Jones’ total liabilities stood at $891.0 million, down from $935.5 million as at
December 31, 2016. This variation mainly reflects a decrease in long-term debt, as detailed below.

The value of current liabilities was $156.6 million as at June 30, 2017, versus $122.7 million as at December 31,
2016. This variation is attributable to a $33.3 million increase in accounts payable and accrued liabilities as a
result of greater business activity in the second quarter of 2017, when compared to the fourth quarter of 2016,
partially offset by the effect of local currency translation on U.S. dollar denominated accounts payable and
accrued liabilities.

CURRENT ECONOMIC CONDITIONS

Operations
The Company’s core railway tie and utility pole product categories are integral to the North American basic
transportation and utility infrastructure. Such infrastructure needs to be regularly maintained which provides
Stella-Jones with relatively steady demand for its core products. In periods of economic growth, the Company
may also benefit from additional demand stemming from expansions to the railway and telecommunication
networks.

Based on current market conditions, and as previously anticipated, Management expects higher year-over-year
overall sales for Stella-Jones in the second half of 2017. However, operating margins will remain negatively
impacted by product mix and softer pricing in certain regions.

In the railway tie product category, North American railroads will continue to maintain their continental rail
network, as operators constantly seek optimal line efficiency. Following the early delivery of certain orders in
the second quarter, the Company anticipates that railway tie sales for the second half of 2017 will be relatively
stable compared with the same period last year. Moreover, softer pricing in the railway tie product category is
expected to continue to affect sales and negatively impact operating margins, which should gradually return to
historical levels by the end of 2018.

In the utility pole product category, demand for regular maintenance projects has historically been relatively
steady. Following softer demand in 2016, the Company expects a gradual return to normal patterns in the second
half of 2017. The Company is also encouraged by improving demand for special projects. Operating margins for
this product category are also expected to be negatively affected by the geographical sales mix.

In the residential lumber product category, the Company expects to further benefit from continued demand for
new construction and outdoor renovation projects in the North American residential and commercial markets.
