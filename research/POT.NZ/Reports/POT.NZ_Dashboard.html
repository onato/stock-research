              Recent Developments

                Stella Passage consent risk — the single biggest swing factor. POT's core growth project (385m Sulphur Point berth extension + 315m Mount Maunganui wharf extension, automated stacking cranes, dredging) requires Fast-track Approvals Act consent. The original application was struck down by the High Court in August 2025 (judicial review brought by Ngāti Kuku Hapu Trust on a Schedule 2 drafting/scope technicality). Parliament passed a Fast-track Approvals Amendment Act in December 2025 to fix the error, and POT re-lodged a fresh application in January 2026 — but iwi/hapū cultural-impact objections remain unresolved and a further legal challenge or negotiation impasse is a live possibility, not a tail risk. This is why the DCF carries a 25% bear-case weight (lifted from a 15% house default) — a repeat blockage has already happened once and could shelve the capacity expansion for the decade.

                DCF Valuation

                ⚠ Sanity check TRIPPED — the gap is a market-pricing phenomenon, not a modelling error

                The house owner-FCF DCF returns a base intrinsic value of NZ$4.14 (probability-weighted NZ$4.15) against a market price of NZ$8.115 — roughly −49%. The multiples implied at that IV (P/E 22.4x, P/B 1.24x, EV/EBITDA 14.0x) sit below POT's own 10-year trading minimums (31.4x, 1.47x, 18.0x respectively) — so this is not a component error in the model (base year is correctly mid-cycled on underlying NPAT, capex is normalized, WACC 7.75% sits at the low end of the sanctioned range with no illiquidity premium, risk is charged once via scenario weights). The gap reflects a documented monopoly / council-controlled-float scarcity premium — the same class of finding as AIA.NZ (house IV $1.90 vs $8.76 market price). The market prices POT at a ~5.5–6% forward IRR because the 54% Bay of Plenty Regional Council stake restricts free float and income/passive demand is captive to what remains.
