Beiersdorf’s Shares




Beiersdorf’s Shares

The first half of 2026 was marked by an overall robust yet volatile performance across international capital markets. Geopolitical
tensions, higher energy prices and rising inflation risks led to periods of increased volatility. At the same time, strong corporate
earnings and substantial investments in artificial intelligence and digital infrastructure supported expectations for future corporate
growth and earnings. Monetary policy remained restrictive in light of the changing inflation outlook. Despite the challenging
environment, the major international equity markets mostly closed the first half of the year with gains.

Beiersdorf shares performed well at the beginning of the year. As the first half progressed, however, the company’s more muted
growth outlook increasingly came into focus. Against the backdrop of a still weaker skin care market and a volatile economic
environment, Beiersdorf guided for only flat to slightly growing organic sales growth for the full year. At the same time, the
company expected an adjusted EBIT margin slightly below previous year for the Group as well as for the Consumer and tesa
business segments. The adjustment of expectations triggered a significant negative share price reaction. While the simultaneously
announced share buyback programme of up to €750 million over two years sent a positive signal regarding the company’s
medium-term capital allocation, it could only partially offset the short-term headwinds.

The more cautious guidance was particularly linked to the performance of the core NIVEA brand. In the first half of the year, the
brand recorded an organic sales decline in the mid- to high-single-digit percentage range. Among the key headwinds were
continued slower market growth, trade conflicts in Europe, the conflict in the Middle East and an initially weaker sun care season
compared with the prior year. In addition, the brand’s core portfolio had not yet returned to a growth trajectory.

Beiersdorf is addressing this development through a targeted strategic realignment. Resources are being allocated more evenly
across all categories. At the same time, the company aims to strengthen its offering in more accessible price segments, place a
greater focus on volume growth and further increase local relevance in key markets. In addition, improvements in the efficiency of
marketing investments are intended to increase their impact.

While Group sales declined organically overall, weighed down specifically by the weaker performance of NIVEA, the Derma
business with Eucerin and Aquaphor continued its positive growth trajectory. The La Prairie brand still recorded declines in the first
quarter, largely attributable to temporary headwinds. These factors played a less significant role in the second quarter, enabling La
Prairie to return to growth.

Beiersdorf shares recorded losses over the course of the first half of the year and traded significantly below their year-end 2025
level at the end of June. Nevertheless, the company remains financially and strategically well positioned. Strong growth in the
Derma business, the ongoing rebalancing of NIVEA to return the brand to growth, a promising innovation pipeline and expansion
in key growth markets continue to provide a solid foundation for the company’s future development.

Europe
In the Europe region, organic sales declined by 6.5%. Nominal sales were 6.4% below the previous year at €1,837 million (previous
year: €1,962 million).

In Western Europe, Beiersdorf recorded an organic sales decline of 6.0%. In Germany, Switzerland and Italy, sales remained below
the prior-year level. In contrast, the United Kingdom and Spain recorded slight positive sales development. Key growth drivers at
brand and category level included Eucerin Body, Eucerin Face, Eucerin Sun and the Hansaplast Wound Care category. By contrast,
most NIVEA categories recorded a decline in sales. At La Prairie, ongoing challenges in the travel retail business continued to weigh
on sales development.

Sales in the Eastern Europe region declined organically by 8.8%. From a brand and category perspective, development in all core
markets remained below the prior-year level.

Americas
In the Americas region, sales declined organically by 1.5%. On a nominal basis, sales totaled €1,131 million, 1.9% below the
previous year's level of €1,153 million.

Nominal sales in North America remained below the prior-year level at €493 million and declined organically by 2.4%. Positive sales
growth was recorded by the Eucerin Face and Aquaphor Body & Lip categories. The NIVEA categories and Coppertone recorded a
decline in sales.

Latin America recorded an organic sales decline of 0.8%. Brazil developed positively and achieved sales growth, while business
development in Mexico remained below the prior year. Positive sales growth was recorded by the NIVEA Body Care, NIVEA Body
Cleansing, Eucerin Body and Eucerin Face categories. In contrast, NIVEA Deo and NIVEA Face Care remained below the prior-year
level.


Africa/Asia/Australia
The Africa/Asia/Australia region recorded an organic sales decline of 2.1%. On a nominal basis, sales of €1,145 million (previous
year: €1,215 million) were 5.7% below the prior year. Business development in the Middle East remained challenging due to the
ongoing geopolitical tensions. Positive growth momentum was generated in particular by Japan, India and Thailand. The sales
development in China was also noteworthy and was attributable to the strategic realignments initiated in the previous year. Among
the most successful brands and categories in Africa/Asia/Australia in the first half of 2026 were NIVEA Face Care and Eucerin Face.
The plaster business also developed positively. The remaining NIVEA categories recorded declining sales.

In the Consumer division, EBIT excluding special factors for the first half of the year stood at €632 million (previous year: €691
million). The EBIT margin was 15.4% (previous year: 16.0%). The development of the gross margin was negatively impacted by
transaction-related foreign exchange effects and a higher cost level, which more than offset the slight positive pricing effects. Active
cost management and temporary phasing effects led to a decline in general administrative expenses. The development of other
operating result is essentially attributable to foreign exchange effects from operating activities as well as the release of provisions.
The special effects in the Consumer segment reduced EBIT excluding special factors by €8 million (previous year: a reduction of
€15 million). For further details regarding the composition of the special factors, please refer to the “Special Factors” table under
“Results of Operations – Group”.

Risks and Opportunities




Risks and Opportunities

The Middle East crisis has had a significant impact on the financial year 2026. The resulting shocks to energy prices and global
supply chains are expected to further increase inflation, dampen consumer sentiment, and constrain economic growth in 2026—
likely extending into 2027.

Consumer segment
Direct impacts on our business are already evident in rising material prices and increased logistics costs. At the same time,
consumers are reducing their spending, which is negatively affecting revenue development in 2026, particularly in the mass market
segment. The ongoing implementation of our NIVEA “Rebalancing” strategy, the targeted acceleration of growth in our successful
Derma business, and internal efficiency measures, we are mitigating these adverse effects to the greatest extent possible.
In the medium term, both the frequency and intensity of geopolitical tensions may increase. This could lead to a structural rise in the
general price level, particularly for materials and logistics services. Pressure on disposable income is expected to remain elevated.

Opportunities arise in particular from the continued positive development of our Derma business with the active ingredient
Thiamidol™ in the United States and China. The global rollout of Derma Epigenetics™ in the anti-aging segment is also
progressing promisingly. In addition, we also expect the implementation of Nivea's "Rebalancing” strategy to lead to improved
competitiveness, particularly through targeted measures with stronger local relevance and improved affordability for our
consumers.

tesa segment
The challenging external environment is affecting the tesa segment, particularly with regard to material cost development and
demand in key end markets. Material costs increased as a result of higher energy-related input costs and rising oil prices. Initial
effects were also observed in logistics costs. tesa is addressing these developments through consistent price and cost management,
as well as the ongoing implementation of efficiency measures.

Furthermore, demand in key sales markets remains subdued. With softened market conditions, automotive production remains in
decline, while at the same time, sales volume in the consumer electronics sector - particularly for smartphones and tablets –
continues to decrease in comparison to last year. Muted consumer sentiment negatively impacts demand for non-essential
consumer goods.

tesa is responding to these challenges through operational agility, targeted commercial measures, and investments to strengthen
execution, supported by continued supply chain diversification to enhance resilience and flexibility.


At the time of preparing this report, no risks related to future developments were identified that could jeopardize the continued
existence of the Beiersdorf Group.

Outlook for 2026




Outlook for 2026

Expected Macroeconomic Developments*

Global economic developments in the first half of 2026 were characterized by ongoing geopolitical tensions in the Persian Gulf and
elevated oil prices, which significantly intensified inflationary pressures and dampened economic momentum. Despite higher price
levels, the U.S. economy remained relatively resilient, supported by stable underlying conditions, while rising inflation weighed on
growth in the euro area. Monetary policy remained restrictive, as interest rate cuts are not expected before 2027. Should
geopolitical tensions ease, bond yields could decline and the euro may appreciate moderately against the U.S. dollar.

Economic developments in Germany during the first half of 2026 were marked by heightened uncertainty. The conflict in the
Middle East negatively affected business expectations, as reflected in the continued weakness of the ifo Business Climate Index.
While economic conditions and GDP growth in the first quarter remained slightly positive, momentum is expected to have
weakened in the second quarter due to rising energy prices and softer demand. At the same time, accommodative monetary
conditions and expansionary fiscal measures provided support. Overall, only moderate growth is expected for the full year, while
risks remain elevated.

The euro area recorded weak but increasingly stabilizing economic activity in the first half of 2026. The Purchasing Managers’ Index
(PMI) remained below the growth threshold, although the pace of decline slowed recently and initial signs of stabilization emerged.
While the manufacturing sector remained mildly expansionary, the services sector continued to weigh on overall performance. GDP
declined in the first quarter, although excluding Ireland, the euro area posted moderate growth. High energy prices and
geopolitical uncertainty continued to constrain demand. At the same time, inflation increased again. The European Central Bank
(ECB) began raising interest rates, and lower growth combined with higher inflation is expected for 2026.

The U.S. economy continued to grow solidly in the first half of 2026, although momentum moderated compared with the strong
growth rates seen in previous quarters. Growth is increasingly being driven by a limited number of factors, particularly AI-related IT
investments, while other sectors such as construction and residential investment have weakened. Geopolitical tensions, especially
those related to the conflict involving Iran, remain a risk factor but have not materially slowed economic activity. Favorable financing
conditions and fiscal stimulus measures continue to provide support. Inflation remains well above target due to higher energy
prices but is expected to decline slowly over the course of the year. Overall, we expect solid economic growth in 2026,
accompanied by elevated uncertainty and persistent price pressures.

For the Japanese economy, moderate growth is expected for full-year 2026. Key drivers are anticipated to be robust domestic
demand, rising real wages, and sustained investment activity. At the same time, risks associated with geopolitical tensions, higher
energy prices, and increasing trade conflicts remain elevated and could weigh on economic performance.

Economic developments across the emerging markets present a mixed picture. China’s economy entered 2026 on a stronger-than-
expected footing, driven primarily by export-led growth resulting from the global AI boom and increased demand for green
technology products, while geopolitical factors such as rising energy prices had a dampening effect. Nevertheless, despite
