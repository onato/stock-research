The Medicare LCD Catalyst

Medicare non-coverage of Cxbladder became formally effective 24 April 2025, eliminating roughly 60% of company revenue overnight. Pacific Edge pursued reconsideration and a new LCD request; a Novitas Contractor Advisory Committee (19-Feb-2026) heard strongly supportive clinical testimony, directly precipitating a draft LCD (DL40378) published 14 May 2026 proposing coverage for Cxbladder Triage and Triage Plus — and explicitly excluding competing biomarkers. Triage Plus would be priced at US$1,328/test, a 75% premium to the old US$760. The draft is not final: Novitas has discretion on timing/content and a comment period is underway, with finalization expected end-CY2026 to Q2 CY2027 (outside date 15-May-2027 + 45 days). Management states that if the draft is NOT finalized as proposed, the company would need "significant restructuring and potentially raise further capital."

Company Overview

Pacific Edge Limited is a Dunedin, New Zealand-headquartered molecular diagnostics company that develops and commercializes the Cxbladder suite of urine-based RNA/DNA biomarker tests for bladder cancer detection, risk stratification (hematuria evaluation) and post-treatment surveillance. The company operates CLIA/CAP-certified laboratories in New Zealand and the US and is dual-listed on the NZX and ASX (ticker PEB). Its business has been dominated for the past two years by the loss and attempted recovery of US Medicare reimbursement, which historically represented roughly 60% of revenue.

Business Model

Pacific Edge sells laboratory-developed diagnostic tests (Cxbladder Triage, Detect, Monitor, and next-generation Triage Plus/Surveillance Plus) that clinicians order per-patient; revenue is transactional (fee-per-test), driven by test volumes and reimbursement rates. The company is not yet profitable and depends on US commercial scale-up and reimbursement policy to reach breakeven.

                Valuation Philosophy & Sanity Check

                Why not a conventional DCF: PEB has never generated positive FCF and FY2026 revenue halved on a single payer decision. Extrapolating any historical series describes the pre-non-coverage business, which no longer exists. Value is built forward from the binary event: probability-weighted LCD outcomes, each with its own component-built path and explicitly-modelled dilution.

                The finding: House weighted IV NZ$0.055 (street NZ$0.060) vs NZ$0.25 — the model says the price already pays for LCD success several times over at a risk-appropriate discount rate. Even the bull case is worth NZ$0.092 at 16%, a third of the price. The bear case is a near-total loss (NZ$0.016, -94%). This reads as a binary-event lottery priced above its actuarial value, not a margin-of-safety opportunity.

                What would move it: Final LCD publication (content + effective date) is the single dominant input. As-drafted finalization moves weight from base/bear toward bull. Retirement/exclusion collapses value toward NZ$0.016. Secondary catalysts: Q2-Q3 FY2027 claim-by-claim Medicare receipts, the actual May/June-2026 issue price, and Surveillance Plus CPT-PLA progress (Dec-2026).

                Sanity check (passed, inverted framing): P/E and EV/EBITDA are undefined (negative earnings in every year). Implied base-case P/Sales (5.4x) and P/B (1.4x) sit below the 10-year ex-mania averages (14.5x / 6.2x) — so the model itself is not unusually conservative. It is the market that is paying an above-history multiple: ~27.5x FY26 sales at NZ$0.25, above the 14.5x ex-outlier historical average. To justify NZ$0.25 at a 15-16% required return needs ~NZ$60-70m of FY2036 owner FCF (~NZ$220m+ revenue, ~2x the model's bull case) — or a sub-10% discount rate on the bull path.
