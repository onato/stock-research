Redispatch cost

 What is redispatch?
 • Operators of renewables capacity feed their production into the grid networks
 • Under times of network overload (lots of renewables feeding in simultaneously), TSOs and/or DSOs instruct producers of electricity to reduce or to switch off
   their production
 • Producers get reimbursed for the time of reduced feed-in at current market prices


  What is happening in 2023?
  • At the end of each year, network operators estimate the volume and costs of redispatch measures
    for the following year and include these in the network charge calculation
  • Usually, price and volume assumptions match the outturn relatively closely, making redispatch a
                                                                                                                       >100%
    pure pass-through
  • For 2023, redispatch price assumptions were fixed during times of high power prices (Q4/2022)               100%            100%   <100% <100% <100%
    which reduced since then
  • Prices for Q1 2023 turned out lower, leading to lower redispatch cost vs. what was assumed in
    allowed revenues
  • This leads to temporary positive EBITDA effects
  • Those run via the regulatory account; will be recouped with t+3 over 3 years and returned to grid
