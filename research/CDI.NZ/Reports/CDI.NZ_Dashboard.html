⚠ Model approach: why this is not a standard FCF DCF

Reported operating cash flow has been structurally negative for three straight years (FY2023 -$10.3m, FY2024 -$8.1m, FY2025 -$9.0m) because cash is continuously reinvested into growing the development-property land bank (inventory build) — growth capex disguised as working capital, not distress. A Gordon-growth FCF DCF off that series would be meaningless for this business.

Instead, this model blends two legs, weighted 50/50: an NTA/book-value leg (Year-5 book NTA/share capitalised at a market-observed exit P/B) and an earnings-power leg (Year-5 NPAT capitalised at an exit P/E), each also discounting projected dividends along the way. Exit multiples for both legs are anchored to CDI's own 2017-2025 trading history rather than an assumed re-rating — specifically the depressed FY2022-2025 regime (P/B 0.69x-0.74x), not the FY2017-2021 boom-era range (P/B 1.07x-1.40x), because management has explicitly guided that boom-era conditions have not returned.

The discount rate is stacked to 17% WACC (11.5% NZ small-cap base + 1.5pp cyclicality + 2.0pp illiquidity + 1.25pp control + 0.75pp land concentration) to reflect CDI's extreme 3.1x peak-to-trough NPAT swing, thin ~27% free float, MCK/Hong Leong majority control removing takeover optionality, and concentrated landholdings.

The model's built-in sanity check PASSED — the base-case implied P/B (0.35x) sits below even the stock's own historical trading floor, and the implied forward P/E (13.7x) sits well below history — so the discount-rate mechanics are not manufacturing an implausible valuation.

Conclusion: at a risk-appropriate 17% discount rate, CDI's persistent ~0.59-0.71x discount to book looks like a fair reflection of its cyclicality, illiquidity and governance structure rather than a straightforward mispricing. All three scenarios here (base -39.1%, bull -10.5%, bear -62.0%) show negative upside to the current $0.65 price — this is presented honestly as a valuation conclusion, not styled as a buy signal.
