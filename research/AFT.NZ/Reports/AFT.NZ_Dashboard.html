Company Overview

AFT Pharmaceuticals is a New Zealand-headquartered specialty pharmaceutical company that develops, in-licenses, and distributes a portfolio of over 150 pharmaceutical products across seven therapeutic categories: pain, eyecare, vitamins and minerals, allergy, gastrointestinal, dermatology, and hospital injectables.

Flagship franchise Maxigesic (patented ibuprofen/paracetamol combination tablet and IV formulation) is licensed or sold in 87 countries. Direct sales forces in Australia and New Zealand generate the majority of revenue and profit, complemented by investment-phase international hubs in UK, Europe, North America, and South Africa. Dual-listed on NZX (AFT) and ASX (AFP).

FY2026: Record revenue NZ$254.7m (+22%), operating profit NZ$24.4m (above NZ$20-24m guidance), NPAT NZ$14.1m (+24%). Products now in 87 countries (up from 80 in FY2025).

Business Model

Three revenue channels: direct product sales in Australasia (~83% of FY2026 revenue, highly profitable core), product sales and royalties from international hubs, and milestone/royalty income from out-licensing IP to global partners. The profitable Australasian engine funds investment-phase international expansion and a growing R&D pipeline.

Competitive Position

Competitors: Reckitt (Nuromol), Haleon/GSK (Panadol, Voltaren), J&J (Tylenol, Motrin), Bayer, Pfizer Consumer, Chemist Warehouse private label

Moat factors:

  • Patent portfolio: Maxigesic tablet and IV patents extend to 2032-2044; European Maxigesic IV patents granted 2025; new IV iron patents filed in FY2026
  • Global licensing network: Maxigesic licensed in 100+ countries, approved in 50+, launched in 30+ markets - compounding royalty stream
  • Regulatory approvals: Maxigesic IV approved in US, UK, AU, Canada, EU - high barrier to replication for combination IV pain products
  • CMS J-code: Permanent HCPCS J-code for US hospital reimbursement removes key adoption barrier for Hikma promotion
  • Australasian distribution: Long-term relationships with Chemist Warehouse, pharmacy chains, and hospital formularies

Growth Drivers

  • Maxigesic IV US hospital channel: permanent CMS J-code removes reimbursement barrier; Hikma stepping up promotion in FY2027; opioid crisis structural tailwind (~81,700 US overdose deaths in 2024)
  • International hub earnings inflection: UK and South Africa expected to contribute positively in FY2027; European injectable portfolio (acquired 2025) launching 1H FY2027
  • Combogesic Rapid US distribution via Mark Cuban's Cost Plus Drugs (all 50 states); Amazon expansion for consumer OTC range
  • R&D pipeline: IV iron Phase III (~1,360 patients globally, US$7.4B market 2033), antibiotic eyedrop (FDA IND advancing), Stablepharma thermostable injectables (US$6B+ market), Pascomer vascular birthmarks
  • South Africa: Pharma Dynamics acquisition added 13 products; now 18 SA products (vs 4 in Sept 2025)
  • New markets: 87 countries (up from 80 in FY2025); Thailand and Taiwan added FY2026; Grand Life Sciences China IV iron deal
  • Secular tailwinds: Ageing populations, self-medication trends, OTC market ~5-7% CAGR Australia/NZ, Rx-to-OTC down-scheduling

Key Risks

  • Moderate: Licensing income lumpiness (NZ$8.5m FY2024 vs. NZ$0.7m FY2025 vs. NZ$3.1m FY2026) creates earnings volatility and quality questions
  • Moderate: International hub execution - collective operating loss NZ$6.0m in FY2026; delays extend earnings drag
  • Moderate: CFO succession disruption - two-step transition (Tubby retired May 2026; Bosley did not proceed; Houliston appointed June 2026)
  • Moderate: Rising net debt NZ$38.7m at March 2026 (vs. NZ$14.5m in FY2025) - inventory build, SA acquisition, R&D ramp to NZ$25m
  • Moderate: Competition from Reckitt (Nuromol directly competes with Maxigesic in Australasia); Chemist Warehouse/Sigma merger creates more concentrated buyer
  • Moderate: R&D pipeline failure - Phase III programmes could fail trials or miss regulatory approvals
  • Moderate: Currency risk - ~59% of revenue in AUD; also USD, GBP, EUR, KRW, CNY exposure; FX compressed FY2026 gross margin
  • Moderate: Key person dependency on CEO Dr Hartley Atkinson (co-founder, central to strategy and licensing deal-making)
  • Low: Consumer spending slowdown (pain relief and essential health products have relatively inelastic demand)

Bull Case

  • FY2026 above-guidance execution (NZ$24.4m vs. NZ$20-24m range) with 22% revenue growth demonstrates consistent track record; FY2027 NZ$300m+ target supported by multiple independent growth levers
  • Maxigesic IV US hospital: permanent CMS J-code accelerates Hikma formulary wins in the world's largest hospital market during the opioid crisis - structural non-opioid tailwind
  • International hub earnings inflection in FY2027 validates the Australasian hub replication model - UK and SA profitable after multi-year investment losses
  • R&D pipeline asymmetric upside not in DCF: IV iron (US$7.4B market), Stablepharma (US$6B+), antibiotic eyedrop - each opportunity alone exceeds current ~NZ$385m market cap if successful
  • Analyst consensus Buy with average target ~NZ$4.65 (vs. NZ$3.67 current), implying 27% upside. High insider ownership ~69% fully aligns management with shareholders

Bear Case

  • NZ$300m FY2027 revenue target requires 18%+ growth but international hubs have not yet contributed to earnings despite multi-year investment; new CFO leadership adds execution risk at peak investment phase
  • CFO two-step succession (Tubby/Bosley/Houliston) introduces financial leadership continuity risk at exactly the wrong time - R&D rising to NZ$25m and multiple hub integrations underway
  • R&D (NZ$25m) plus international hub losses consume Australasian cash generation; near-term FCF constrained; dividend growth limited
  • Licensing income remains structurally volatile and unpredictable; excluding licensing, operating profit is more modest and ex-licensing P/E is significantly higher than headline
  • Small-cap illiquidity (~NZ$385m market cap); limited institutional coverage; prolonged investment phase with rising net debt could compress valuation multiple if FY2027 earnings disappoint
