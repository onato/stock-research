                DCF Valuation

                ⚠ Model Assumptions to Understand Before Trusting These Numbers

                1. The 17% WACC is a deliberate stack, not a default. It is built from an 11.5% NZ small-cap base + 2.5% nano-cap illiquidity premium (market cap is only ~NZ$11m) + 1.75% register-concentration premium (Mason Roberts Holdings / Josef Roberts control ~66% of shares, and the 2024 capital return was litigated by a minority holder) + 1.25% single-market discretionary-hospitality cyclicality premium.
                2. The base case relies on multiple expansion, not growth, for most of the upside. It assumes the EV/owner-FCF multiple re-rates from today's ~2.7x to an implied exit multiple of ~6.8x, while revenue is modelled at just 1.0-1.5%/yr. If the market's illiquidity discount never closes, the 82.7% upside does not materialise even if the underlying business performs exactly as modelled.
                3. FY2026's 91.8% profit jump is a normalization, not an inflection. It is flattered by the ABSENCE of FY2024/FY2025 legal costs (~$206k and ~$222k, tied to contested shareholder litigation) plus a one-off $288k gain on the December-2025 sale of the company-owned Ponsonby store. It should be read as a clean year following two depressed ones. Note also that the model's projected Year-1 (FY2027) owner FCF of $2.20m sits roughly 15% above FY2026's actual owner FCF of $1.915m on an assumed margin path — a further, separate assumption layered on top of the multiple re-rating.
