    〇 Eisai Co., Ltd. (“the Company”) and its affiliates (collectively referred to as “the Group”)
       recorded the following consolidated financial results for the nine-month period ended
       December 31, 2019.

       Revenue:                                ¥486,065 million    (up 4.0% year on year)
       Operating profit:                        ¥73,338 million    (up 28.4% year on year)
       Profit before income taxes:              ¥75,809 million    (up 27.5% year on year)

       Profit for the period:                   ¥73,883 million    (up 68.9% year on year)

    〇 Revenue increased mainly due to anticancer agent Lenvima demonstrating significant
       growth continuously, absorbing the factors, that contribute to decrease in revenue,
       including transfer of the generic pharmaceutical subsidiary in April 2019. The Company
       recorded milestone payments of ¥16,434 million (¥25,008 million in the same period of the
       previous fiscal year) from Merck & Co., Inc., Kenilworth, N.J., U.S.A. as revenue.
    〇 Combined revenue from all four global brands increased by 44.5% year on year to ¥134,418
       million. This consists of ¥80,522 million (up 85.8% year on year) from Lenvima, ¥30,555
       million (down 2.4% year on year) from anticancer agent Halaven, ¥18,736 million (up 30.6%
       year on year) from antiepileptic agent Fycompa, and ¥4,605 million (up 13.8% year on year)
       from antiobesity agent BELVIQ.
    〇 Although partnership model had contributed to cost reduction, research and development
       expenses increased mainly due to the aggressive resource investment in anti-amyloid beta
       protofibril antibody BAN2401 developing as a next generation disease modifying treatment
       for Alzheimer’s disease and Lenvima developing its combination therapy with Merck & Co.,
       Inc., Kenilworth, N.J., U.S.A.’s anti-PD-1 antibody pembrolizumab.
    〇 Selling, general and administrative expenses increased mainly due to the increase in
       shared profit paid to Merck & Co., Inc., Kenilworth, N.J., U.S.A. following Lenvima’s revenue
       growth.
    〇 As a result of the above, operating profit increased significantly by 28.4% year on year. In
       addition, income taxes decreased due to a reversal of provisions in accounting on income
       taxes in the U.S. as well as a decrease in income taxes at the Company following a
       repayment of paid-in capital from a U.S. subsidiary to the Company in order to resolve cash
       imbalance in the Group. As a result, profit for the period further increased by 68.9%.

[Performance by Segment]
  (Revenue for each segment indicates revenue from external customers)
 The Group’s business is comprised of pharmaceutical business and other business. The
 pharmaceutical business is organized into the following six reporting segments in this report:
 Japan, Americas (North America), China, EMEA (Europe, the Middle East, Africa, and
 Oceania), Asia and Latin America (primarily South Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong, India, ASEAN,
 Central and South America) and OTC and others (Japan). Effective from this fiscal year, aiming
 for more flexible strategy execution, the Group separated OTC and others from Japan
 pharmaceutical business, to newly establish the OTC and others business. The year on year
 changes in the segment information for this report are based on this new segment information.


 <Japan pharmaceutical business>
 〇 Total revenue came to ¥193,174 million (down 12.2% year on year) and segment profit was
     ¥76,493 million (down 9.6% year on year). Revenue decreased mainly due to the transfer
     of generic pharmaceutical subsidiary.
 〇 Regarding revenue from neurology products, co-promotion revenue for Lyrica, a pain
     treatment being co-promoted with Pfizer Japan Inc., totaled ¥22,068 million (up 1.9% year
     on year), revenue for insomnia treatment Lunesta totaled ¥9,819 million (up 11.7% year on
     year), and revenue for Fycompa totaled ¥3,002 million (up 32.4% year on year), each
     achieving growth. Aricept, a treatment for Alzheimer’s disease, recorded revenue of
     ¥10,984 million (down 25.7% year on year). Among oncology products, Lenvima achieved
     significant growth with revenue of ¥10,229 million (up 37.9% year on year), and Halaven
     earned revenue of ¥7,312 million (down 2.8% year on year). Fully human anti-TNF-α
     monoclonal antibody Humira showed growth, earning revenue of ¥39,289 million (up 5.7%
     year on year).
 〇 Parkinson’s disease treatment Equfina was launched in November 2019.


 <Americas pharmaceutical business>
 〇 Revenue totaled ¥91,997 million (up 33.6% year on year), with segment profit of ¥49,844
     million (up 68.1% year on year).
 〇 Regarding revenue from neurology products, revenue for antiepileptic agent Banzel was
     ¥16,999 million (up 29.4% year on year), Fycompa was ¥9,760 million (up 41.5% year on
     year), each achieving significant growth. Revenue for BELVIQ came to ¥3,081 million (up
     4.2% year on year). Among oncology products, Lenvima achieved significant growth,
     recording ¥47,113 million (up 86.0% year on year). Revenue for Halaven came to ¥11,205
     million (down 9.8% year on year).

(2) Financial Position
 [Assets, Liabilities, and Equity]
  〇 Total assets as of the end of the period amounted to ¥1,051,383 million (down ¥20,136
    million from the end of the previous fiscal year). Although, trade and other receivables
      increased following the increase in revenue, as well as property, plant and equipment
      increased following the application of IFRS 16 “Leases”, cash and cash equivalents
      decreased mainly due to the repayment of long-term borrowings and payment of trade and
      other payables. Also, the amount assets held for sale decreased due to the transfer of
      generic pharmaceutical subsidiary.
  〇 Total liabilities as of the end of the period amounted to ¥382,369 million (down ¥37,169
      million from the end of the previous fiscal year). Although other financial liabilities increased
      following the application of IFRS 16 “Leases”, long-term borrowings as well as trade and
      other payables decreased. At the same time, liabilities directly associated with assets held
      for sale also decreased due to the transfer of generic pharmaceutical subsidiary.
  〇 Total equity as of the end of the period amounted to ¥669,014 million (up ¥17,033 million
    from the end of the previous fiscal year) due to recording profit for the period in significant
      excess of the payments for dividends.
  〇 As a result of the above, the ratio of equity attributable to owners of the parent was 61.3%
      (up 2.7 percentage points from the end of the previous fiscal year).



[Cash Flows]
  〇 Net cash from operating activities amounted to an inflow of ¥29,217 million (down ¥31,253
    million from the same period of the previous fiscal year). Profit before income taxes
      increased, while working capital increased due to an increase in trade receivables following
      an increase in revenue and the amortization of deposits received (reimbursement for
      research and development payment from Merck & Co., Inc., Kenilworth, N.J., U.S.A.).
  〇 Net cash used in investing activities amounted to an outflow of ¥26,458 million (inflow of
      ¥3,042 million in the same period of the previous fiscal year). Capital expenditures totaled
      ¥31,415 million (up ¥23,893 million from the same period of the previous fiscal year) mainly
      due to the acquisition of intangible assets.
  〇 Net cash used in financing activities amounted to an outflow of ¥77,557 million (down
      ¥10,591 million from the same period of previous fiscal year). The amount of dividends paid
      was ¥45,849 million, while the amount of repayment of long-term borrowings was ¥40,000
    million.
  〇 As a result, cash and cash equivalents as of the end of the period stood at ¥212,531 million
      (down ¥79,393 million from the end of the previous fiscal year). Free cash flows (net cash
      from operating activities less capital expenditures) for the period was negative and ¥2,197
      million.

(3) Research & Development Pipeline, Alliances, and Other Events
    [Status of Ongoing Research & Development Pipelines]
   〇 Anticancer agent Lenvima (lenvatinib, product name for renal cell carcinoma indication in
      Europe: Kisplyx)
      Approved for use in the treatment of thyroid cancer in over 60 countries including Japan,
         the United States, in Europe and Asia. An application for the agent for an additional
         indication of differentiated thyroid cancer was accepted in China in November 2019. The
         Company has received a notification from Japan’s Ministry of Health, Labour, and
         Welfare (MHLW) to the effect that the “all-case surveillance” special drug use-results
         survey condition required for approval of the agent in treatment of thyroid cancer was
         cleared, in Japan in December 2019.
        Approved in combination with everolimus for use in the treatment of renal cell carcinoma
         (second-line) in over 50 countries, including the United States and in Europe.
        Approved for use in the treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma (first-line) in over 55
         countries including Japan, the United States, in Europe, China and in Asia.
        Approved in combination with the anti-PD-1 antibody pembrolizumab from Merck & Co.,
         Inc., Kenilworth, N.J., U.S.A. for the treatment of patients with advanced endometrial
         carcinoma that is not microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) or mismatch repair deficient
         (dMMR), who have disease progression following prior systemic therapy and are not
         candidates for curative surgery or radiation, under the U.S. Food and Drug
         Administration (FDA) initiated program “Project Orbis” in the United States, Australia,
         and Canada in September 2019.
        A Phase III study in combination with pembrolizumab for use in the treatment of
         endometrial carcinoma (second-line) is underway in Japan, the United States, Europe,
         and other countries.
        A Phase III study of the agent in separate combinations with everolimus and
         pembrolizumab in renal cell carcinoma (first-line) is underway in Japan, the United States
         and Europe.
        The combination therapy with pembrolizumab was granted Breakthrough Therapy
         Designation for advanced and/or metastatic renal cell carcinoma in the United States.
        A Phase III study in combination with pembrolizumab for hepatocellular carcinoma (first-
         line) is underway in Japan, the United States, Europe and China.
        In July 2019, the combination therapy with pembrolizumab was granted Breakthrough
         Therapy Designation in the United States for the potential first-line treatment of patients
         with advanced unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma not amenable to locoregional
         treatment.
        Respective Phase III studies of the agent in combination with pembrolizumab for
         melanoma (first-line), nonsquamous non-small cell lung cancer (first-line) and PD-L1
         positive non-small cell lung cancer (first-line) are underway in the United States, Europe
         and other countries. Also, a Phase II study of the agent in combination with
         pembrolizumab for melanoma (second-line), as well as a Phase II basket trial in multiple
         cancer types are underway in the United States, Europe and other countries.
         Furthermore, Phase III studies for endometrial carcinoma (first-line), bladder cancer
         (first-line) and non-small cell lung cancer (second-line), have been respectively initiated

     A Phase III study for Lennox-Gastaut syndrome is underway in Japan, the United States

1.     IFRS 16 “Leases”
     (1) Accounting Policy regarding IFRS 16 “Leases”
     Lessee accounting
     Right-of-use assets and lease liabilities are recognized by the Group at the lease commencement date.
     Right-of-use assets representing the right to use the underlying assets over the lease terms are initially measured at
     cost, which comprises the amount of the initial measurement of the lease liabilities, plus any initial direct costs incurred
     and removal and restoration costs, less any lease incentives received. Right-of-use assets are measured applying a
     cost model based on IAS 16 “Property, Plant and Equipment” and depreciated using the straight-line method from the
     commencement date to the earlier of the end of the useful life of the right-of-use assets or the end of the lease term.
     Applying IAS 36 “Impairment of Assets”, right-of-use assets are assessed to determine whether impairment occurs.
     Lease liabilities are measured at the present value of the lease payments that are not paid at the commencement date.
     The lease payments are discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease. If the rate cannot be readily determined,
     the Group generally uses the incremental borrowing rate as the discount rate. After the initial measurement, lease
     liabilities are measured by increasing the carrying amount to reflect interest on the lease liability and reducing the
     carrying amount to reflect the lease payments made. In case that lease contracts are modified or renewed, the lease
     liabilities are subsequently reassessed by remeasuring to reflect changes to the lease conditions. Following the
     remeasurement of lease liabilities, a revision to the carrying amount of right-of-use assets is recognized.
     The Group elects not to recognize right-of-use assets and lease liabilities for those leases with a short term of no more
     than 12 months and those with small amount of assets, but recognizes the lease payments related to these leases as
     expenses by applying straight-line method over the lease terms.


     Lessor accounting
     The Group conducts leasing activities of certain property, plant and equipment. Leases that transfer substantially all
     the risks and rewards incidental to ownership of property, plant and equipment are classified as finance leases. Then
     the Group derecognizes the underlying assets and recognizes the net investment in the lease at the present value of
     the lease payments.
     Leases that do not transfer substantially all the risks and rewards incidental to ownership of underlying assets are
     classified as operating leases. Then the Group continues to recognize the underlying assets and recognizes the lease
     payments as lease revenue by applying straight-line method over the lease terms.


     (2) Application method and major changes with the application of IFRS 16 “Leases”
     In accordance with the transition method of IFRS 16 “Leases” (“this Standard”), the Group elects to apply this Standard
     to its leases retrospectively, and applies the method to recognize the cumulative effects of the initial application of this
     Standard at the date of initial application (April 1, 2019). In applying this Standard, the Group assesses whether
     contracts contain leases based on IAS 17 “Leases” (“IAS 17”) and IFRIC 4 “Determining whether an Arrangement
     contains a Lease.”
     Lease liabilities are recognized for leases previously classified as operating leases under IAS 17 at the date of initial
     application of this Standard.. There is no impact for the retained earnings at the beginning of this fiscal year because
     right-of-use assets are recognized at amount equal to the lease liabilities (after adjustment for prepaid or accrued lease
     payments). In the condensed interim consolidated statement of financial position as of December 31, 2019, right-of-
     use assets are included in property, plant and equipment, and lease liabilities are included in other financial liabilities.

transfer transactions are intended to achieve the objective of the strategic alliance above, thus it is appropriate that these
transactions should be treated as a single transaction.

portion of other liabilities (advances received) in the fiscal year ended March 31, 2019 and treated as a single transaction
with all of the remaining shares (66.6% of the number of shares issued) transferred in April 2019.
