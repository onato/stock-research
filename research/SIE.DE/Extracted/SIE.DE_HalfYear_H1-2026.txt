Introduction
Siemens AG’s Half-year Financial Report complies with the applicable legal
requirements of the German Securities Trading Act (Wertpapierhandelsgesetz –
WpHG) and comprises condensed Half-year Consolidated Financial Statements,
an Interim Group Management Report and a responsibility statement in
accordance with section 115 of the WpHG.
The Half-year Consolidated Financial Statements are in accordance with IFRS
applicable to interim financial reporting as issued by the IASB and as adopted by
the EU.
This Half-year Financial Report should be read in conjunction with our Combined
Management Report and our Consolidated Financial Statements for fiscal 2025,
which include a detailed analysis of our operations and activities as well as
explanations of financial measures used.

A.3 Outlook
We confirm our outlook for the Siemens Group for fiscal 2026, which we raised in February 2026, compared with the expected
developments for fiscal 2026 presented in our Combined Management Report for fiscal 2025.
For the Siemens Group, we continue to expect comparable revenue growth in the range of 6% to 8% and a book-to-bill ratio above 1 for
fiscal 2026.
For fiscal 2026, we anticipate EPS pre PPA in a range of €10.70 to €11.10, up from a range of €10.40 to €11.00 as presented in our
Combined Management Report for fiscal 2025.
This outlook excludes burdens from legal and regulatory matters.

A.4 Risks and opportunities
In our Siemens Report for fiscal 2025 we described certain risks which could have a material adverse effect on our business, financial
condition (including effects on assets, liabilities and cash flows), results of operations and reputation, our most significant opportunities
as well as the design of our risk management system.
During the reporting period, we continued to assess the risk from economic, political and geopolitical conditions as the most significant
challenge for us due to increasing geopolitical uncertainty.
The ongoing tensions related to the Middle East conflict increase geopolitical uncertainty and intensify risks arising from political
and economic conditions: The ongoing conflict in the Middle East led to a de facto blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, leading to disruptions
in oil and natural gas supply and potential damage to critical energy infrastructure across the region. Sharply increased energy prices are
already negatively affecting the global economy. A further escalation into a prolonged military conflict could massively disrupt global
energy supply and general supply chains, including critical relationships with key suppliers and logistics providers. It could involve a
multi‑month closure of the Strait of Hormuz, attacks on alternative export routes, and lasting severe damage to oil and LNG infrastructure
in the Middle East resulting in significant multi-year production losses. In addition, this could trigger non‑linear risks, such as higher market
volatility, a rapid transition to a more defensive risk stance, liquidity stress, and sharp asset price corrections. Private credit, AI-related
valuations, and highly indebted countries or firms could be particularly affected. Such market turbulence could amplify the macroeconomic
downturn, reduce customer demand, delay investment decisions, especially if confidence weakens rapidly or inflation expectations
become less anchored.
Beyond continuous monitoring of current developments and their management within regular business operations, Siemens has
established various initiatives and global task forces to analyze implications and implement overarching mitigation measures where
appropriate.
Assessment of the overall risk situation
While our assessments of certain individual risks have changed during the first half of fiscal 2026 due to developments in the external
environment, effects of our own mitigation measures and the revision of our risk assessment, the overall risk situation for Siemens did not
change significantly as compared to the prior year-end except for the increasing uncertainty in geopolitics, trade, economic and other
policies.
Additional risks and opportunities not known to us or that we currently consider immaterial could also affect our business operations and
reputation. At present, no risks have been identified that either individually or in combination with other risks could endanger our ability
to continue as a going concern.

NOTE 1   Basis of presentation
The accompanying Half-year Consolidated Financial Statements as of March 31, 2026, present the operations of Siemens AG and its
subsidiaries (the Company or Siemens). The Half-year Consolidated Financial Statements are in accordance with IFRS applicable to interim
financial reporting as issued by the IASB and as adopted by the EU and should be read in conjunction with the Siemens Consolidated
Financial Statements as of September 30, 2025. The Half-year Consolidated Financial Statements apply the same accounting principles
and practices as those used in the fiscal 2025 Consolidated Financial Statements. Results for the interim reporting period are not necessarily
indicative of future results. In interim periods, tax expense is based on the current estimated annual effective tax rate of Siemens. The
presentation of certain prior-year information is reclassified to conform to the current year presentation. The Half-year Consolidated
Financial Statements were not audited, and were authorized for issue by the Managing Board on May 4, 2026. For further information on
changes in estimates (including income taxes), disaggregation of revenue and on segment information, see information in our Interim
