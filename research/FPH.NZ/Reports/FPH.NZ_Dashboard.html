DCF Valuation

Why This DCF Says FPH Is Expensive — Read This First

Fisher & Paykel Healthcare has traded at a persistent 30-60x trailing P/E premium for essentially its entire listed history, reflecting razor/razorblade consumables economics (~90% of Hospital, ~88% of Homecare revenue is recurring), multi-decade clinical-practice-change growth largely independent of admission cycles, a fortress net-cash balance sheet, and scarcity value as one of the NZX's few globally-relevant large-cap growth compounders.

A conservative, component-built owner-FCF DCF at a large-cap-appropriate 9.0% WACC and 3.5% terminal growth produces a base intrinsic value of NZ$21.47 — about 50% below the NZ$42.58 market price. This is not a modelling defect. It is the honest output of capitalizing FPH's cash flows at a market-consistent discount rate; the market has, for over a decade, accepted a lower implied forward return than 9% to own this specific asset. The sanity check below confirms the current price multiple (P/E 53.4x) sits in line with, not above, FPH's own historical average (50.6x ex-outliers) — the market is not pricing FPH at an unusual extreme today.

Treat the DCF as a conservative floor/anchor, not a "sell" signal in isolation.
