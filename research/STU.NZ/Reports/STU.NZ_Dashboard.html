Revenue, Margin &amp; Profitability

Three consecutive loss-making years and a multi-year volume trough — FY2026 shows the first clear recovery in trading, obscured at the bottom line by a NZ$51.9m impairment.

Revenue &amp; YoY Growth

FY2026 revenue of $438.9m is +13.9% YoY off the FY2025 trough ($385.4m), but still well below FY2016's $515.9m — eleven years of revenue growth net to roughly zero.

EBITDA &amp; EBITDA Margin

Statutory EBITDA of -$45.8m includes the FY2026 impairment and exit costs. Management's normalised EBITDA (excluding one-offs) was +$9.9m, up from +$2.1m in FY2025 — a genuine operating recovery this chart understates.

Net Income

FY2026's -$61.2m is the largest loss on record, driven by a $51.9m non-cash impairment of business-unit carrying values plus $3.8m of other exit costs tied to leaving reinforcing/wire and plate processing — not a cash operating collapse.

Cash Flow &amp; Balance Sheet

The going-concern question in three charts: does cash cover the business, has equity been eroded, and how much of the balance sheet is now debt-funded.

Operating Cash Flow vs Free Cash Flow

Reported FCF has stayed positive through the downturn ($5.7m FY2026), but the DCF's owner-FCF adjustment — charging the full NZ$25m of annual lease cash instead of the NZ$18m rent-principal line that sits in financing — turns FY2026 owner FCF negative (-$13.3m). See the DCF section below.

Shareholders' Equity &amp; Total Debt

Equity fell from $182.3m to $121.8m in FY2026 alone, mostly the impairment. Debt rose to $60m (from zero net debt as recently as FY2024) after the debt-funded Perry Metal Protection acquisition, taken on in the depths of a downturn.

Shares Outstanding

166.0m through FY2023-24 to 183.6m after the FY2025 capital raise (+10.6%) — the last step-change in the count; no buybacks since FY2024.

Half-Year Trajectory (H1 FY2022&ndash;H1 FY2026)

Half-yearly detail shows the deterioration and the recent inflection more precisely than the annual figures alone.

H1 Revenue &amp; Net Income

H1 FY2022 $282.2m revenue / $14.3m profit → H1 FY2025 $196.0m / -$10.4m loss → H1 FY2026 $211.9m / -$12.4m loss. Revenue troughed at H1 FY2025 and is recovering (+8.1% H1 FY2025→H1 FY2026), but the bottom line has not followed because rising net debt now carries a real interest cost through the loss-making trough.
Model approach: This is a mid-cycle earnings-power valuation with an asset cross-check, not a growth DCF, because the FY2026 audit report carries a material-uncertainty-related-to-going-concern paragraph. The model does not extrapolate the FY2026 loss (a trough distorted by a $51.9m non-cash impairment) or the FY2022 windfall (a steel-price inventory gain that reversed); instead it ramps EBITDA margin over four years to roughly FY2024 plus half the benefit of exiting the structurally challenged reinforcing/wire and plate businesses, and capitalises only that. The decisive adjustment is IFRS-16: reported free cash flow looks positive because ~$18m of lease-principal repayment sits in financing, not operating, cash flow. Charging the full ~$25m of lease cash produces mid-cycle owner FCF of roughly $8-10m a year against $48m of net bank debt — at a 14% cost of capital that intrinsic value in the base case is essentially zero, negative in the bear case (flows do not service rent plus debt; a rescue raise would dilute holders to a few cents), and only reaches a positive ~$0.20-0.25/share in a bull case assuming a full decade of FY2022/23-level margins. Book value (NTA ~$0.40/share) is not treated as a floor: the market has paid 0.5-1.0x book for ten years while the price fell 87%, and in a wind-down leases and bank debt absorb most of it — NTA is only reachable via a trade buyer for the network. The honest label for NZ$0.305 against this analysis is a speculative option on a cyclical recovery or a takeout, not a margin-of-safety value buy.
