1. Overview of Operating Results and Financial Position
(1) Operating Results for the Second Quarter of the Fiscal Year Ending March 31, 2027
    For the six months ended September 30, 2026 (April 1, 2026 to September 30, 2026), operating results were as follows.
    In April 2026, the Company acquired the edaravone business from Tanabe Pharma Corporation (hereinafter, “Tanabe
    Pharma”). During the six months ended September 30, 2026, the provisional accounting treatment for the business
    combination was finalized.
    Regarding the application of the equity method to ViiV Healthcare Ltd. (hereinafter, “ViiV”), for which additional shares
    were acquired in the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, since the allocation of purchase price has not been completed
    in the six-month period ended September 30, 2026, provisional accounting treatment has been applied based on
    reasonable information available at this time. During the six months ended September 30, 2026, a provisional fair value
    assessment of identifiable intangible assets as of the acquisition date was conducted, and the impact of amortization of
    the intangible assets based on this assessment was reflected in our share of profit of investments accounted for using
    the equity method.
    Regarding the acquisition of shares in Torii Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (hereinafter, “ Torii Pharmaceutical ” ), the
    succession to the pharmaceutical business of Japan Tobacco Inc. (hereinafter, “JT Pharmaceutical Business”), and
    the acquisition of shares in Akros Pharma Inc. (hereinafter, “Akros”), all conducted in the fiscal year ended March 31,
    2026, the provisional accounting treatment for the business combinations was finalized in the six-month period ended
    September 30, 2026. The figures for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026 have been retrospectively adjusted accordingly.

   Revenue was 350.4 billion yen (up 64.5 percent year on year), reaching a record high for the first half of the fiscal year.
   The main components of revenue were domestic prescription drug sales of 74.3 billion yen (up 101.7 percent year on
   year), revenue from overseas subsidiaries and exports of 95.0 billion yen (up 210.2 percent year on year), and royalty
   income of 165.4 billion yen (up 27.9 percent year on year).
   Regarding domestic prescription drugs, revenue increased due to the recognition of revenue from products related to
   Torii Pharmaceutical, the recognition of revenue from edaravone (product name in Japan: RADICUT), a treatment for
   amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and other conditions, and the expanded prescriptions of QUVIVIQ, a treatment for
   insomnia. Revenue from overseas subsidiaries and exports increased due to the recognition of revenue from edaravone
   (product name in the U.S. and Canada: RADICAVA) in North America (the U.S. and Canada), together with higher sales
   of cefiderocol (product name in the U.S.: Fetroja, product name in Europe: Fetcroja) in Europe and the U.S. Royalty
   income increased due to higher royalty income resulting from increased sales of ViiV’s HIV franchise, as well as the
   recognition of royalty income related to the former JT Pharmaceutical Business.

   In terms of profits, cost of sales, selling, general, and administrative expenses, and research and development expenses
   increased due to the consolidation of Torii Pharmaceutical as a subsidiary, as well as the succession to the JT
   Pharmaceutical Business and the edaravone business. Of these, selling, general and administrative expenses increased
   mainly due to the amortization of intangible assets related to the succession to the former JT Pharmaceutical Business
   and the edaravone business and sales-related expenses for the U.S. business. Additionally, during the six months ended
   September 30, 2026, we recorded impairment losses and other items related to the conclusion of share transfer
   agreements for our Chinese group companies, Shenzhen Liancheng Medicine Company Limited and Nanjing Chang Ao
   Pharmaceutical Co., Limited, as other expenses. Despite these increased expenses, operating profit increased to 115.2
   billion yen (up 55.8 percent year on year) due to higher revenue and the recognition of share of profit of investments
   accounted for using the equity method following ViiV’s becoming an equity-method affiliate.
   Profit before tax was 107.3 billion yen (up 9.9 percent year on year). Profit increased as operating profit expanded,
   despite a decrease in financial income resulting from the deduction of dividends received from ViiV, which had been
   recognized as financial income until the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, from “Investments accounted for using the
   equity method” in the consolidated statement of financial position from the interim period of the fiscal year ending
   March 31, 2027, following ViiV’s becoming an equity-method affiliate, and an increase in financial expenses due to the
   recognition of foreign exchange losses.
   Profit attributable to owners of parent was 119.5 billion yen (up 44.0 percent year on year). This was mainly attributable
   to an increase in profit before tax and a decrease in income tax expenses, resulting from the recognition of deferred tax
   assets on tax loss carryforwards due to the expectation of future taxable income at the U.S. subsidiary following the
   succession to the edaravone business during the six months ended September 30, 2026.
   As a result, operating profit and profit attributable to owners of parent both reached record highs for the first half of the
   fiscal year.

   2) Reasons for Revisions to Consolidated Earnings Forecasts
   During the consolidated interim period, Shionogi exceeded its forecasts for revenue and all profit measures. This
   performance primarily reflects the steady contribution to results from the growth investments undertaken in fiscal 2025.
   In the overseas business, the transfer of the RADICAVA business for the treatment of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)
   progressed smoothly, supporting steady development of Shionogi’s rare disease business platform. In infectious
   diseases, an area of longstanding strength, sales of cefiderocol in Europe and the United States remained solid. In the
   domestic business, products in the quality-of-life disease area, including QUVIVIQ for insomnia, products associated
   with Torii Pharmaceutical, and RADICUT for ALS and other indications, delivered growth. Despite a lower-than-expected
   prevalence of COVID-19, growth in these products further advanced the establishment of a stable earnings base that is
   less susceptible to fluctuations in infectious disease prevalence. In the HIV business, a key pillar of Shionogi’s earnings,
   ViiV Healthcare continued to report strong sales of its core two-drug oral regimen Dovato, as well as the long-acting
   injectable products Cabenuva and Apretude, which are expected to become medium- to long-term growth drivers. As a
   result, royalty income and share of profit of investments accounted for using the equity method increased. These benefits
   from growth investments, together with the solid performance of existing businesses, drove the strong results.
   Based on the strong progress of these key businesses, Shionogi has revised its full-year earnings forecasts upward. In
   the overseas business, in addition to the successful launch of the RADICAVA business, Shionogi intends to position rare
   diseases as a new growth area through the acquisition of IntraBio and to establish new business platforms in Europe as
   well as the United States. Following completion of the acquisition, Shionogi plans to begin selling AQNEURSA, a
   treatment for neurological manifestations of Niemann-Pick disease type C (NPC) and ataxia in ataxia-telangiectasia (A-
   T), thereby expanding its contribution to patients with NPC and A-T. In the domestic business, Shionogi expects further
   growth from QUVIVIQ as market penetration accelerates. In the HIV business, following GSK’s upward revision of its
   forecast for ViiV Healthcare based on ViiV’s strong performance, Shionogi expects an increase in royalty income and
   share of profit of investments accounted for using the equity method.

4) Reasons for Revision to the Interim Dividend and Annual Dividend Forecast (Dividend Increase)
Shionogi is committed to maximizing corporate value by promoting management measures that strike an optimal balance
between growth investments and shareholder returns, allowing shareholders to experience mid- to long-term profit
growth. Our basic policy on shareholder returns is to provide stable dividends, using a dividend on equity (DOE) of 4%
or more as a guide.
As described above in the “Reasons for Revisions to Consolidated Earnings Forecasts,” the benefits of the growth
investments undertaken since fiscal 2025 have steadily contributed to our business performance, significantly
strengthening our earnings base and cash-generating capacity. Based on our policy of returning the benefits of business
growth to shareholders, Shionogi has decided to revise upward the interim dividend and annual dividend forecasts. Our
policies on capital allocation and shareholder returns, taking into account our future growth strategy, capital efficiency,
and financial discipline, will be presented in the next Medium-Term Business Plan, which is scheduled to be announced
in the next fiscal year.
After comprehensive consideration of these factors, Shionogi has decided to increase the interim dividend by 2 yen per
share from the previous forecast announced on May 12, 2026, and by 7 yen per share from the prior fiscal year, to 40
yen per share. Shionogi has also revised its year-end dividend forecast upward by 8 yen per share from the previous
forecast to 46 yen per share. Together with the interim dividend, the annual dividend is therefore expected to be 86 yen
per share, an increase of 15 yen per share from the prior fiscal year, representing the 15th consecutive annual dividend
increase.
