     In the first quarter of FY2026(January 1, 2026 – March 31, 2026), the Yokohama Rubber Group continued
   to pursue the “exploitation” of the strengths of its existing businesses and the “exploration” of new
   value while implementing its medium-term management plan Yokohama Transformation 2026 (YX2026),
   which aims to complete the transformation begun during the previous medium-term plan. This effort again
   produced strong results in all of the Group’       s businesses. First-quarter consolidated results included sales
   revenue of ¥303,806 million, up 10.4% YoY, and business profit was ¥44,439 million, a YoY increase of
   84.6%. Operating profit was ¥26,007 million, up 34.5% YoY despite a one-time expense of ¥13.0 billion on
   the closure of a U.S. tire plant in Salem, Virginia. Profit attributable to owners of the parent increased 72.6%
   YoY to ¥14,717 million.
     Tire segment sales revenue totaled ¥278,118 million (+11.1% YoY), accounting for 91.5% of the Yokohama
   Rubber Group’     s consolidated sales revenue.
     Consumer tire original equipment (OE) sales revenue increased year on year as strong sales in Japan of
   vehicle models equipped with YOKOHAMA tires compensated for the continued weakness in Japanese
   automakers’sales in China. Replacement tire sales revenue also increased year on year. While sales in North
   America declined owing to unfavorable weather conditions and other factors, continued thorough sales
   activities in Japan, strong sales of high-inch and other high-value-added tires in Europe, and efforts to develop
   new customers and expand business with existing customers in China, India and other regional markets boosted
   overall sales of replacement tires in the first quarter.
     Off-highway tire (OHT) business sales revenue also increased year on year on successful efforts to expand
   sales in a harsh demand environment. Sales of tires for agricultural machinery increased year on year as efforts
   to strengthen customer relations enabled us to increase our market share amid gradually rebounding demand. In
   the replacement tire market, promotion of our multi-brand strategy focused on our Mitas, Alliance, and Galaxy
   brand tires as well as a rebound in demand in North America led to higher sales.
     MB (Multiple Businesses) segment sales revenue totaled ¥23,985 million (+4.2% YoY), accounting for 7.9%
   of the Group’   s consolidated sales revenue.
     The segment’     s hose & couplings business saw its sales decline year on year as lower production of new
   vehicles in North America offset strong demand for hydraulic hoses in the replacement market in Japan.

      The industrial products business’sales revenue increased year on year. In addition to maintaining its high
    share of the market for conveyor belts, the business continued to win new contracts for its marine products and
    increased sales of its defense-related equipment, which it has been focusing on in recent years.
      The strong increase in first-quarter consolidated business profit is attributed to the consumer tire business’
    increased sales of high-value-added tires (AGW) and high-inch tires, the OHT business’increased sales
    volume, the MB segment’       s efforts to improve profitability of its existing businesses, and various internal
    efforts to drastically reduce costs and implement structural reforms.

(2) Consolidated Financial Position

      Total assets as of March 31, 2026, were ¥2,046,220 million, an increase of ¥47,860 million from the end of
    the previous consolidated fiscal year (December 31, 2025). The increase primarily reflects increases in
    property, plant and equipment; trade receivables; and inventories.
      Total liabilities as of March 31, 2026, were ¥993,583 million, an increase of ¥35,453 million from the end of
    the previous consolidated fiscal year. The increase in liabilities primarily reflects an increase in interest-bearing
    debt.
      Total equity as of March 31, 2026, came to ¥1,052,637 million, an increase of ¥12,406 million from the end
    of the previous consolidated fiscal year. The increase primarily reflects an increase in other components of
    equity due primarily to the impact from foreign exchange market fluctuations.
