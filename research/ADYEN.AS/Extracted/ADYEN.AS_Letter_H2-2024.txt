Dear shareholders,                           February 13, 2025
Looking back on 2024, we are proud to have delivered a strong
year of growth. A productive second half contributed to this,
with H2 net revenue – the metric on which we manage the
business – arriving at €1,082.7 million, up 22% YoY. In line with
our building blocks, expanding with existing customers
remained a primary driver of this period’s results, as we further
increased our share of wallet. This continued expansion
demonstrates our value as a long-term partner to leading
global enterprises, their appetite for innovation, and the value
Adyen’s premium functionality brings.
Since the time of Adyen's founding in 2006, we have paved our
own path by making decisions that set us apart and best
position us for long-term success. These ranged from
developing our tech stack entirely in-house, to building for the
needs of enterprise businesses, investing in our banking and
acquiring license portfolio, and scaling our global team with
the highest talent standards. We have, by design, done things
differently. The benefits of this approach are compounding,
and allowed us to capitalize on exciting opportunities in H2.

Demand for payments as a key strategic enabler
Global commerce is reaching new levels of sophistication, with
customer expectations steadily increasing. Today, industries
need not be convinced that payments can be a strategic
revenue driver. Rather, more sectors are independently
recognizing this fact, and now seeking the partner most
capable of unlocking value. In tandem, consumers around the
world demand the highest level of ease, speed, and channel
fluidity. In fact, 61% of shoppers expect personalized
experiences, and 55% will abandon their cart if their preferred
                                    1
payment method is not available. Adyen has distinguished
itself as the technology partner that can best meet these needs
for businesses and consumers alike.

Our innovative approach remains at the forefront of our
enterprise appeal. This period, Adyen strengthened our long-

online multi-brand fashion destination. The collaboration
includes a joint focus on enhancing the security and
convenience of Zalando's online payments, with continuous
improvements in 3D Secure transactions as part of the
Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2) framework. One of the
key innovations in our partnership is the implementation of
Network Token Optimization, which has transformed
Zalando's online payment process. The introduction of
Network Token Optimization has enabled Zalando to achieve
higher authorization rates and conversion success, while
ensuring a frictionless payment experience and added
convenience for Zalando shoppers.
By harnessing tokenization and AI at scale, Adyen’s digital
solution has pronounced decision-making advantages, which
we’re seeing front and center in North America as a result of
our ongoing US Debit investments. Following the 2023 Durbin
Amendment revision, which was modified to widen its scope
and create more competition among signature networks like
Visa, Mastercard, and Discover, all debit cards issued in the US
have been mandatorily branded by at least one additional
unaffiliated network. This increased competition has led to
lower costs for enterprises, but also increased complexity.
When we saw that the cheaper networks often came with
worse performance, we seized the opportunity to do things
differently and better. Our intelligent payment routing not only
minimizes interchange and scheme fees for lower costs, but
was built to achieve this without compromising on approval
rates. This can be unlocked in an instant, with no additional
code required for Adyen customers. Once leveraged, our AI
analyzes a range of factors for each payment – including the
scheme and issuer – and then chooses the best network based
on success rates and scheme fees. As Adyen remains network
agnostic, we can focus fully on performance and cost
reduction.

1.3 Long-term merchant contract & contract assets

Long-term merchant contract
In 2018, Adyen entered into a long-term contract with eBay for the provision of payment services that resulted in the initial recognition
of contract assets settled with a cash advance and issue of warrants over Adyen’s shares. During 2024, the related contract was
extended from January 31, 2025 to June 30, 2025, with no impact on the recognition and subsequent measurement of the contract
asset and warrant reserve.
The contract assets are separated into a monetary and non-monetary component and are assessed for impairment annually with
reference to the remaining (net) benefits from the long-term merchant contract. The monetary component was fully repaid and
amortized in 2022.
During H2 2024 the second tranche milestone was met, vested, and the related warrant was subsequently exercised by eBay. A total of
403,724 shares were issued to eBay at €240 per share. The gross equity balance relating to tranche 2 and related deferred tax were
transferred from ‘warrant reserve’ to ‘share premium’ and ‘retained earnings’, respectively. The dilutive effect of the exercised warrant
in 2024 (tranche 2) was reflected in the shares outstanding at 2024 year-end. The exercise price for the third and fourth tranches were
also fixed when the second tranche milestone was met. As such, tranche 3 and 4 were reclassified from ‘derivative liabilities’ to ‘warrant
reserve’ as the classification criteria for equity were met (note 3). There were no further tranche milestones met during 2024. As per
December 31, 2024, the ‘warrant reserve’ was carried at historic cost (EUR 1,599).

Contract assets
Management derived its best estimate of the future (net) revenue from expected payments volumes and fees determined in the
merchant contract, net of directly attributable costs to fulfil the remaining payment service obligations. The contract assets were not
impaired at December 31, 2024 and 2023 as the remaining estimated (net) benefits from the merchant contract exceeded the contract
assets balance at year-end.
Contract asset costs mainly relate to integration and development fees that are directly incremental to obtain the multi-year contracts
and do not represent separate performance obligations. Adyen will amortize these costs (non-monetary component) against revenue
(settlement fees) on a pro rata basis as the related revenue is recognized. The non-monetary component relate to the long-term
contract with eBay (refer above). During H2 2024, Adyen entered into multi-year service agreements with its merchants totaling EUR
1,658 (H2 2023: EUR 0), for which costs related to obtain these agreements have been capitalized as contract assets (‘other contract
assets’).
