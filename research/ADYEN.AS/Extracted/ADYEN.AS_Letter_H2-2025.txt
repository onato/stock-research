                 Evolving our foundation for the next era of commerce
                 Our ability to solve complex, real-world problems has been anchored in two foundational layers: a
                 single, global tech stack and our own licensed banking infrastructure. Together, they give us direct
                 control and enable us to reduce friction, limit points of failure, and deliver consistent performance
                 globally.
                 This foundation allows us to serve some of the world’s most demanding businesses — large, complex
                 enterprises operating across channels and regions. By building and running the full stack ourselves, we
                 can move funds securely, settle reliably, and scale financial products without intermediaries. In an
                 environment of rising complexity, this level of control remains a core differentiator.
                 As technology evolves, so must our foundation. While these two layers remain core to our offering, we
                 are building on them to meet the demands of today and tomorrow. Advances in AI, increasingly
                 sophisticated fraud, and the growing misuse of digital systems are exposing the limits of static,
                 document-based approaches to identity and risk. Designed for a different era, traditional controls add
                 friction for legitimate businesses and shoppers, while struggling to prevent abuse at scale.
                 To address this, we have integrated a third foundational layer: Dynamic Identification. Moving beyond
                 static checks, we designed this layer to draw on trillions of interactions across our online and in-person
                 flows. By embedding this intelligence directly into our stack, we assess risk dynamically and adapt
                 decisions in real time, enabling us to eliminate friction while tightening security with surgical precision.
                 Dynamic Identification builds on the foundation of our unified payments technology platform and
                 banking licenses, and acts as a core enabler of trusted commerce, increasingly shaping how our
                 products are designed.

                 Expanding Adyen Uplift
                 The most immediate impact of Dynamic Identification is visible across our optimization and risk
                 products. It is the intelligence layer that powers Adyen Uplift, enabling decisions that balance
                 conversion, cost, and risk across the full payment flow rather than in isolation.
                 Building on this foundation, we introduced the newest module within Adyen Uplift in 2025: Personalize.
                 It was developed and validated through pilots with a select group of enterprise customers in the second
                 half of the year, focusing on one of the most common trade-offs merchants face as they scale across
                 channels: how to lower payment costs without negatively impacting conversion.
                 Lower-cost payment methods are often available, but encouraging shoppers to choose them
                 indiscriminately can increase checkout abandonment and degrade the customer experience. Dynamic
                 Identification allows this trade-off to be managed intelligently. By understanding who the shopper is
                 and how they behave across both online and in-person touchpoints, we can personalize the payment
                 experience in real time, guiding shoppers toward preferred and lower-cost options only when the data
                 indicates they are likely to complete the transaction.
                 Insights from the H2 pilots1 demonstrate the value of this adaptive approach. Merchants observed
                 conversion improvements of up to 6%, alongside transaction cost reductions of up to 3%, achieved
                 through personalized optimization rather than static, rule-based, and generic logic.

                 Addressing policy abuse at scale
                 For our customers, an underestimated share of losses comes not only from traditional payment fraud,
                 but also from policy abuse: repeated exploitation of returns, promotions, and refunds that often appear
                 legitimate in isolation but compound into material cost over time. Without visibility into repeat behavior,
                 merchants are left to rely on manual reviews or broad policy restrictions, increasing friction for
                 legitimate customers while failing to address the underlying problem.
                 In the second half of 2025, we applied Dynamic Identification to this challenge through targeted pilots
                 with enterprise customers. By linking refund activity at the identity level, rather than viewing
                 transactions in isolation, we were able to surface patterns that had previously remained hidden.
                 The pilots showed strong engagement, with merchants using these insights on a daily basis, rather than
                 only for ad hoc investigations. More importantly, they reported a step change in confidence: they were
                 able to identify abuse clearly, measure its true scale, and pinpoint its sources. This replaced
                 fragmented, manual processes with shared, data-driven visibility. Capabilities such as identifying top
                 refund contributors at the shopper level were consistently cited as materially reducing investigation
                 time and operational overhead.
                 This visibility also uncovered significant, previously undetected exposure. One global luxury group
                 identified individual shoppers each receiving up to €5k in refunds, in some cases up to twenty times
                 their average basket value, revealing potential material losses that had gone unnoticed. In another case,
                 a large sports and entertainment customer identified a shopper with roughly 70% of transactions
                 refunded over several years, exposing a long-standing abuse pattern that had not been visible through
                 traditional transaction-level analysis.
                 These examples illustrate a broader dynamic: while refunds are common and often legitimate, a small
                 subset of shoppers can drive a disproportionate share of losses. We are uniquely positioned to make
                 this behavior visible and actionable as Dynamic Identification allows our customers to intervene
                 precisely and avoid blunt policy changes that negatively impact conversion and trust.

                 The age of agentic commerce
                 Looking ahead, Dynamic Identification is also a critical enabler of emerging models such as agentic
                 commerce. As this evolution unfolds over time, traditional identity signals are likely to fall away.
                 Transactions initiated by agents will require new trust frameworks, relying on infrastructure, behavioral
                 context, and adaptive risk models rather than direct human interaction.
                 In H2, we focused on understanding our customers' needs and how we can best build to meet them. We
                 held extensive conversations with enterprise merchants across retail, luxury, travel, entertainment, and
                 platforms to understand both their ambitions and their concerns. While merchants see clear potential in
                 agent-led commerce, they are equally clear about what must not change: ownership of the customer
                 relationship, control over payments and data, and confidence that new channels can be adopted
                 without introducing new risk.
                 These needs of our customers directly shape our approach. Rather than building isolated agent
                 experiences, we are extending our existing platform so that agent-initiated transactions become
                 another channel within a merchant’s existing workflows, governed by the same principles of control,
                 security, and interoperability. Dynamic Identification plays a central role here, enabling verifiable

                 Unified Commerce engineered for peak moments
                 One of our foundational advantages is rooted in Unified Commerce (UC). While many providers treat in-
                 person payments as a separate business line, we view the physical point of sale as a critical source of
                 intelligence. By unifying physical and digital environments, we capture a complete view of the customer
                 journey, turning every in-person interaction into actionable data that strengthens our ecosystem. In
                 practice, UC is not just about connectivity, but about performance, intelligence, and control at the
                 moments that matter most.
                 Success in modern commerce depends on real-time recognition. By operating one platform, merchants
                 don’t just process transactions; they recognize shoppers consistently across channels using shared
                 identity and infrastructure. This allows them to act on that data instantly, whether that means
                 personalizing an offer or streamlining a checkout, even during the most intense peak trading moments.
                 During the Black Friday Cyber Monday (BFCM) weekend, we processed 837 million transactions
                 across online and in-store channels, while maintaining 99.9999% uptime and recognizing nearly 95% of
                 approximately 400 million unique shoppers in real time. These moments expose whether payment
                 providers can maintain performance and reliability when volume, complexity, and operational pressure
                 peak simultaneously.
                 The data also highlights the continued importance of physical retail within an increasingly omnichannel
                 customer journey. During BFCM, in-store average basket values were 28% higher than online.
                 Commerce at scale is also increasingly global: peak transaction volumes compared to a typical Friday
                 increased 2.8x in the United States, 4.5x in Spain, and 6.1x in Denmark, reflecting growing cross-border
                 and multi-market demand.4
                 This global peak performance is the result of ongoing deliberate focus on providing best-in-class in-
                 person payments. In H2, we expanded our in-person payment (IPP) capabilities with the launch of two
                 new terminals, the S1E4 Pro and S1F4 Pro, built for high-traffic environments and fully integrated with
                 merchants’ online operations. This is what makes our UC offering unique: performance at the point of
                 sale is engineered into the platform, not added as an extension.
                 The UC foundation that proves itself during peak moments continues to support customers year-round,
                 across industries. In H2, we announced the expansion of our partnership with LVMH5 to support a more
                 unified in-store and online experience across its “Maisons,” where reliability and shopper recognition
                 are critical at scale. We further deepened our work with Lufthansa,6 extending unified payments across
                 reservations, onboard, and airport touchpoints to support complex global journeys. We also partnered
                 with Uber to launch “Tap to Ride” airport kiosks, powered by Adyen terminals, enabling travelers to
                 book rides instantly upon arrival without relying on mobile connectivity.
                 Across these verticals, the pattern is consistent: when digital and physical commerce converge,
                 performance at the point of sale becomes a multiplier for scale. Unified Commerce enables Adyen to
                 perform in the most demanding moments of global commerce, and compound that advantage across
                 the platform long after peak moments pass.

                 From payments to global financial infrastructure
                 As our customers scale globally, payments increasingly become the starting point rather than the
                 endpoint of their financial needs. Momentum across issuing, capital, and accounts reflects the
                 expanding role these products play in enabling new revenue streams for platform customers.
                 In 2025, issuing reached an inflection point, with volumes growing 8x YoY as platforms embedded
                 cards directly into their core workflows. This shift supports a wide range of operational use cases, from
                 supplier payouts to employee expenses, while reducing reliance on external banking relationships.
                 Growth also accelerated across capital and account products. Capital loan volume more than doubled
                 YoY with over 80% of businesses returning for subsequent loans. Recent launches such as Fresha
                 Capital7 and YetiPay8 extend these capabilities into vertical SaaS platforms, where fast, embedded
                 financing directly supports business growth.
                 Alongside this, business accounts and cash management products continued to scale, improving
                 access to funds and increasing financial flexibility. New capabilities gained traction quickly, with
                 CashOut reaching a hundreds-of-millions-of-euros annualized run rate while still in pilot and strong early
                 adoption from customers such as Vagaro.9
                 Together, these products form modular building blocks that enable platforms to manage end-to-end
                 money movement on infrastructure we fully control, bringing acquiring, balances, payouts, and issuing
                 into a single operating environment.

                 The enterprise opportunity: introducing Intelligent Money Movement
                 As enterprise customers scale, fragmented money movement becomes a structural constraint. Pay-ins,
                 settlements, and payouts spread across banks, providers, and geographies, limiting visibility and
                 reducing liquidity efficiency. We address this by extending our platform beyond payments to manage
                 the full lifecycle of funds for enterprise customers, using the same integrated foundation that powers
                 our payments capabilities.
                 Intelligent Money Movement (IMM) brings money-in, money management, and money-out into a single
                 operating environment. By consolidating these flows on our platform, customers reduce fragmentation,
                 gain real-time cash visibility, and improve liquidity efficiency. IMM is not a standalone layer, but an
                 integrated part of the platform, enabling enterprises to manage funds directly at the source rather than
                 across disconnected systems.
                 We are initially focusing on industries with complex fund flows, including marketplaces, travel, mobility,
                 and insurance, in regions where we operate under our own banking licenses. In these markets,
                 enterprises can rely on a single foundation to orchestrate payments, accounts, and payouts across
                 entities and geographies — for example, collecting customer funds, settling them in real time, holding
                 balances where needed, and paying out sellers or partners through one integrated system.
                 The scale of this opportunity is reflected in our joint research with Boston Consulting Group.10 The study

                 Net revenue
                 Net revenue17 was €1,270.7 million in H2 2025, up 21% YoY on a constant currency basis, or 17% YoY
                 on a reported basis, driven by increased share of wallet with existing customers. For the Full Year (FY)
                 2025, net revenue was €2,364.2 million, up 21% YoY on a constant currency basis, or 18% YoY on a
                 reported basis.
                 Net revenue includes €6.6 million of net interest income in H2 2025, up 14% YoY. For FY 2025, net
                 interest income was €12.6 million, up 52% YoY.
                 Regional net revenue contributions remained consistent, with EMEA contributing 57%, followed by
                 North America at 27%, APAC at 10%, and LATAM landing at 6%.
                 In the second half of the year, EMEA net revenue increased 17% YoY on both a reported and constant
                 currency basis, driven by increased share of wallet and partially offset by lower market volume growth.
                 North America grew 17% YoY, or 26% on a constant currency basis, supported by ongoing share of
                 wallet gains, though moderated by slower growth from APAC-headquartered online retailers18 and a
                 weaker U.S. dollar. APAC accelerated slightly to 14% growth YoY, or 22% on a constant currency basis,
                 mostly driven by deepened relationships with our existing customers. LATAM net revenue increased
                 34% YoY, or 37% on a constant currency basis, driven by share of wallet gains with existing
                 international customers.
                 For the fourth quarter of 2025, net revenue increased 19% YoY on a constant currency basis and 15% on
                 a reported basis. While reported growth was impacted by foreign currency headwinds, underlying
                 performance was in line with expectations. The difference between Q3 and Q4 constant currency
                 growth rates was driven by settlement timing, and a stronger comparable period in Q4 last year. The
                 stronger Q4 of last year was due to a robust holiday season that was amplified by significant wallet
                 share gains including with some of the aforementioned APAC-based online retailers.

            1.2 Long-term merchant contract & contract assets
            Adyen capitalizes contract costs relating to multi-year service contracts with its merchants. These costs mainly relate to
            integration and development fees that are directly incremental to obtain the multi-year contracts and do not represent a separate
            performance obligation. Adyen will amortize these costs against revenue (settlement fees) on a pro rata basis as the related
            revenue is recognized.
            Management derived its best estimate of the future (net) revenue from expected payments volumes and fees determined in the
            merchant contract, net of directly attributable costs to fulfil the remaining payment service obligations. The contract assets were
            not impaired at December 31, 2025 and 2024 as the remaining estimated (net) benefits from the merchant contract exceeded the
            contract assets balance at year-end.
            In 2018, Adyen entered into a long-term contract with eBay for the provision of payment services that resulted in the initial
            recognition of contract assets settled with a cash advance (monetary component) and issue of warrants (non-monetary
            component) over Adyen’s shares. The non-monetary component was fully amortized as at June 30, 2025, and the warrant
            reserve in equity was reclassified to retained earnings.
