Company Overview

Adyen is a Dutch financial technology company that operates a single, self-built global platform combining payment acquiring, processing, risk/fraud management, and — via its own EU, US, and UK banking licenses — embedded financial products such as issuing, business accounts, and capital. It serves large enterprises and fast-growing software platforms/marketplaces (Uber, Spotify, Meta, McDonald's, eBay, Etsy, Starbucks, LVMH, Lufthansa) across three commercial pillars: Digital, Unified Commerce, and Platforms.

Geographic mix: EMEA ~57% of FY2025 net revenue, North America ~27%, APAC ~10%, LATAM ~6%. FY2025 processed volume €1,394.3bn (+8% reported, ~21% ex. one large normalizing customer); Q1 2026 processed volume €382.0bn, +21% YoY.

Business Model

Adyen earns a small "take rate" plus fixed fees on total payment volume processed, net of interchange/scheme fees passed through to card networks and issuing banks. Owning the full stack and its own banking licenses lets it offer one global integration rather than stitched-together vendors. More than 80% of net revenue growth historically comes from existing customers expanding usage ("land and expand"). Adyen pays no dividend and conducts no buybacks — it retains 100% of profit, so equity growth is driven purely by earnings/multiple, not share-count reduction.

Competitive Position

Competitors: Stripe, PayPal/Braintree, Checkout.com, Fiserv, Global Payments/Worldpay, FIS, Worldline, Block/Square, Nexi

Stripe processed ~$1.9tn in 2025 (+34% YoY) vs Adyen's €1.4tn (+8% reported/~21% ex. one customer) — a genuine, worsening competitive datapoint on TPV growth.

Moat:

  • Single, self-built global tech stack — one codebase spanning gateway, processing, acquiring, risk
  • Own banking licenses (NL/EU, US federal branch, UK PRA/FCA) — regulatory barrier few replicate
  • Unified Commerce — ~95% shopper recognition across channels at BFCM 2025 peak (837m transactions, 99.9999% uptime)
  • High switching costs — enterprise churn well under 1%
  • Land-and-expand economics — >80% of growth from existing wallet share
  • Dynamic Identification / data network effect — emerging, unproven as a durable pricing lever

Growth Drivers

  • Land-and-expand with existing large enterprise customers, cross-sell Unified Commerce ↔ Digital
  • Platforms acceleration: net revenue +50% YoY FY2025, issuing volumes +8x YoY, capital loan volume doubled (>80% repeat borrower rate)
  • Intelligent Money Movement (IMM) — CashOut at hundreds-of-millions annualized run-rate while still in pilot
  • Dynamic Identification / Adyen Uplift AI layer for conversion, fraud reduction, agentic-commerce readiness
  • North America expansion — 550-650 net new hires planned FY2026, primarily US teams
  • Unified Commerce proof points at peak scale (BFCM 2025)
  • Emerging markets: India (UPI), Japan, Brazil (Pix), Mexico
  • First-ever M&A: Talon.One (loyalty/promotions) + Orb (billing), closed 1-Jul-2026
  • Agentic commerce positioning with OpenAI, Google, Cloudflare, Visa, Mastercard

Key Risks

  • [HIGH] Competitive intensity from Stripe, Checkout.com, PayPal/Braintree — Stripe moving upmarket into Adyen's core enterprise segment
  • [HIGH] Digital pillar deceleration — +9% YoY reported Q1 2026, slowest print in company history for the largest pillar
  • [HIGH] Leadership transition — Co-CEO health step-back, CFO departs 31-Aug-2026, new CPO, first-ever M&A integration, all concurrent
  • [MODERATE] Single large-volume customer distorting reported processed-volume growth since Q3 2024
  • [MODERATE] Net income sensitivity to falling interest rates (own-cash finance income)
  • [MODERATE] FX — weaker USD was a FY2025 headwind (18% reported vs 21% CC growth)
  • [MODERATE] M&A integration risk — first acquisitions ever, breaking 15+ years of build-not-buy discipline
  • [MODERATE] Banking-license regulatory obligations across multiple jurisdictions; PwC auditor term expires after FY2026
  • [MODERATE-HIGH] Global e-commerce/consumer spending cyclicality

Bull Case

  • Best-in-class enterprise payments platform with a structural tech + banking-license moat; 53-55% EBITDA margins even as guidance normalizes
  • Platforms/embedded finance inflecting (issuing +8x YoY, capital loans doubling, Platforms +35-50% YoY) — large incremental TAM beyond volume-based take-rate economics
  • Severe reset (-33% to -42% YTD 2026, -72% from Aug-2021 peak) may be an attractive entry if 20-22% FY2026 guidance and take-rate stability prove conservative
  • Land-and-expand with >80% of growth from existing customers, sub-1% churn — high revenue visibility, low CAC
  • Unified Commerce data/recognition advantage widening via Talon.One/Orb and Dynamic Identification

Bear Case

  • Digital pillar (~56% of revenue) decelerated to slowest-ever growth (+9% YoY Q1 2026); June-2026 pricing revision is direct evidence take-rate power is under genuine pressure
  • Three negative catalysts compounded within ~6 months (guidance cut, CFO resignation, pricing revision/downgrades) — the -72% drawdown may reflect correctly-priced structural deceleration, not overreaction
  • Unusually broad leadership transition concentrated in one window for a founder-culture company
  • First-ever M&A breaks 15+ years of build-not-buy discipline — raises the question of whether organic growth alone is sufficient
  • 55%+ EBITDA margin target already pushed from 2026 to 2028; 2026 margin guided flat; net income exposed to falling rates
