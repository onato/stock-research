 E. Notes to the Condensed Consolidated Interim Financial Statements
1.   Corporate information
     Yangzijiang Shipbuilding (Holdings) Ltd. (the “Company”) is listed on the Singapore Exchange and
     incorporated and domiciled in Singapore. The address of its registered office is 36 Robinson Road, #20-
     01 City House, Singapore 068877. The principal place of business is 9 Raffles Place, #54-01, Republic
     Plaza, Singapore 048619.

     The principal activities of the Company are investment holding and agency service for shipbuilding and
     related activities. The principal activities of its significant subsidiaries are:

     a) Shipbuilding, production and processing of large-scale steel structures;
     b) Facilitating the sale and export of ships for the shipbuilder and trading of ship related equipment;
     c) Chartering of vessels; and
     d) Holding of investment property.

2.   Basis of Preparation
     The condensed consolidated interim financial statements as at and for the six-month period ended 30
     June 2025 have been prepared in accordance with Singapore Financial Reporting Standards
     (International) 1-34 Interim Financial Reporting (SFRS(I) 1-34) issued by the Accounting Standards
     Council Singapore. The condensed interim financial statements do not include all the information required
     for a complete set of financial statements. However, selected explanatory notes are included to explain
     events and transactions that are significant to an understanding of the changes in the Group’s financial
     position and performance of the Group since the last consolidated annual financial statements for the
     year ended 31 December 2024.

     The accounting policies adopted are consistent with those of the previous financial year which were
     prepared in accordance with SFRS(I)s, except for the adoption of new or amended standards effective
     as of 1 January 2025 as set out in Note 2.1.

     The condensed consolidated interim financial statements are presented in Renminbi (“RMB”), which is
     the Company’s functional currency.

2.1. New or amended standards adopted by the Group
     Nil

2.2. Use of judgements and estimates
     In preparing the condensed consolidated interim financial statements, management has made
     judgements, estimates and assumptions that affect the application of accounting policies and the reported
     amounts of assets and liabilities, income and expense. Actual results may differ from these estimates.

     The significant judgements made by management in applying the Group’s accounting policies and the
     key sources of estimation uncertainty were the same as those that applied to the consolidated financial
     statements as at and for the year ended 31 December 2024.

     Estimates and underlying assumptions are reviewed on an ongoing basis. Revisions to accounting
     estimates are recognised in the period in which the estimates are revised and in any future periods
     affected.

3.   Seasonal operations
     The Group’s businesses are not affected significantly by seasonal or cyclical factors during the financial
     period.

4.   Segment and revenue information
     Management has determined the operating segments based on the reports reviewed by the Executive
     Chairman and head of respective business departments (collectively known as “Management Team”) that
     are used to make strategic decisions.

     The principal activities of shipbuilding segment are that of shipbuilding and offshore marine equipment
     construction. The principal activities of shipping segment consist of charter hire income earned by vessel
     owning companies.

     Other segments include terminal services, trading, ship design services, sales of residential units, and
     investment property. These are not identified as reportable operating segments, as they are not separately
     reported to the Management Team. The results of these operations are included in “Others”.

Revenue

In 1H2025, the Group successfully delivered a total of 23 vessels on schedule, including 4 delivered by its joint venture,
Yangzi-Mitsui Shipbuilding Co., Ltd. ("Yangzi-Mitsui"). This compares to 35 vessels delivered in 1H2024, of which 7
were contributed by the joint venture. Shipbuilding revenue for the period was slightly lower at RMB12,249 million,
compared to RMB12,398 million in 1H2024, primarily due to the commencement of construction for oil tankers, which
carry a lower average unit price compared to containerships. However, the impact of the lower revenue contribution
from oil tankers was largely offset by revenue from the construction of large dual-fuel containerships.

Revenue from the shipping business declined by 15% year-on-year to RMB511 million in 1H2025, mainly due to
weaker charter rates during the period.

The Group is also engaged in various other businesses, including terminal services, trading, ship design services,
and investment property. Revenue from these segments rose significantly from RMB46 million in 1H2024 to RMB117
million in 1H2025, primarily driven by higher trading volumes. The increase in trading activity was largely attributable
to the sale of raw materials to Tsuneishi Group (Zhoushan) Shipbuilding Co., Ltd. (“Tsuneishi Zhoushan”), a 34%-
owned associated company of the Group.

Following the commencement of the LNG terminal conversion project and renovation works at the Singapore office
building held as an investment property, revenue contributions from terminal services and investment property will be
temporarily suspended. In 1H2025, the Group recognised revenue of RMB20 million from the sale of residential units
held under Jiangsu Yangxing Property Co., Ltd., a subsidiary acquired in 1H2024. The units were primarily sold to
employees.

Operating cost

Cost of sales for the shipbuilding business decreased by 14% year-on-year to RMB7,932 million in 1H2025,
compared to RMB9,183 million in 1H2024. This reduction was primarily driven by lower steel costs, and a change in
product mix with a higher contribution from oil tankers, which generally have lower costs compared to containerships.
There was no allowance or reversal of allowance for losses recognised on onerous contracts during the period.

The operating costs for the shipping business segment were higher than the costs incurred during the same period
last year. This increase can be attributed to the increased repair and maintenance activities, leading to higher
operating expenses during the current period.

Gross profit

In 1H2025, the Group’s shipbuilding business achieved a significant improvement in gross profit margin, increasing
from 26% in 1H2024 to 35% in 1H2025. This improvement was primarily driven by lower steel costs, successful
execution of secured contracts with improved pricing, and the smooth delivery of large dual-fuel containerships.

The shipping business segment recorded a gross profit margin of 26% in 1H2025, compared to 41% in 1H2024,
reflecting the impact of weak charter rates for bulk carriers during the period.

Other income

The "Other income" category primarily comprises interest income from bank deposits, interest income from ship

In 1H2025, interest income experienced growth, rising from RMB332 million in 1H2024 to RMB486 million. This
increase was primarily driven by higher interest income from bank deposits, resulting from a rise in cash and cash
equivalents held by the Group during the same period.

Other gains/(losses) - net

The category of "Other gains/(losses) - net" encompasses several components, including foreign exchange-related
gains/losses, fair value changes on derivative financial instruments and financial assets at fair value through profit or
loss, gain on disposal of property, plant, and equipment as well as subsidy income.

In 1H2025, the Group recorded total other gains of RMB139 million, compared to other losses of RMB119 million in
1H2024. The net gains were mainly driven by foreign exchange gains of RMB67 million, fair value gains of RMB7
million on derivative financial instruments, subsidy income of RMB41 million, and a gain of RMB6 million from the
disposal of property, plant and equipment, which primarily related to the sale of two barge carriers by the shipping
segment during the period.

Expenses

In 1H2025, total administrative expenses increased to RMB358 million, compared to RMB278 million in 1H2024. The

11. A commentary at the date of the announcement of the significant trends and competitive
    conditions of the industry in which the group operates and any known factors or events that may
    affect the Group in the next reporting period and the next 12 months.


 There have been some relatively positive developments in the tariff negotiations between the U.S. and some major
 economies like Japan, which the tariffs have now been capped at 15% from the previously proposed 25%. However,
 the newly agreed tariffs remain significantly higher than the 2.5% level in place before the Trump administration.

 Shipbuilding industry

 According to BIMCO, global shipbuilding contracting fell by 54% year-on-year (“yoy”) in 1H2025. The decline came
 amid growing concerns over the impact of the U.S. tariffs on global trade volumes. By vessel type, ordering activity
 for bulk carriers, tankers, and gas carriers slowed due to weaker freight rates, while demand for containerships
 remained relatively resilient. 1
 While shipowners may seek alternatives in response to the port fees proposed by the United States Trade
 Representative (“USTR”), capacity available outside of China remains limited. BIMCO highlighted that South Korea
 and Japan are facing challenges in scaling up production, primarily due to labour shortages from declining populations,
 which has led to rising labour costs and reduced competitiveness. Moreover, although the U.S. and India are building
 shipbuilding capacity led by government efforts, it will take time to scale up production meaningfully.
 Long-term shipbuilding demand remains resilient, underpinned by the structural shift toward maritime
 decarbonisation. In April 2025, the International Maritime Organisation (“IMO”) established the Net-zero Framework,
 which combines mandatory emissions limits and greenhouse gas (“GHG”) pricing across the industry. The measures
 are set to be formally adopted in October 2025 before entry into force in 2027, will become mandatory for large ocean-
 going ships over 5,000 gross tonnages, which account for 85% of the total CO2 emissions from international
 shipping.2


 Shipping industry

 During the period under review, the shipping industry experienced greater volatility, largely attributed to tariff shocks
 and geopolitical tensions. Notably, the Baltic Exchange Dry Index (“BDI”) reached a twelve-month low at 715 points
 on 31 January 2025, while rebounding to a twelve-month high of 2,120 points on 23 July 20253. This surge, driven
 by the increase in the capesize index, could signal a potential recovery of the shipping industry in 2H2025, although
 the outlook remains subject to macroeconomic conditions and market volatility.
