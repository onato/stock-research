[Company-wide]
Q: Concerning the impact of the situation in the Middle East: While there is high
   dependence on the Middle East for methanol, what is the outlook for securing
   raw materials for POM and acetic acid? Additionally, for products where raw
   material costs are rising sharply, is it possible to pass on these costs to prices?
A: We source methanol from multiple regions, including the Middle East, North and
   Central America, and Southeast Asia, so the risk of supply disruptions due to the
   situation in the Middle East is low. We have secured supply through June and
   July, and we are currently coordinating with suppliers regarding the period
   beyond that.
   In response to rising raw material prices, we have been notifying customers of
   index-linked price adjustments for POM since April. Additionally, as announced
   on our official website, we have implemented two rounds of price increases for
   acetic acid and ethyl acetate. The first round of price increases has been
   generally accepted, and negotiations regarding the second round are currently
   underway.
   Currently, methanol prices remain high at USD 640 per ton, while acetic acid
   prices have not risen as significantly. In our acetyl chain, acetic acid export sales
   are the most significantly impacted by methanol and acetic acid market
   conditions. However, since these exports are not profitable, we have been
   curbing sales volumes since the previous fiscal period. Consequently, passing on
   price increases through derivative products has become a key priority.
[High Performance Polymers]
Q: Regarding the POM business in China, while methanol market conditions there
  differ from those in other regions, what is the current status of raw material
  costs and price pass-through?
A: Our plants in China source raw materials based on domestic methanol market
  conditions, so there is no significant cost difference compared to local
  competitors. However, since the rise in methanol prices in China has been
  relatively gradual, it is not easy to pass on these cost increases to customers.
  Our policy is to proceed with price adjustments while seeking our customers’
  understanding.


Q: Regarding the FY2026 forecast for High-Performance Polymers: What are the
  scale of scheduled maintenance, depreciation expenses, and supply-and-
  demand trends in China?
A: While the Fuji Plant underwent a large-scale scheduled maintenance in FY2025,
  a small-scale scheduled maintenance is planned for FY2026, and maintenance
  costs are expected to decrease by approximately 600 million yen compared to
  the previous fiscal year. Similarly, at the Malaysia Plant, the elimination of the
  scheduled maintenance carried out in FY2025—which cost just under 3 billion
  yen—will have a positive impact. Meanwhile, there will be no significant changes
  in terms of scheduled maintenance at the China Plant.
  Regarding depreciation expenses, in China, they are expected to increase by
  approximately 600 million yen due to the second expansion of the POM plant
  (Note: The figure of 300 million yen mentioned in the response during the
  financial results briefing was incorrect), while at the Malaysia plant, depreciation
  is expected to decrease by approximately 1 billion yen as the asset reaches the
  end of its useful life.
  In FY2024, we expanded the production capacity of our China plant to 90,000
  tons through the first expansion of the POM Plant. This expansion serves as a
  substitute for the volumes previously exported from Japan and Malaysia to China.
  With the second expansion scheduled to begin operations in the second half of
  FY2026, we plan to shift production away from the portion currently
  supplemented by imports from Malaysia and to capture growing demand in China.
  We expect the China plant to continue operating at full capacity.
[Materials]
Q: Regarding the forecast for the materials business in FY2026, you mentioned that
   you expect prices to decline for acetate tow sold to local manufacturers. Could
   you provide more details?
A: For local manufacturers, which account for 20% of our acetate tow sales volume,
   the inventory adjustment that began in FY2025 is still ongoing. Sales volumes
   have been lower than initially anticipated, and it has become difficult to secure
   the new contracts we had planned. One factor contributing to the prolonged
   inventory adjustment is a shift in customers’ perceptions of optimal inventory
   levels. While customers previously held excess inventory, they are now working
   to optimize their stock levels as supply remains sufficient. Meanwhile, sales to
   major tobacco manufacturers—which account for approximately 80% of total
   sales—are based on long-term contracts, so there have been no significant
   changes in sales volume.


Q: Are sales of acetate tow to local customers being affected by the situation in the
   Middle East or by increased production by competitors? Also, when do you expect
   the inventory adjustment to be resolved?
A：We believe that the impact of inventory adjustments is greater than that of the
   overall increase in market supply. We expect the inventory adjustments to be
   resolved after January 2027. While we typically begin negotiations around
   autumn for contracts covering the January–December period, we have already
   received inquiries from some local customers.


Q：Regarding the EBITDA forecast analysis on page 22 of the presentation materials,
   what would be the impact if we were to consider only the materials segment?
   The EBITDA for the Materials segment under the new segmentation is negative
   4 billion yen—what is the breakdown of that figure?
A：The negative impact of price influences and increased expenses is dragging
   down Materials’ EBITDA. In terms of volume, sales growth contributed a positive
   2.3 billion yen, while an improvement in plant utilization—due to lower plant
   operating rates in the previous period resulting from production adjustments
   (excluding the impact of the CO plant)—contributed a positive 4.4 billion yen.
   These factors result in a total volume-related impact of approximately 6.7 billion
   yen. On the other hand, price-related factors—including a decline in selling prices
   and rising raw material costs—represent a negative impact of just over 10 billion
   yen. The impact of the CO plant trouble is also included in the material costs,
   but this amounts to a positive 5.4 billion yen. While the net result of these factors
   is positive, this is offset by increases in expenses due to higher R&D costs and
   the absence of production adjustments from the previous period, as well as the
   negative impact of the lower-of-cost-or-market method.
Q：I understand that the price impact amounts to 10 billion yen, but which has a
   greater impact: the selling price or the purchase price? Regarding the price of
   pulp, I believe it is determined by an annual formula contract, but is this covered
   by the contract within the formula, or is it outside of it?
A：The decline in sales prices is having a greater impact. While pulp prices are
   rising—partly due to current cost increases—the main factor affecting this fiscal
   period is that some of our pulp suppliers have announced price increases. Since
   we have certain product lines that require the exclusive use of these specific
   products, we are negotiating with the pulp manufacturers, but we have had to
   accept some of the increases. In that sense, the price increase is driven more by
   special factors than by the standard pricing formula.


[Smart]
Q：Regarding Smart Segment’s solvents for the semiconductor industry, are there
   any issues with rising raw material costs or procurement? What is the current
   status of inquiries and price pass-through?
 A：We source propylene oxide, a key raw material, from two domestic suppliers,
   and we have secured a supply until June. Regarding other raw materials, while
   it is difficult to obtain delivery dates more than two months in advance due to
   our suppliers’ monthly naphtha procurement cycles, there are currently no
   concerns about supply disruptions. We will continue to coordinate with our
   suppliers. In the semiconductor sector, demand related to AI is strong, and given
   customers’ high need for stable supply, price revisions are generally being
   accepted in full.
Q：If price increases resulting from cost pass-through have taken hold, is there
   room for better-than-expected results in FY2026?
A：The purpose of this price increase is to pass on the cost of rising raw material
   prices, and we will not raise prices beyond the amount of the cost increase. While
   there is a possibility of higher-than-expected profits, we prioritize stable supply.
   We aim to achieve our current projected segment profit of 1 billion yen.
