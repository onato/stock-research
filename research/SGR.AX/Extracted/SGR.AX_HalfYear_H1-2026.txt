GOING CONCERN1
•   There remains material uncertainty regarding the Group’s ability to continue as a going concern. Key interdependent events and initiatives in the near terms which are critical to the Group’s liquidity and financial outlook include:
    •     the quantum and timing of the AUSTRAC penalty;
    •     continued support of the Group’s Senior Facility Agreement (SFA) lenders;
    •     the Group’s ability to successfully complete the WhiteHawk refinancing proposal;
    •     completing the transaction entered into with the joint venture partners to exit the DBC joint venture and consolidate the Gold Coast assets. The exit from DBC is dependent on the satisfaction of various conditions precedent are which
          outside the control of the Group, including releasing the Group from its parent company guarantee (~$0.7 billion The Star share of drawn DBC debt);
    •     the Group's ability to return to suitability via the restoration of The Star Sydney’s casino licence, and the withdrawal by the Queensland Government of the suspension of The Star Gold Coast’s casino licence;
    •     driving revenue growth through customer-focused initiatives and implementing further cost out during FY26.

•        If the Group is unable to successfully address or resolve any one or more of the matters outlined above, this is likely to impact the Group’s ability to remain a going concern and its ability to pay its debts as and when they fall due. The interim
         financial report does not include any adjustments relating to the recoverability and classification of recorded asset amounts or to the amounts and classifications of liabilities that might be necessary should the entity not continue as a going
         concern.

BUSINESS UPDATE


                                       •   H1 FY26 normalised revenue was $585 million1, down 10% from H1 FY25. The reduction in revenue was due to:
                                                 – Continued challenged operating conditions due to the impact of mandatory carded play and cash limits in NSW, and stricter regulatory requirements across
                                                   all properties
                                                 – The closure of the Treasury Brisbane Casino in August 2024 and commencement of trading at The Star Brisbane (which is equity accounted)
                                       •   H1 FY26 EBITDA was a loss of $8 million, an improvement of 71% from H1 FY25, reflecting lower corporate costs and an increase in The Star Brisbane operator fee.
                                           Operating expenses remain elevated due to remediation and transformation activities, with increased resourcing in risk, controls and safer gaming functions
                                       •   Despite the significant challenges faced by the business, we are working on opportunities to improve revenue which, in conjunction with our cost-out initiatives and
   H1 FY26                                 roll-off of remediation costs over the next 24 months, are expected to support earnings improvement over the medium term.

   OVERVIEW                            •   Cost out initiatives, including the streamlining of the corporate office, are being implemented and explored to support long-term success of the organisation, and
                                           strengthen The Star’s financial position.
                                       •   The Bally’s Corporation and Investment Holdings strategic investment approved by shareholders in June 2025 has completed during the period
                                       •   The focus on liquidity remains a key priority for The Star, including management of the Company’s existing debt facilities and refinance alternatives.
                                       •   The Star continues to prioritise a return to suitability including delivering and embedding the key elements of the remediation plan. The reinstatement of Star’s
                                           casino licences is critical to improving performance, attracting and retaining the best people and ensuring ongoing access to capital
                                       •   The Star continues to work with its joint venture partners to exit its equity interest in Destination Brisbane Consortium (DBC) and consolidate its position on the Gold

BUSINESS UPDATE – LIQUIDITY AND REFINANCING PROCESS

               •   The focus on liquidity remains a key priority for The Star, including management of the company’s existing debt facilities and refinance alternatives
               •   On 30 September 2025, The Star confirmed its lenders agreed to provide covenant waivers for the September 2025 testing period

 liquidity     •   On 27 February 2026, the Group received a waiver of the 31 December 2025 covenant tests under the SFA.
               •   Under the terms of the waiver, the Group will need to deliver a refinancing commitment letter by 31 March 2026, and execute a refinancing of the SFA by 15 May
                   2026 to avoid a default under the terms of its SFA.




               •   In January 2026, The Star appointed MA Moelis Australia as financial advisor to assist with the evaluation and execution of financing options for the Group
               •   On 26 February 2026, The Star executed a term sheet with WhiteHawk Capital Partners (WhiteHawk) for a proposed refinancing of the Group’s debt (Refinancing
                   Proposal). The proposal provides for refinancing of the Group’s existing debt and incremental liquidity.
 refinancing   •   The Star cautions that the executed term sheet is non-binding and may not lead to a definitive credit agreement.

 process       •   The Star and WhiteHawk are working towards a binding commitment by 31 March 2026, and to consummate the Refinancing Proposal by 15 May 2026.
               •   The Star believes that it will be able to complete the Refinancing Proposal, including the execution of long form documentation, confirmatory due diligence and
                   satisfaction of other customary conditions (noting that a determination of the AUSTRAC case is not a condition to completing the Refinancing Proposal).
