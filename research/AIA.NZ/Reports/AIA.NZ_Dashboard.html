DCF Valuation

⚠ Model approach: This is a component-built owner-FCF DCF, not a simple multiple-of-FCF extrapolation. Reported free cash flow has been negative every year FY2023-FY2025 (-NZ$247m to -NZ$581m) and management guides a further NZ$1.0-1.6bn/yr of capex through roughly FY2029/30 as the domestic jet terminal and airfield expansion are built. The model discounts this full negative-then-positive 10-year cash flow path at a 7.5% WACC. Result: a base intrinsic value of NZ$1.90 vs a market price of NZ$8.76 — implying just ~6.2x EV/EBITDA against AIA's own 17.8x historical average (ex-outlier years). That gap is the market paying a duration/scarcity premium for irreplaceable monopoly airport infrastructure that a textbook Gordon-growth DCF, discounting years of capex-supercycle cash burn, cannot reproduce. The bear case's negative equity value (-NZ$0.68/share) is a leverage-risk warning signal, not a literal price target — most of the capex programme is already contractually committed (NZ$1.31bn of PP&E purchase obligations at Dec-2025) and debt-funded, so it does not simply switch off in a downside scenario. Treat the DCF outputs below as a conservative cash-flow floor and cross-check against the EV/EBITDA memo and regulatory-asset-base context, not as a standalone fair value call.

This is not a DCF error: it quantifies the size of the duration/scarcity premium the market assigns to irreplaceable, regulated monopoly airport infrastructure — a premium a textbook WACC-based Gordon-growth DCF, discounting a multi-year capex-driven cash burn, structurally cannot reproduce.
