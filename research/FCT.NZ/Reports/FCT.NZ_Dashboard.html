⚠️

This is NOT a standard FCF/DCF valuation. F&C Investment Trust is a closed-end investment trust, not an operating company — it has no capex, no stock-based compensation, no working-capital cycle, and its reported net income is dominated by unrealised mark-to-market portfolio revaluation (e.g. H1-2022 total return -£630.1m vs FY2021 +£919.3m). The valuation below uses a NAV-compounding + exit-discount model instead: intrinsic value = today's known NAV per share × (1 − a scenario fair discount to NAV), cross-checked against a 5-year IRR build of dividends plus a discounted exit value. NAV is already stated net of all borrowings (the £544m of long-term loans plus RCF drawdowns are deducted inside the published NAV figure), so net debt is deliberately NOT subtracted a second time anywhere in this model — portfolio gearing is captured entirely inside the total-return assumption as a return amplifier.

The base case shows only +1.7% NZD upside — essentially fairly valued. That is not a modelling shortfall: F&C's cost of equity (~9%, appropriate for a large, liquid, globally diversified FTSE 100 equity fund) sits very close to its own realistic expected NAV total return (~7.5% base case). Because expected return and the discount rate are nearly equal, almost all of the available edge in this ticker comes from the discount to NAV narrowing or widening from here — there is very little edge in the NAV-compounding engine itself, which is priced efficiently.

Read F&C as a low-cost, income-growing, globally diversified equity holding (55-year dividend streak, 0.45% ongoing charges), not as a value/re-rating opportunity at today's ~8.7% discount.
