Owner Cash Flow & Hospitality Operations

Why headline free cash flow overstates what owners actually keep, and the venue-level drivers of the business

Reported FCF vs Owner FCF (after lease principal & interest)

Savor is an IFRS-16 lessee across 18 venues: lease principal (~NZ$3.05m in FY2025) and lease interest (~NZ$0.72m) both sit in financing cash flow, so reported FCF (NZ$5.98m FY2025) overstates what owners actually keep by ~NZ$3.8m a year. Owner FCF was NEGATIVE FY2021-23 during the venue build-out and only turned positive in FY2024-25; FY2026's estimated 0.55m is a capex-elevated trough (Bar Ziti and Flush Golf fit-outs), not the run rate.

Operating Cash Flow vs CapEx

FY2026 capex not yet disclosed (results release only); DCF model estimates ~NZ$2.4m, elevated versus NZ$0.3-1.3m in FY2024-25 for the two new Britomart venues.
Model approach: Two things to hold in mind reading this section. First, the base-case intrinsic value of NZ$0.135 sits BELOW the current price of NZ$0.172 -- on a probability-weighted basis (NZ$0.123) the house model reads the stock as roughly 28% overvalued, even though it screens cheap on trailing multiples (FY2026 EV/EBITDA ~5.5x, P/E ~10.9x). The gap is a size/illiquidity/cyclicality premium the market is not charging: at NZ$0.172 the market is capitalising the promised January-2027 dividend at roughly 13.6-14.4%, well inside this model's 16.5% WACC. Second, the bear-case intrinsic value is NEGATIVE (-NZ$0.036/share): in the bear path, owner FCF of only NZ$0.5-0.7m/year does not cover the NZ$6.3m of net bank debt at a 16.5% discount rate. That is a real signal about a levered, single-city microcap with 65% of earnings concentrated in five summer months -- not a modelling artifact -- and limited liability floors the actual downside at zero.
