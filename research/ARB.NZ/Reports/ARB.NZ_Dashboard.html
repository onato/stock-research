                DCF Valuation

                ⚠ Terminal Value Floor-Bound — Sanity Check Tripped Low, Not High

                The component-built owner-FCF DCF initially produced terminal exit multiples of just 2.3x–3.3x EV/Adjusted-EBITDA across all three scenarios — below ArborGen's own 10-year historical MINIMUM multiple of 4.64x (set at the FY2026 impairment-driven trough). The market has never, even at its worst, capitalised this business that cheaply. This is because owner-FCF margin is still rising at Year 10 in every scenario (base: 3.0%→5.9%) — a pure Gordon-growth capitalisation of an immature terminal margin materially understates value.

                Fix applied: terminal value is floor/cap-bound to ArborGen's own 10-year EV/Adjusted-EBITDA trading range — Base floor 4.64x (10yr min) / cap 7.35x (10yr avg); Bull floor 7.35x / cap 11.30x (10yr max); Bear floor 2.78x (60% of 10yr min, further de-rating) / cap 4.64x. Gordon growth fell below the floor in all three scenarios, so all three are floor-bound.

                Investment framing: this remains a deep-value / asset-discount situation, not a growth story. At NZ$0.065 the stock trades at just 0.16x book value and near the bottom of its 10-year P/B range (0.24x–0.47x), reflecting a market that is pricing structural US South decline and balance-sheet pressure. Owner FCF has been roughly break-even for a decade. The probability-weighted IV (NZ$0.022, weighted 45% bear / 35% base / 20% bull given leadership vacancy, rising net debt and management's own “structural not cyclical” framing of the US downturn) sits below the current price — this DCF does NOT support a value case at today's price on a pure income-approach basis. Any bull thesis here rests on Brazil execution, an eventual US recovery, and/or asset optionality (Ridgeville building sale, $54.1m IP carrying value) not fully captured by the income approach.
