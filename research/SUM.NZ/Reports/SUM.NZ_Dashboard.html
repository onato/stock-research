Company Overview

Summerset Group Holdings is one of New Zealand's largest retirement village developers and operators, developing, building, and operating a portfolio of 44 villages across New Zealand with an expanding presence in Victoria, Australia. Founded in 1997 and listed on the NZX in 2011, the company operates under the Occupation Right Agreement (ORA) model whereby residents pay an upfront licence fee for the right to occupy a unit, with Summerset earning a Deferred Management Fee (DMF) of 25% of the ORA price when they leave. At FY2025 year-end, total assets stood at NZ$9.2 billion with 8,673 total units in portfolio and 9,500+ residents.

Business Model

Capital-intensive ORA model: residents pay upfront for occupation rights, generating DMF income on exit (25% of ORA price over five years) plus recurring care fees and village services.

Bull Case

  • Demographic tailwind is one of the most powerful structural growth drivers in NZ/AU investment universe - decades of compounding demand from a land bank assembled at historical cost
  • Australia momentum is real: Cranbourne North village centre delivered, Chirnside Park 60% pre-sold, 4 villages simultaneously under construction - if Summerset replicates NZ model, it doubles TAM
  • Resale gains are compounding, capital-light income: 32 villages generating resales, embedded value $1.9b of future DMF cash requiring no additional capital
  • RBNZ rate cuts reduce finance costs AND improve NZ property conditions (dual tailwind) - Summerset is operationally leveraged to normalising rates
  • Balance sheet discipline sharply differentiates from Ryman (required two ~$1b capital raises since 2023) - Summerset maintained 27.8% margins through the difficult 2022-2025 cycle

Bear Case

  • Debt of $1.97b leaves limited headroom - any unexpected stress could require dilutive equity issuance or painful build rate reduction
  • Australian execution risk is significant and often underestimated: scaling from 56 to 300 units/year by end FY27 is ambitious; cost overruns could impair returns
  • Retirement Villages Act mandatory buy-back reform could create liquidity pressure in a weak market
  • Care business structural headwind: 58% of opex is employee costs and government funding lags wage inflation persistently
  • IFRS NPAT highly volatile (swung from $332m FY24 to $259m FY25) due to fair value movements - confuses investors and pressures stock when property market stalls

DCF Valuation

Model approach: The DCF sanity check tripped on the prior model, which used Operating Cash Flow ($548m) as the free-cash-flow proxy and produced a base intrinsic value of $38.69 (≈263% upside) — implying ~36x P/E, a multiple Summerset has never sustained. For an ORA/DMF retirement-village operator, reported OCF is inflated by resident occupation-right receipts (capital inflows the company must continuously redeploy into new units), so it overstates distributable cash roughly 2.3x. The model was rebuilt using Underlying Profit ($234.2m) — management's preferred earnings measure — discounted at cost of equity (no net-debt subtraction, since UP is already after finance costs), with terminal value capped at historical exit P/E multiples. The corrected base IV of $17.87 implies ~18.5x P/E versus a 10-year median of ~15x and 1.30x NTA (below the 2.11x historical max), now within sanity bounds. Framing: at $8.70 (~9x Underlying Profit, 0.63x NTA — below its 10-year minimum on both) the thesis is income + steady demographic-driven growth at a depressed multiple with a re-rate to normalised earnings, NOT a fair-value-revaluation multibagger. The bear case ($6.57, −25%) provides a genuine downside anchor.
Methodology Note: The FCF proxy is Underlying Profit (management's preferred earnings measure, which strips out fair-value property revaluations), NOT operating cash flow. For ORA/DMF operators, OCF is inflated by resident ORA receipts — capital/financing inflows that must be redeployed into new units — so it is not distributable free cash. Underlying Profit is post-finance-cost, so it is discounted at cost of equity and net debt is not subtracted again. Terminal value is capped at the scenario's historical exit P/E (bear 12.7x / base 14.9x median / bull 29x max).

NTA Cross-Check

Stock trades at NZD $8.70 vs NTA $13.75/share (P/NTA 0.63x) — a 37% discount to net tangible assets. NTA is the revalued book value of the physical retirement-village portfolio and provides an asset-backed floor. The discount reflects the market's pricing of $1.97bn net debt, NZ Retirement Villages Act reform risk, NZ property-market uncertainty, and Australian execution risk.
