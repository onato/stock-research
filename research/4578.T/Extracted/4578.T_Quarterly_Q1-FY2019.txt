1)  Pharmaceuticals
  Revenue for the first three months of FY2019 (from January 1 to March 31, 2019) totaled ¥209,411 million (up 10.5% year
on year), with business profit of ¥32,776 million (up 7.0%), reflecting the contribution of increases in sales of drugs in global
markets and new drugs in Japan. As a result, increases in both revenue and profit were achieved.

 Main products
   The Company positions the long acting antipsychotic agent ABILIFY MAINTENA, the antipsychotic agent REXULTI, and
 the V2-receptor antagonist Samsca/JINARC＊1/JYNARQUE*2 as its three global products. The sales of those products totaled
 ¥72,661 million (up 39.7% year on year).

     *1: Brand name for ADPKD treatment in multiple regions outside Japan
     *2: Brand name for ADPKD treatment in the U.S.

  Antipsychotic agent ABILIFY MAINTENA for extended-release injectable suspension, for intramuscular use
      Sales of ABILIFY MAINTENA was ¥23,031 million (up 16.8% year on year) reflecting the contribution of expanding
    prescriptions as a treatment for bipolar disorder, of which the additional indication was approved in the U.S. in 2017, in
    addition to efforts to promote the product’s efficacy as a treatment for schizophrenia in global markets and growing
    awareness of the drug’s ease of use.

  Antipsychotic agent REXULTI
       Antipsychotic agent REXULTI has been highly evaluated in the U.S. for its efficacy and safety as a new treatment
    option for an adjunctive therapy in major depressive disorder and a treatment for schizophrenia. In Japan, REXULTI was
    launched in April 2018 as a treatment for schizophrenia, and has smoothly increased its prescriptions. As a result, sales of
    REXULTI was ¥19,105 million (up 31.2% year on year).

  V2-receptor antagonist Samsca
       In Japan, high recommendation in various guidelines contributed to expanding its prescriptions for the treatments for
    hepatic edema and cardiac edema. Also as a drug for autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (“ADPKD”), an
    intractable kidney disease, the effort toward growing awareness of the disease and the promotion of appropriate use of
    Samsca according to the treatment guidelines led to an increase in the number of patients and high continuity rates. Sales
    of Samsca as an aquaretic agent also have grown overseas and, as a result, overall sales of Samsca was ¥19,641 million
    (up 22.1% year on year).

  V2-receptor antagonist JINARC/JYNARQUE
     As a treatment for ADPKD, JYNARQUE (brand name in the U.S.) was launched in the U.S. in May 2018. Sales
    increased sharply due to a smooth launch of the Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) in the U.S. and an
    increase in prescriptions in the country owing to the effort toward growing awareness of the disease. In addition, the
    number of patient users has been increasing in European markets. As a result, sales was ¥10,883 million (up 554.6% year
    on year).

  Anti-cancer agent LONSURF
      In the U.S., activities to promote the product as the therapies for unresectable advanced or recurrent colorectal cancer
    contributed to an increase in sales. In addition, approval was granted in February 2019 for the additional indications as a
    treatment for the unresectable advanced or recurrent gastric cancer. In Europe, although the prescriptions increased as the
    number of countries in Europe where it has been put on the market is growing, the sales decreased due to a delay in
    shipments to Servier, our licensing partner. As a result, overall sales totaled ¥8,289 million (up 3.3% year on year).

  Others
     Sales of antiallergic agent Bilanoa increased significantly (sales of ¥4,264 million, up 211.1% year on year), as the
    product has been highly evaluated by medical specialists and patients as a new treatment option for pollen allergy.
    Increases in sales of antiepileptic drug E Keppra (sales of ¥9,965 million, up 16.3% year on year) and anti-cancer agent
    Abraxane (sales of ¥6,464 million, up 21.5%) and other factors also contributed to increases in both revenue and profit
    for the period under review.

2)  Nutraceuticals
    Factors that boosted sales were growth of overseas sales volume of POCARI SWEAT, an electrolyte supplement drink, a
 sharp rise in sales of EQUELLE, a food product containing equol, due to the expansion of new customers, and an increase in
 sales at Daiya Foods Inc., which develops, manufactures and sells plant-based foods in North America. Meanwhile, sales was
 affected by limited distribution in some regions due to climate factors, a decrease in sales in the U.S. supplement business
 mainly due to inventory adjustment at retail stores and a decrease in sales at Nutrition & Santé SAS, a subsidiary that sells
 nutrition and health food in Europe, mainly affected by new competitors entering the market. As a result, revenue for the
 period under review totaled ¥72,938 million (down 0.4% year on year), with business profit of ¥6,568 million (down 19.0%).

  POCARI SWEAT
     While the sports drink market shrank in Japan*1, sales volume was flat compared with the prior year period as

3)   Consumer products
   Sales volume for mineral water products increased, with main contributors being direct sales channels and the mineral water
mainstay brand CRYSTAL GEYSER’s 700-milliliter bottles, the largest size for personal use, which can be attributed to the
success of proactive communication marketing activities for expanding the brand’s user base, mainly targeting young people.
Meanwhile, sales were affected by a fall in sales volume for MATCH, a carbonated electrolyte drink containing vitamins, in
reaction to the previous year’s revamping of Berry MATCH, a product flavored with mixed berries, as well as a decrease in
investment gain on the equity method. As a result, revenue for the period under review totaled ¥7,048 million (down 3.5% year
on year), with business profit of ¥1,639 million (down 40.1%).

4)    Others
   In the specialty chemical business, sales remained steady compared with the prior year period, despite a decline in shipments
of foaming agents. In the fine chemical business, sales decreased compared with the prior year period due to factors such as a
delay in order receipts of antibiotic substance.
   In the transportation and warehousing business, sales increased compared with the prior year period mainly due to the growth
in handling volume of the Group products and increase in new customers spurred by the “common distribution platform” in line
with external customers.
   As a result, revenue for the period under review totaled ¥33,143 million (down 1.9% year on year), with business profit of
¥1,935 million (down 21.2%).

Assets
 Total assets as of March 31, 2019 were ¥2,616,433 million, an increase of ¥139,799 million compared to ¥2,476,634 million at
the end of the previous fiscal year. Current assets increased by ¥75,618 million, and non-current assets increased by ¥64,180
million.

(Current Assets)
 Total current assets as of March 31, 2019 were ¥1,008,721 million, an increase of ¥75,618 million compared to ¥933,102
million at the end of the previous fiscal year. This was mainly due to increases in cash and cash equivalents by ¥51,619 million,
inventories by ¥8,235 million and other financial assets by ¥22,300 million, while there was a decrease in trade and other
receivables by ¥11,021 million.

(Non-current Assets)
 Total non-current assets as of March 31, 2019 were ¥1,607,712 million, an increase of ¥64,180 million compared to
¥1,543,532 million at the end of the previous fiscal year. This was mainly due to increases in property, plant and equipment by
¥59,802 million and other financial assets by ¥9,222 million as a result of the adoption of IFRS 16 “Leases” (hereinafter
referred to as “IFRS 16”).

Liabilities
(Current Liabilities)
 Total current liabilities as of March 31, 2019 were ¥443,735 million, an increase of ¥16,233 million compared to ¥427,502
million at the end of the previous fiscal year. This was mainly due to increases in lease liabilities by ¥12,050 million and other
current liabilities by ¥16,838 million as a result of the adoption of IFRS 16, while there was a decrease in trade and other
payables by ¥11,725 million.

(Non-current Liabilities)
 Total non-current liabilities as of March 31, 2019 were ¥437,704 million, an increase of ¥120,838 million compared to
¥316,865 million at the end of the previous fiscal year. This was mainly due to increases in bonds and borrowings by ¥76,874
million as a result of the issuance of bonds and lease liabilities by ¥47,611 million due to the adoption of IFRS 16.

Equity
 Total equity as of March 31, 2019 were ¥1,734,993 million, an increase of ¥2,727 million compared to ¥1,732,266 million at
the end of the previous fiscal year. This was mainly due to an increase in other components of equity by ¥10,545 million as a
result of stock market and exchange rate fluctuations, while there was a decrease in retained earnings by ¥7,980 million as a
result of a ¥27,092 million payment of dividends and quarterly net income attributable to owners of the Company of ¥19,152
million.

                 IFRS                                Description of new standards, interpretations and amendments

IFRS 16   Leases                           Amendment concerning accounting treatment for leases
      Adoption of IFRS 16 “Leases”
        Starting from FY2019, the Group adopted IFRS 16 “Leases” (issued in January 2016, hereinafter referred to as “IFRS
      16”).
        On adoption of IFRS 16, leases previously classified as operating leases under the principles of IAS 17 “Leases”
      (hereinafter referred to as “IAS 17”) were recognized as right-of-use assets and lease liabilities as of the date of the
      application of IFRS 16 (January 1, 2019). In addition, operating lease payments that were expensed in the condensed
      interim consolidated statement of income as incurred under the previous accounting standards are recorded as
      depreciation expense for right-of-use assets and interest cost for lease liabilities, and reclassified from a reduction in
      cash flows from operating activities to a reduction in cash flows from financing activities in the condensed interim
      consolidated statement of cash flows.
        In accordance with IFRS 16, for the leases that the Group has contracted as a lessee, right-of-use assets are measured
      at acquisition cost, and lease liabilities are measured at the present value of total lease payables.
        Right-of-use assets are depreciated by using the straight-line method from the commencement date to the earlier of the
      end of the useful life of the right-of-use asset or the end of the lease term.
        Lease payments are apportioned between the financial costs and the reduction in the lease liabilities based on the
      interest method. The financial costs are recognized in the condensed interim consolidated statement of income.
        The Group elects not to recognize right-of-use assets and lease liabilities for leases on intangible assets and short-term
      leases within 12 months. The Group recognizes the total lease payments associated with short-term leases on either a
      straight-line method or another systematic basis over the lease term.
        In accordance with the transitional measures under IFRS 16, the Group retrospectively adopted IFRS 16 and
      recognized the cumulative effects of the application as an adjustment to the opening balance of retained earnings for
      2019Q1. In transitioning to IFRS 16, the Group chooses the practical expedient detailed in IFRS 16 paragraph C3 and
      assesses whether contracts contain leases in accordance with IAS 17 and IFRIC 4 “Determining whether an
      Arrangement contains a Lease.”
        Lease liabilities are measured at present value of the remaining lease payments discounted using the lessee’s
      incremental borrowing rate at the date of initial application.
        The weighted average lessee’s incremental borrowing rate applied to lease liabilities recognized in the condensed
      interim consolidated statement of financial position at the date of initial application is 3.9%. Right-of-use assets are
      initially measured at the initial measurement amount of the lease liability adjusted by the amount of any prepaid or
      accrued lease payments.
        As a result, compared with the application of the former accounting standard, as of the beginning of FY2019 Q1,,
      right-of-use assets increased by ¥59,324 million as property, plant and equipment in the condensed interim consolidated
      statement of financial position, and retained earnings decreased by ¥23 million. The following is the reconciliation of
      operating lease contracts disclosed applying IAS 17 as of December 31, 2018 and lease liabilities recognized in the
      condensed consolidated statement of financial position at the date of initial application.

       (1) Overview of reportable segments
            The Group’s reportable segments are the constituent units of the Group for which separate financial information is
           available and which are subject to periodic reviews by the Board of Directors in order to make decisions on
           allocation of business resources and to evaluate the business performances of the respective segments.
           As the Group’s holding company, the Company directs the Group’s strategic planning, monitors group operations
           and provides various services to its group companies. Business activities are conducted by the Group’s subsidiaries
           and associates.
            Centering on healthcare business, the Group operates activities inside and outside Japan relating to the following
           four reportable segments: “Pharmaceuticals,” “Nutraceuticals,” “Consumer products” and “Others” businesses.
            The Group defines the reportable segments as follows: “Pharmaceuticals” comprises manufacturing and sales of
           prescription drugs, intravenous solutions, and others.
          “Nutraceuticals” comprises manufacturing and sales of functional beverages, etc., over-the-counter drugs and
         nutritional supplements. “Consumer products” comprises manufacturing and sales of mineral water, soft beverages
         and food products.
            “Others” encompasses logistics, warehousing, manufacturing and sales of chemical products, evaluation systems
