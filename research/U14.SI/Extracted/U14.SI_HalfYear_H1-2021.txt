  1   Basis of Preparation


      The condensed interim financial statements for the six months ended 30 June 2021 have been prepared in accordance with SFRS(I) 1-
      34 Interim Financial Reporting issued by the Accounting Standards Council Singapore. The condensed interim financial statements
      do not include all the information required for a complete set of financial statements. However, selected explanatory notes are
      included to explain events and transactions that are significant to an understanding of the changes in the Group’s financial position
      and performance of the Group since the last annual financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2020.

      The accounting policies adopted are consistent with those of the previous financial year which were prepared in accordance with
      SFRS(I)s, except for the adoption of new and amended standards as set out in Note 2.

      The condensed interim financial statements are presented in Singapore dollar which is the Company’s functional currency.



  2   New and amended standards adopted by the Group


      The Group adopted the standards, anendments and interpretations to existing standards that are mandatory for application for the
      financial period beginning 1 January 2021 or from their effective date, if later. The following are the amended financial reporting
      standards that are relevant to the Group:

      -   Amendments to SFRS(I) 9 Financial Instruments and SFRS(I) 7 Financial Instruments: Disclosures - Interest Rate Benchmark Reform
          (Phase 2)


      The adoption of the above amendments did not result in any substantial change to the Group's accounting policies nor any
      significant impact on the financial statements.



  3   Key accounting estimates, assumptions and judgements

      In preparing the condensed interim financial statements, management has made estimates, assumptions and judgements that affect
      the application of accounting policies and the reported amounts of assets and liabilities, income and expenses. Actual results may
      differ from these estimates.

      Estimates, assumptions and judgements are continually evaluated on an ongoing basis.              Revisions to accounting estimates are
      recognised in the period in which the estimates are revised and in any future periods affected.

      The estimates, assumptions and judgements applicable for the current financial period were the same as those applied to the
      consolidated financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2020, as follows:
      -   Fair values of investment properties
      -   Classification of the Group’s serviced suites as investment property or property, plant and equipment
      -   Revenue and cost of sales recognition from sales of development properties
      -   Valuation of development properties
      -   Impairment of hotel properties
      -   Revenue recognition of technology operations
      -   Impairment of trade receivables
      -   Fair values of unquoted financial assets, at FVOCI

      Information about assumptions and estimation uncertainties that have the most significant risk of resulting in a material adjustment
      within the next interim period is as follows:

      Fair values of investment properties

      The Group carries its investment properties at fair value with corresponding changes being recognised in the income statement. In
      determining fair values, valuation techniques which involve certain estimates are used by valuers. The key assumptions to determine
      the fair value of investment properties include market-corroborated capitalisation rate, growth rate and discount rate.

      The majority of the valuation reports as at 31 December 2020 contained a ‘material valuation uncertainty’ clause. Most of the valuers
      continued to include this ‘material valuation uncertainty’ clause in the valuation reports as at 30 June 2021 as they are still faced with
      an unprecedented set of circumstances caused by COVID-19 and an absence of relevant/sufficient market evidence on which to base
      their judgement. This clause does not invalidate the valuation but implies that there is substantially more uncertainty than under
      normal market conditions.

      The uncertainty caused by the COVID-19 pandemic is factored into the valuation of the investment properties, specifically in
      estimated passing rents, vacancy rates, lease incentives, capitalisation rates, growth rates, discount rates and cash flow projections.

      Management has assessed that the valuation methods and estimates are reflective of current market conditions.

A.   Review


     The statements of financial position of VOL Group Limited ("the Company") and its subsidiaries ("the Group") as at 30 June 2021 and
     the related consolidated income statement, consolidated other comprehensive income, consolidated statement of changes in
     equity, statement of changes in equity and consolidated statement of cash flows for the six-month period then ended and certain
     explanatory notes have not been audited or reviewed by the Company's auditors.



     Review of performance of the Group

     Revenue

     Group revenue in the first half of 2021       increased by $285.3 million or 31% to $1,193.5 million from $908.2 million in the
     corresponding period of 2020. As compared to the respective segment's revenue recorded for the first half of 2020,

     -    revenue from property development for the period was $307.8 million or 81% higher due mainly from higher progressive
          recognition of revenue from Avenue South Residence, The Tre Ver and Clavon, offset partially by lower revenue from Amber45, V
         on Shenton and Park Eleven, Shanghai.
     -    revenue from property investments for the first half of 2021 also increased by $11.0 million or 5% due mainly to lower rental
          rebates extended to tenants affected by the COVID-19 pandemic.
     -    revenue from hotel operations for the six months ended 30 June 2021 was lower by $10.8 million or 8% mainly from the
         Singapore hotels where the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the Singapore hospitality industry was from end of February
         2020 onwards. The Group's Singapore hotels entered into Government Quarantine Facility ("GQF") contracts commencing March
         2020, but such GOF business have significantly reduced in the first half of 2021. The hotels in China however contributed higher
          revenue for the period as compared to the corresponding period last year as China's tourism industry is recovering post
         pandemic.
     -   technology operations recorded a $10.4 million or 9% lower revenue in the current period arising from lower sales of information
         technology due to a delay in fulfilling orders as a result of global supply constraint.
     -    investment income was $12.3 million or 41% lower due mainly to a decrease in final dividends and absence of special dividends
          paid by UOB during the first half of 2021.


     Gross profit margin

     Gross profit margin of 32% for first half of 2021 was lower than the gross profit margin of 38% for first half of 2020 due mainly to (i)
     lower sales from property development projects with higher profit margins; and (ii) reduced margins from the impact of COVID-19 on
     hotel operations.


     Miscellaneous income

     Miscellaneous income in the form of COVID-19 government assistance and grants amounting to $11.4 million for the first half of 2021
     was significantly lower than the $25.5 million recorded for the first half of 2020.


     Expenses

     Marketing and distribution expenses for the half year ended 30 June 2021 were $16.2 million higher than same time last year due
     mainly to higher commission from the sales of units at Avenue South Residence, The Tre Ver, Clavon and Park Eleven, Shanghai.
     Finance expense was lower by $18.7 million or 40% due mainly to the gradual decline in interest rates since the onset of COVID-19.


     Profit & Loss

     The pre-tax profit before fair value losses was $214.3 million, a $17.5 million or 9% increase from the profit of $196.8 million in the first
     half of 2020 due mainly to higher profit from property development and property investment and lower finance expenses.

     With lower attributable fair value losses of $16.9 million recognised for the first half of 2021 compared to $185.8 million recorded for
     the first half of 2020, profit after tax and non-controlling interest increased by $173.5 million or 211% to $91.3 million from a loss of
     $82.1 million in the corresponding period of 2020.


     Net tangible asset and gearing

     The Group's shareholders’ funds rose from $9.79 billion as at 31 December 2020 to $9.93 billion as at 30 June 2021 due mainly to (i)
     fair value gains on financial assets at fair value through other comprehensive income and (ii) profit recognised for the period, set off
     partially by (iii) deduction of dividends paid against retained earnings. Consequently the net tangible asset per ordinary share of the
     Group increased to $11.71 as at 30 June 2021 from $11.55 as at 31 December 2020.

     The Group’s gearing ratio remained constant at 0.29 as at both 30 June 2021 and 31 December 2020.

     A commentary at the date of the announcement of the significant trends and competitive conditions of the industry in which
     the Group operates and any known factors or events that may affect the Group in the next operating period and the next 12
     months


     New COVID-19 variants continued to spread globally with several Southeast Asian countries reporting record high infection rates in
     recent weeks, further disrupting economic activities in the region. According to the Ministry of Trade and Industry, Singapore
     economy grew by 14.7% on a year-on-year basis in the second quarter of 2021 due to the low base in the second quarter of 2020 but
     remained 0.6% below its pre-pandemic level in the second quarter of 2019.


     The private residential property index published by the Urban Redevelopment Authority showed an increase of 0.8% in the second
     quarter of 2021, compared to the 3.3% in the previous quarter. The private residential market continues to remain healthy with a total
     of 6,459 new private homes sold in the first half of 2021 compared with 4,018 in the same period last year. However, the construction
     sector saw rising costs due to manpower shortage and safe management measures which affected productivity at the worksites.


     Singapore office rents in Central Region rose 1.3 per cent in 2Q 2021, compared with the 3.3 per cent increase in the previous quarter.
     Vacancy rates rose from 11.9% in 1Q 2021 to 12.6% in 2Q 2021. Office rents are expected to continue to rise in second half of 2021,
     supported by a gradual economic rebound, steady demand from growth sectors such as technology & media, wealth management,
     family business and healthcare companies, amid a tapering supply pipeline. However, the office rental market may still face
     headwinds in the medium term as more firms review their work space requirements post COVID-19.


     The retail sector continues to face challenges due to the re-imposition of tightening measures in May this year and the latest Phase 2
     (Heightened Alert) in July. Singapore retail rents for the Central Region fell by 0.5 per cent in 2Q 2021, following a decline of 4.4 per
     cent in the previous quarter. Islandwide vacancy rate of retail space remained at 8.5 per cent as at end of 2Q 2021. Retail rents are
     expected to continue to be under pressure albeit at a slower pace as the market is poised to benefit from an improvement in
     economic activity and consumer sentiment on the back of the vaccination rollout.


     With many countries still implementing travel restrictions intended to control the spread of the more contagious COVID-19             variants,
     the hospitality sector is likely to continue to be negatively impacted in the short term due to the lack of international visitors.


     In the United Kingdom, it is expected that the near-term economic uncertainty will recede as lockdown restrictions are lifted and the
     vaccination programme continues its rapid rollout. However, the commercial and hospitality sectors in UK are still likely to face
     challenges in the medium term as the uncertainty continues to linger post-Brexit.
