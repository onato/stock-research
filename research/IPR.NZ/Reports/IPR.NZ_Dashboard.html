DCF Valuation

⚠ Model Approach: Why This Is NOT a Growth DCF

Iperion has never earned a dollar of revenue in any period on record (FY2019 through FY2026, all audited) and free cash flow has been negative every single year (FY2026: −$269,849; FY2025: −$447,900; FY2024: −$734,204). There is no positive base FCF to anchor a projection, no growth rate to compound, and no terminal value that can be meaningfully capitalised via Gordon growth. A conventional component-built or flat-margin FCF DCF applied here would either extrapolate a structural, uninterrupted cash-burn machine into an increasingly negative and meaningless number, or require fabricating a hypothetical future revenue ramp dressed up as a "DCF" when no such business yet exists.

Instead this model uses a probability-weighted asset/scenario framework — the same approach used elsewhere in this repository for AFC.NZ and BGI.NZ. Three explicit scenarios: Bear (65% weight) — related-party support is withdrawn and the company winds up, consuming its remaining $53,803 of net assets in wind-up costs, leaving ordinary equity holders with nothing; Base (28% weight) — the company persists as a going-concern shell, valued at FY2026 net tangible assets plus a modest NZX-listing scarcity value (NZ$200k–$400k, no disclosed RTO counterparty); Bull (7% weight) — the multinational chocolate-manufacturer trial converts into a first signed commercial agreement, ramping to a niche $1.5m revenue run-rate by year 5, net of a heavily dilutive $750,000 capital raise.

The result: a probability-weighted intrinsic value of NZ$0.0002587/share — roughly 91% below the $0.003 stale quote — and even the single most optimistic bull scenario modelled lands 68% below that price. The current 28.7x Price/Book multiple is the historical anomaly, not the model: Iperion's own FY2019–FY2025 P/B range (computed from actual monthly trade prints against reported book value) was just 0.21x–4.3x. The 28.7x reading is a mechanical artifact of a share price frozen since December 2023 while reported book equity collapsed roughly 85% since FY2024 alone — a stale-price artifact, not a re-rating.
