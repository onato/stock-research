<Q&A Session>

Q. The H2 plan for the Department Store Business projects that while gross sales will exceed the
previous forecast, selling, general, and administrative expenses (SGA) will also increase,
resulting in business profit that falls short of the previous forecast. Please explain the reasons
behind this.

A. There is a timing shift of approximately 1.3 billion yen in SGA for H1. Since that amount will be
recognized in H2 or later, business profit for H2 is expected to be lower than originally planned. In
addition, to boost top-line revenue, we anticipate an increase in advertising expenses as well as
commissions, and costs related to system upgrades are also expected to rise in H2. While these
factors will drive top-line growth, they will also lead to an increase in SGA.

Q. It was explained that “fusion” provides new value to customers. Considering examples such
as HAERA, what new opportunities do you think are opening up through this “fusion”?

A. “Fusion” does not mean that Daimaru Matsuzakaya Department Stores and PARCO will simply
merge into one, but rather that the two companies will combine their respective strengths at a high
level. While we believe that HAERA has succeeded in realizing a portion of this, there is still room
to further advance fusion. At HAERA, we are seeing cross-shopping between customers of both
Matsuzakaya and PARCO, and we plan to evaluate the effectiveness of this trend going forward.
   When we conducted customer surveys in various regions as part of the process of formulating the
next Medium-term Business Plan, we found that there were more customers than expected who
shop at PARCO while also visiting Department Stores, as well as customers who shop at
Department Stores while also visiting PARCO and other shopping centers. On the other hand, it is
not widely recognized that Daimaru Matsuzakaya and PARCO are part of the same group. We view
this as a significant area for growth. Specific initiatives will be announced next spring.

Q. Could you explain the factors behind the H1 business profit for the Developer Business and
adjustments exceeding the plan?

A. In the Developer Business, profit margins in the interior design business improved, resulting in
gross profit that exceeded the plan. Adjustments are attributable to a timing shift in the recognition
of the holding company’s standalone expenses to H2, as well as cost containment measures.
However, since adjustments also include inter-segment eliminations, we expect full-year results to
be in line with the initial plan.

Q. I would like to ask about trends among domestic customers at Department Stores,
breaking them down into gaisho customers and non-gaisho customers. Regarding gaisho,
could you explain to what extent the increase in the number of top-tier customers is
contributing, and what factors are driving this growth in customer numbers? In addition,
could you please provide an update on the progress to date and the outlook for non-gaisho
customers?

A. With regard to gaisho, the number of accounts has been increasing, and we are stepping up our
efforts to acquire new customers. Currently, the customers with the highest annual purchase volume
tier are driving growth in both sales and average sales per customer. Both the number of customers
in this tier and sales have seen double-digit growth. This was driven by various initiatives
implemented during H1, including a focus on events targeting gaisho customers. In addition, a

Q. Regarding the Medium-term Business Plan, you explained that management that
prioritizes resource allocation toward growth opportunities is still underway. How does this
approach need to change? Also, how does this relate to your ability to respond to inflation?

A. In my view, Daimaru Matsuzakaya Department Stores was among the industry leaders in terms
of its ability to cope with deflation. On the other hand, I believe that addressing inflation will take a
considerable amount of time, including a shift in values. One example of this is that we have relatively
few independently operated sales floors compared to others in the industry. Going forward, given
that inflation will make it easier to increase sales but will also drive up costs, we may need to shift
from our current risk-averse operational approach to one that takes more risk. For example, one
option would be to allocate resources to growth areas, such as re-expanding independently
operated sales floors that have been scaled back in the past. Rather than simply repeating what we
have done in the past, we will thoroughly examine what kind of value we can provide for our
customers.

Q. Regarding Daimaru Matsuzakaya Department Stores’ gross sales forecast for H2, what is
the main reason for the upward revision from the initial forecast?

A. For H2, we have primarily raised our forecast for duty-free sales. We expect duty-free sales in
the Department Store Business to increase by 5.8% year-over-year.

Q. Regarding the timing shift in expense recognition, you reported that the amount for the
Department Store segment is approximately 1.3 billion yen. Which quarter, the first or the
second, accounts for the larger portion of this amount?

A. Expenses that were primarily expected to occur in Q2 have been deferred to H2 or later.

Q. In this revised annual plan, the Department Store Business is the only segment for which
the forecast has been revised upward, and forecasts for all other segments remain
unchanged. Was this decision made based on the results of H1, or was it because the
differences were not significant enough to warrant a change to the plan?

A. The reason we revised upward only the Department Store Business and left the other segments
unchanged was that we reassessed them in light of the current momentum in each segment. As a
supplementary note, within the Developer Business, the Interior Design Business performed well in
H1, including in terms of profit margins. However, for H2, the full-year forecast was left unchanged,
taking into account the risk that external construction projects might not proceed as scheduled due
to supply chain disruptions and other factors.
