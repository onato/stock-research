                        Comments from ASX Managing Director and CEO Helen Lofthouse continued:
                        Responding to the ASIC Inquiry

                        “The calling of an Inquiry by ASIC and the subsequent interim report published last December is a critical inflection
                        point for ASX. Implementing the strategic package of actions – what we’re calling our Commitments plan – is a top
                        priority and we are already moving swiftly on several key elements.

                        “First, we are creating a clear, dedicated governance structure for our clearing and settlement facilities. It will promote
                        the independence and responsible investment in each of the clearing and settlement facilities to support financial
                        system stability. These boards will then be supported by dedicated resources, as well as clearly defined shared
                        services support from ASX Group. Our aim is to bring greater independence while maintaining the benefits for our
                        customers of being part of the Group.

                        “Second, we are resetting the Accelerate program. This is a vehicle driving long-term enterprise-wide change so that
                        risk management excellence and business resilience is the standard. The reset is to align with our regulators, to set
                        appropriately aspirational workstream target states, and to adjust scope in response to the Inquiry Panel’s findings,
                        including an additional workstream on governance and independence.

                        “Third, ASX will accumulate an additional $150 million above our current net tangible asset value by 30 June 2027. This
                        capital charge is expected to be funded by a reduced dividend payout ratio and a discounted dividend reinvestment
                        plan which is expected to operate for at least the next three dividend payments.

                        Transforming ASX

                        “When we set out our transformation strategy in 2023, we laid out our plans to make significant investments in ASX to
                        modernise our technology and secure our pathway to long-term sustainable growth through delivery of resilient,
                        evolving services for the market.

                        “The execution of the technology modernisation program has progressed steadily, supported by uplift in underlying
                        frameworks such as project delivery, procurement and quality engineering. We have also driven significant uplift in
                        customer engagement and delivery of product and service enhancements for the market. But this execution has not
                        been consistent for all parts of our strategy. Best practices for risk management and resilience are continually maturing,
                        but at ASX we have not yet achieved the standards of excellence that we aspire to in our role as a steward of critical
                        market infrastructure.

                        “This led us to establish the Accelerate Program, and to carefully examine the culture and capability factors which have
                        impeded progress in the past. We are now working closely with our regulators to ensure that we are aligned with them

                        Outlook and capital management settings
                        Listings activity continued to show momentum in 1H26, including the IPO of BMC Minerals as well as several dual
                        listings such as Channel Infrastructure and Ryman Healthcare. In the seven months ended 31 January, net new capital
                        quoted – which measures new and secondary listings while taking into account delistings – reached $28.7 billion. This
                        included 66 new listings, of which 13 were international listings, reflecting ASX’s strong global proposition. Compared to
                        this time last year we have seen a rise in enquiries from entities considering a listing and this has continued despite the
                        rising interest rate environment.

                        Strong cash market activity continued into the second half with total value for January up by 47% compared to the
                        same month last year. This volume growth is being driven by ongoing volatility from fluctuating geopolitical events as
                        well as expectations surrounding local and global central bank monetary policy.

                        Total futures and options on futures volumes demonstrated robust growth in January, rising by 31% compared to the
                        prior corresponding period. The current environment for rates futures continues to be favourable, with heightened
                        activity observed across the yield curve.

                        On 28 January 2026, ASX updated FY26 total expense growth to be between 20% and 23%, compared with prior
                        expectations of between 14% and 19%. This includes costs associated with responding to the ASIC Inquiry which are
                        expected to be at the top end of our range of between $25 million and $35 million. Excluding ASIC Inquiry related
                        costs, FY26 total expense growth was updated to be between 13% and 15%.

                        Capital expenditure guidance remains unchanged. FY26 capital expenditure is expected to be between $170 million
                        and $180 million, and between $160 million and $180 million in FY27.

                        On 15 December 2025 ASX noted changes to our capital requirements to meet the additional capital charge to which
                        we have committed following the ASIC Inquiry’s interim report. At the time we stated the increased regulatory capital
                        requirement would impact underlying ROE, with ASX’s medium term target range now between 12.5% and 14.0%.

                        The Board has determined a fully franked interim dividend of 101.8 cents per share, payable on 23 March 2026. A
                        discounted dividend reinvestment plan (DRP) will operate for this dividend. Shares allocated will be issued at a 2.5%
                        discount to the share price based on a 9-day volume weighted average price (VWAP) commencing on 25 February.
                        Depending on the participation rate in the DRP, we also have the flexibility to partially underwrite the DRP to achieve
                        our capital targets over time.
