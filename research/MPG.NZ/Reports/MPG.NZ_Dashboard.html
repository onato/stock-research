Metro Performance Glass Limited (MPG.NZ)

Australasia's largest glass processor & glazier for NZ/AU construction · FY ends 31 March · Reports in NZD

Company Overview

Metro Performance Glass (Metroglass) is Australasia's leading processor of flat glass, supplying double-glazed insulated glass units (IGUs) and related processed glass products for the residential and commercial construction and renovation markets. The Group operates two manufacturing plants in New Zealand (Auckland/Highbrook and Christchurch) with a nationwide glazing/branch distribution network, and a wholly-owned Australian subsidiary, Australian Glass Group (AGG), which processes and now also imports IGUs from three sites in Melbourne, Sydney and Hobart. FY26 (year to 31 March 2026) group revenue was $208.2m (NZ $130.4m, AGG A$77.8m), down from $213.9m in FY25, reflecting a prolonged construction-sector downturn in both countries.

Business Model

Metro converts float glass into value-added processed and insulated glass products (toughened, laminated, low-E, double glazing) sold to window/door fabricators, builders and, in New Zealand, direct to trade/retail through its own glazing branch network. Revenue is transactional/project-based (tied to construction activity and building consents) rather than recurring/subscription, so it moves with residential and commercial building cycles with a lag.

Competitive Position

Competitors: Viridian Glass, APL Window Solutions, Evolution Windows NZ, Fairview Windows and Doors, Oceania Glass (AU, closed FY26), regional/independent processors

Moat:

  • Scale manufacturing footprint (moderate) – two NZ plants plus AGG's three AU sites
  • Brand and quality/service reputation (moderate) – 30+ years, DIFOT improved sharply to 93.4% (AKL) / 98.8% (CHC) in FY26
  • Regulatory/product tailwind (moderate) – NZ Building Code and NatHERS mandate higher-performance IGUs
  • Vertical integration into glazing services (weak-to-moderate)

Growth Drivers

  • Structural build-out of double glazing/insulated glass demand from tightening energy codes (NZ, AU NatHERS)
  • Australia's 1.2 million new homes (5-year federal target) supporting medium-term AGG volume recovery
  • Growing mix of higher-margin Low-E, laminated safety glass, digital printing, frameless systems, retrofit double glazing
  • FY26 cost-out program ($4m NZ opex reduction) delivering full-year P&L benefit in FY27
  • Balance-sheet reset (Sept-2025 raise, new 3-year facility to Sept-2028) removes going-concern doubt
  • Potential further NZ market consolidation given structural oversupply (Viridian's failed bid shows ongoing interest)

Key Risks

  • [HIGH] Prolonged construction downturn – FY26 revenue -2.7%, North Island residential weak, no substantive FY27 market growth assumed by management
  • [HIGH] Customer/price concentration and structural NZ market oversupply (per Commerce Commission findings)
  • [HIGH] NZ/AU residential construction cyclicality – consent momentum slowing mid-2026, architects forecast pipeline contraction
  • [MODERATE] Supply chain disruption in Australia – Oceania Glass closure forced full import model, ~A$1m incremental cost
  • [MODERATE] Execution risk – new CEO/CFO and multiple country-manager changes during a fragile recovery
  • [MODERATE] Residual leverage/covenant headroom unproven through a full cycle post-recapitalisation
  • [MODERATE] Heavy lease liabilities ($69.5m IFRS16) exceed bank debt ($35.2m) – reported net debt understates total fixed obligations
  • [LOW-MOD] Dilution risk from further capital raises given prior recapitalisation history

Bull Case

  • Balance sheet fundamentally reset – net debt cut $60.5m→$27.0m, going-concern doubt removed, 3-year facility to Sept-2028
  • FY26 cost-out program ($4m) largely actioned but full-year benefit only lands in FY27 – built-in operating leverage without needing a market recovery
  • Failed Viridian takeover + industry oversupply keep consolidation optionality alive; 51% cornerstone shareholder (Amari/MGI) signals committed capital
  • Structural regulatory tailwind toward double glazing/IGUs in both NZ and AU should lift product mix and margins over time
  • DIFOT/quality KPIs improved sharply in FY26, evidencing a genuine service-led turnaround

Bear Case

  • Still statutorily loss-making (FY26 net loss $0.9m) in a downturn management itself says shows no substantive recovery
  • NZ market structurally oversupplied (Commerce Commission finding) – limits pricing power even as costs inflate
  • Thinly-traded NZX micro-cap, 51% held by one strategic investor – limited free float, concentrated control
  • Track record of value destruction – multiple recapitalisations (2019, Sept-2025 dilutive raise) and AU operational missteps
  • No specific FY27 numeric guidance given – management cites "enough uncertainty" on whether cost program offsets a weak top line

Recent Developments

  • 2026-05-27: FY26 audited results: revenue $208.2m (-2.7%), EBITDA before significant items $18.2m (up from $16.9m), net loss $0.9m (narrowed from $13.5m), net debt cut to $27.0m from $60.5m, going-concern uncertainty removed
  • 2025-12-30: Amari Metals Australia transferred its 51% stake to affiliate Metro Glass Investment (MGI) Pty Ltd – control unchanged
  • 2025-10-17: Viridian NZ BidCo formally withdrew its Commerce Commission clearance application to acquire MPG, ending the takeover attempt
  • 2025-09-19: Recapitalisation completed – $23.9m equity raise (rights offer + Amari placement), new 3-year banking facility to Sept-2028
  • 2025-09: Simon Bennett appointed Managing Director; new Chair and Amari-affiliated director joined the Board
  • 2025 (FY26): Oceania Glass (major AU supplier) closed, forcing AGG to a full import model (~A$1m incremental cost)
  • Sep–Nov 2025: H1 FY26 swung to a $2.9m profit from a $5.0m loss in H1 FY25, aided by an $8.6m debt-extinguishment gain

Reported FCF = OCF − CapEx. The DCF model further deducts IFRS-16 lease principal (~$8.0m in FY26) and interest income to derive owner FCF, which is materially lower than the reported figure shown here.

Net Debt & Cash

Bank debt fell $67.0m → $35.2m over FY25→FY26; net debt (bank debt less cash) is ~$27.0m post the Sept-2025 raise. IFRS-16 lease liabilities (~$69.5m) are tracked separately and excluded here — see help for why.

Balance Sheet Structure

Shareholders' equity troughed at $35.6m (FY2025) then recovered to $60.0m (FY2026) on the $23.9m Sept-2025 equity raise — the jump is capital issuance, not organic earnings.

Stock-Based Compensation

SBC is immaterial and net-negative in both FY25 (-$0.165m) and FY26 (-$0.018m) — treated as an SBC-zero market in the DCF; no dilution is modelled from equity incentives.
