                        Review of Continuing Operations

                        The consolidated loss after tax of the group for the half year amounted to $96.2 million (31
                        March 2022: $2.01 million NPAT).

                        The result reflects the agricultural risk exposure of almond farming, with poor crop years in
                        2022 and 2023.


                        2023 Crop

                        Following three years of a La Nina weather pattern, including record rainfall in 2022 and
                        cooler conditions, the Australian 2023 almond crop was materially impacted, resulting in
                        unusual growing patterns and lower yields. In line with the industry, Select’s 2023 crop is
                        forecast to be below initial expectations. Lower volumes across the weighbridge in addition to
                        lower crack-out rates, have reduced the 2023 crop volume forecast to 17,500MT (2021:
                        28,250MT, 2022: 29,000MT).

                        The 2023 crop harvest has now been completed. Despite some scattered showers during the
                        harvest process there have been stretches of favourable conditions allowing all available
                        product to be harvested.

                        Despite the unfavourable growing conditions, the quality of the crop has improved compared
                        to last year. Inshell levels are lower, however kernel sizes are larger. Insect damage is higher
                        (a function of the lower crop size) however recent investments in state-of-the-art sorters will
                        lead to the final product quality profile improving from last year. Due to a drier 2023 harvest
                        period the prevalence of mould has decreased from 2022 crop levels.

                        Consistent with the requirements of Accounting Standard AASB 141 Agriculture, the
                        harvested portion of the 2023 crop has been valued at the estimated fair value less costs to
                        sell. Due to the 2023 crop volume forecast decreasing to 17,500MT the Net Realisable Value
                        of the crop is less than the total costs to sell the crop. As a result, the full loss of the 2023
                        crop has been reflected in the 1H FY2023 accounts. The recognition of the forecast full year
                        loss has a material impact on the 1H FY2023 financial result. Assuming the assumptions



                                                                      -1-

                        used to estimate fair value at 31 March 2023 remain the same at 30 September 2023, the 2H
                        FY2023 result will not reflect any impact of the 2023 crop result and therefore, is forecast to
                        show a materially lower loss result with the recognition of six months of corporate and finance
                        costs partially offset by non-crop related income.

                        Total growing costs were in line with expectations. Fertiliser costs were higher than for the
                        previous crop (due to price rises as a result of world supply shortages as previously
                        communicated) and additional costs were incurred as a result of the flooding events. These
                        costs were offset by lower water costs and harvest freight (a function of the lower crop). There
                        were additional costs recognised for immature orchards as their maturity profile increases.

                        The Company’s processing facility is in full production with approximately 67% of the crop
                        processed. The quality mix continues to be reviewed to ensure highest grade final product
                        options are targeted. The 2023 crop quality is much better suited for the hi-tech sorters to
                        deliver positive upgrade results. Processing costs are likely to be lower due to the decreased
                        2023 crop volume. However, given the majority of costs are fixed in nature this benefit is not

                        in line with the size of the reduced crop.

                        Freight and logistics operations have returned to pre-Covid levels. Freight costs have reduced
                        from last year as a result of increased capacity in the market.

                        The Company’s value adding facility continues to deliver positive outcomes. It is processing
                        low grade material and producing higher valued product (e.g. paste and sliced material).
                        Additional upgrades have been completed to ensure the production of high quality material at
                        optimal throughput and yield recovery rates.


                        2022 Crop

                        During the 1H FY2023 period the Company completed a detailed review of the remaining
                        2022 stock on hand. This inventory was impacted by the wet finish to the 2022 harvest and
                        had to be mechanically dried. Following this review, a significant portion of the remaining
                        inventory was downgraded due to quality issues, primarily related to mould (from exposure to
                        moisture) that led to further stock deterioration in 1H FY2023. This remaining inventory has
                        either been sold in the market at reduced pricing levels or been allocated to be used through
                        the Company’s value adding facility. This downgrade has had a $24M before tax impact on
                        the 1H FY2023 result.

                        The Company has been actively selling its 2022 crop to ensure that the cash velocity of the
                        remaining stock is captured.


                        Cash flows

                        On the 31st of March 2023 the Company signed a three-year new facility agreement with its
                        banking partners, NAB and Rabobank. This incorporated a $30M increase in facility limits, a
                        further $20M increase that expires in June 2024 and a waiver on the Company’s Fixed
                        Charge Cover Ratio covenant measure to March 2024. Despite the lower 1H FY2023
                        earnings, all required banking covenants have been met with headroom remaining. Debt
                        levels remain well managed and current facilities remain adequate for operations moving
                        forward. As a post balance sheet event the Company has also secured, for two years, its
                        $20m seasonal facility as well as a reduction in the Fixed Charge Cover covenant testing
                        requirement at March 2024 from 3.0x to 2.0x. These give additional committed support to our
                        forward cash position.

                        Operational cashflows were lower than 1H FY2022. Lower almond pricing and the
                        downgraded quality of the 2022 crop reduced cash inflows while payments for growing and
                        harvesting costs for the 2023 crop continued at forecast levels. Despite the forecast lower

                        volume, 2023 crop operational cashflows are forecast to be positive in the second half from
                        sales of inshell and export kernel. Investing cash outflows were lower than 1H FY2022 as the
                        Company consciously lowered its level of capital expenditure while cashflows were forecast to
                        be lower due to crop and market factors. Additionally, tree development costs decreased as
                        the more young trees reached their full maturity profile.


                        Safety and ESG

                        The Company has renewed its focus on safety which will bring both health benefits and
                        operational efficiency gains. In March 2023, Select Harvests redefined its definition of Total
                        Recordable Incident Frequency Rate (TRIFR) to align with external benchmarking definitions.
                        Select’s TRIFR measure is based on Lost Time Injuries (LTIs) and Medical Treatment Injuries
                        (MTIs) only. This has resulted in a new Annual Moving Total (MAT) TRIFR target of 16.3 for
                        FY2023. TRIFR MAT has significantly improved, reducing from 40.9 at the end of FY2022 to
                        21.2 at 31 March 2023.

                        Select’s Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate (LTIFR) MAT is 9.2 and achieves the half-year
                        result target LTIFR MAT of 10.8.

                        Select’s Medical Treatment Injury Frequency Rate (MTIFR) MAT reduced to 6.5 at 31 March
                        2023. Pleasingly the results are below Select’s annual MTIFR MAT of 9.9. Across our key
                        measures Select has bettered its safety results for the first half of 2023 reflecting the ongoing
                        efforts to achieve zero harm and improve wellbeing.

                        The Company continues to maintain its focus on Sustainability, Environment, and
                        Governance (ESG) through the Board’s Sustainability Committee. The Company released its
                        Sustainability Report in February 2023 that clearly outlined focus areas and targets. The
                        Company has had zero environmental incidents during the current reporting period.


                        Balance Sheet

                        The Company’s balance sheet remains in a sound position. There is however, as result of the
                        Company’s reported loss, a $99.5M reduction in equity compared to the same period last
                        year. This comprises the 2022 crop revaluation, reduced 2023 crop and intangibles write
                        down.

                        Inventory and biological asset balances are lower than the same period last year reflecting
                        the lower crop size. Property, plant and equipment balances have reduced with lower
                        spending ($16M of additions) on capital more than offset by annual depreciation.

                        Intangible assets have decreased by $26M reflecting the Company’s decision to record a
                        non-cash impairment of the Company’s Goodwill asset.

                        The Company maintains its policy of recording land and water assets at their cost of
                        acquisition. As at 31 March 2023, the market value of the Company’s land assets are $125M
                        higher and its water assets are $70M higher their respective recorded book values. These
                        assets continue to grow in value over time and provide the Company with additional asset
                        backing security. The Company will complete a comprehensive valuation process again in
                        September 2023 for its land and water assets and this will be detailed in the FY2023 financial
                        statements.

                        Debt levels as at 31 March 2023 are, as per normal, approaching their seasonal peak. With
                        almond prices at lower levels during 2H FY2022 and 1H FY2023 and higher 2023 crop
                        growing costs, debt balances peaked for the FY2023 year in May 2023 and are forecast to
                        decrease during the second half of FY2023.

                        Business Outlook

                        As US growing conditions deteriorated during the bloom period, market pricing started to
                        increase steadily in March 2023 and continued this pattern to early May 2023. Adding to this
                        was the key export market of China coming out of lockdown restrictions. As the market
                        became more active and pricing improved the Company has contracted 60% of the 2023 crop
                        for sale. Based on contracts in place and current market pricing the Company has valued its
                        2023 crop at an average price of $7.45/kg.

                        Over the past 12 months the AUD has remained relatively volatile against the USD with peaks
                        and dips occurring regularly. Select’s foreign exchange hedging policy ensures the exportable
                        crop value is hedged as total sales value certainty increases. Due to the reduction in the 2023
                        crop forecast the full year sales are fully hedged against the USD at a rate of $0.6788. There
                        is a point in time mark to market adjustment recorded in Other Comprehensive Income. This
                        position unwinds as the crop is sold and hedge contracts mature and is incorporated in the
                        2023 crop’s fair value calculation.




For personal use only
                        Global almond pricing has recently increased in line with world demand and an expectation
                        that the 2023 US crop will be materially lower than average.

                        The North American almond crop this year was struck by poor climate conditions that will
                        impact the 2024 crop.

                        There have been a series of industry based US crop estimates with forecasts ranging
                        between 2.2b and 2.4b pounds. Following this, on the 12th of May 2023, the US Department
                        of Agriculture released it forecast subjective estimate for the 2023 Californian Almond Crop of
                        2.5B pounds, 2.5% lower than the 2022 crop and 13% lower than the average of the last
                        three years crop size. The US 2023 crop objective estimate is due on July 7, 2023.

                        The other key determinant of US volumes is inventory carried forward from one season to the
                        next. Three of the past four months have delivered record shipments out of the US and this
                        has reduced the carry out inventory to manageable levels.

                        March 2023 shipments from the Australian Almond Board position report (60% higher than
                        March 2022) were strong and continued the trend of improved market activity and increased
                        stock movements.

                        Key export markets are very active and with China out of lockdown restrictions export
                        demand has returned to pre-Covid levels. Additionally, the important Indian market has been
                        sourcing a variety of almond product. The Company has grown its customer base in the key
                        markets of the Middle East and Southeast Asia allowing for an improved sales profile and
                        customer diversification.

                        We are currently forecasting a range in the 2023 crop sell price of $7.40 to $7.80 and there
                        may be upside to this position.


                        2024 Crop Outlook

                        Tree health remains positive and early indications are that the 2024 crop is on track to return
                        to normal yields. Following three years of the wet La Nina weather patterns, the Bureau of
                        Meteorology has forecast that Australia is likely to move into an El Nino pattern which is
                        typically favourable for growing almonds. The 2024 growing program has commenced and
                        the Company is executing its horticultural plan.

                        Given the wetter than average conditions over the past two growing seasons, water costs for
                        the 2024 crop are expected to remain at lower-than-average levels. Water allocations remain
                        favourable, dam storage levels are high and temporary market water pricing is expected to

                        remain at below average levels, although higher than 2023, on the back of both rising cost,
                        increased water use in a drier year and increased energy costs for pumping water on farms.
                        The exposure to water price variability continues to be managed through the Company’s
                        diversified water policy of owning, leasing and acquiring water on the annual allocation
                        market.

                        Fertiliser pricing has reduced as expected.

                        Market pricing is forecast to further improve as US carryover supply continues to decrease
                        and the US 2023 crop is likely to deliver a below average outcome. Global demand has
                        returned to pre-Covid levels which will likely add to a favourable pricing environment.


                        Risk Management

                        The Company has a Risk Management Policy with a framework and process to identify,




For personal use only
                        assess, manage, and monitor risks throughout all parts of the business. The governance of
                        risk is overseen by the Audit and Risk Committee. The Executive Management Team are
                        responsible for ensuring compliance with the Company’s Risk Management Policy, led by the
                        Chief Financial Officer. Managers are responsible for contributing to all aspects of risk
                        management across the business.

                        The Company maintains and refreshes its risk register annually. The register provides a
                        framework and benchmark against which risks are reported on at different levels in the
                        business, with a biannual report presented to the Board. Each month major risks are reviewed
                        by Senior Management and the Board. They include:
                            • Safety Risks (including employee safety, fire prevention and plant operation). The
                               majority of the Company’s employees work within farm or processing related
                               environments. Policies and procedures are designed and are in place in order to
                               minimise the risks of injuries occurring.
                               The Carina West Processing Facility is a major operating plant that handles the end-
                               to-end process for all of the Company’s almond and bio-mass inventory. Key
                               operating procedures, ongoing capital maintenance, engineering reviews and
                               proactive maintenance are mitigating actions in place to minimise the risk of a safety
                               event (e.g. fire);
                            • Horticultural Risks (including climatic, disease, water management, pollination and
                               quality). The Company’s annual almond crop is subject to numerous agricultural risks.
                               The annual horticultural plan is adjusted each year to allow for factors that may
                               change from year to year (e.g. pest management).
                               Water access remains a key risk to the Company. A policy of owning a third, leasing
                               a third and buying a third of the Company’s water requirements is in place. This
                               reduces the exposure the Company has to water deliverability and cost variability;
                            • Food Safety Risks (including product quality, utilities supply and major equipment
                               failure). The Company’s almond products end up with the end consumer through
                               various channels. Quality testing procedures are in place at each of the Company’s
                               processing stages. Additionally, the Company’s facilities are subject to numerous
                               independent and customer audits to ensure required standards are met;
                            • Financial Risks (including currency, customer concentration, market pricing). The
                               majority of the Company’s sales are transacted in USD. There is a treasury policy in
                               place that ensures the Company’s foreign exchange exposure is managed in
                               accordance with the crop growing and sales cycle.
                               A key sensitivity to the Company’s earnings is exposure to the almond price. The
                               Company aims to mitigate this risk by maintaining contact with key industry bodies in

                                the US and major traders and customer in key export markets. Crop sales plans are
                                developed each year taking into account pricing factors that impact industry supply
                                and demand; and
                            •   Cybersecurity Risks. The Company has numerous information technology (IT)
                                systems across its operations that are key to operating critical infrastructure. IT
                                security is currently in place across the Company’s systems. External expertise is

                        Strategic Initiatives

                        The Company is part way through reviewing its strategy going forward. The working
                        hypothesis for this work has the Company operating over three horizons.

                        In the short horizon the Company focus is on:
                             - Continuing to Process and Market the 2023 crop to achieve an optimal net sell price.
                             - Delivering our 2024 crop horticultural program to ensure a maximum rebound in 2024
                                 production levels
                             - Identifying a series of foundation initiatives to get the Company fit
                             - Increasing our focus on product quality.
                             - Driving for cash velocity

                        The medium horizon is more focused on:
                           - Increasing almond supply
                           - Expanding our processing capacity and efficiency such that the operational gains flow
                              to the bottom line.
                           - Identifying capital expenditure and growth opportunities that deliver favourable
                              paybacks and improve the Company’s financial returns

                        The long horizon remains in development and will focus on more substantive transformation
                        projects. We will provide further detail on these projects at the appropriate time.

                        The Company has established a Project Management Office to drive the execution of these
                        projects. Additional projects will evolve and some will be more successful than others. As at
                        the time of writing (and post half-year balance sheet date) the company has a suite of 28
                        projects of which 18 have been valued. Completed actions to date have generated forecast
                        profit upside and cash flow improvements. It is worth noting some of these gains will be
                        consumed by Australia’s general inflationary environment. We will report further with updates
                        as the program rolls out.

                        Despite the impacts of the past couple of years the Company remains well placed to ensure it
                        can operate through the current cycle and become more robust. Tree health is positive and
                        forecast weather patterns are favourable with the likelihood of moving into a more stable and
                        drier El Nino pattern. The global almond market is active and has returned to pre Covid levels.
                        Previously high supply inventory balances are decreasing to manageable levels and the US
                        2023 crop appears challenged. These factors are forecast to put increased upside pressure
                        on the pricing environment.

                        Select Harvests is developing a detailed plan, including profit and cash improvement
                        initiatives, that have started to generate positive returns. This will ensure the company

                        A key assumption is the crop volume. The forecast takes into account the significant reduction in

                        growing season. The cash flow includes the assumption of 17,500 tonnes for the 2023 crop, to be
                        sold over the next 9-12 months.

                        the group is forecasting net pricing for the 2023 crop to be in the range from A$7.45. The almond
                        price is based on the assessment of the quality of the 2023 crop. The Group has assessed the
                        quality of the crop based on the 67% of almonds processed to date. A change in the quality profile
                        would impact on almond pricing and future cash flows.

                        Operating costs
                        The forecast includes the impact of inflationary increases on input costs, offset by cost savings
                        related to the lower tonnage for the 2023 crop and reduction in the 2024 crop key input costs such
                        as fertiliser. It is anticipated that other costs such, as water, will remain at similar levels to the prior
                        year.

                        Sensitivity of cash flows
                        The group has considered the downside scenarios of changes to almond pricing and volumes.
                        Based on downside scenarios, the Group is satisfied that reasonable changes to key assumptions
