Business model

Salt Carbon Fund is NOT an operating company. It is an NZX-listed managed investment scheme (unit trust, part of the Salt Listed Funds scheme) whose sole substantive asset is New Zealand Units (NZUs) — the compliance carbon credits of the NZ Emissions Trading Scheme (NZ ETS). Launched 7 November 2018 at $1.0000/unit as New Zealand's first NZX-listed carbon fund, it is managed by Salt Investment Funds Limited (part of Salt Funds Management, acquired by Alvarium (NZ) Wealth Management Holdings, now SPH Wealth Holdings, on 1 September 2025) and supervised by The New Zealand Guardian Trust Company. The fund's entire investment case is a single-factor bet on the traded price of NZUs; it has no products, customers, competitors in a commercial sense, revenue growth story, or operating margin to analyze.

The fund raises capital by issuing units on the NZX, uses the proceeds to buy and hold NZUs (and, since the June 2025 mandate widening, potentially Treasury Units and 'Carbon-Related Businesses'), and its Net Tangible Assets (NTA) per unit moves essentially one-for-one with the NZU spot price. There is no operating revenue: NZUs pay no coupon, dividend or yield. The fund's only 'income' is negligible interest on any uninvested cash. Historically the fund did not distribute income to unitholders ('We do not currently intend to make distributions'); the June 2025 PDS update softened this to 'We may make distributions,' but no distribution policy has yet been implemented. Value is realised only by selling units on the NZX or by the manager buying back units (see Treasury Units below), not by any business cash flow.

Bull case

  • NAV has already recovered sharply from the FY2026 (31 March 2026) low of $1.3786 to a published NTA near $1.72 by early August 2026 as NZU spot prices rallied on tightening private-account supply — the current NZX market quote (~$1.546) appears to lag this recovery, so a buyer today may be acquiring exposure at roughly a 10% discount to the fund's own stated NAV
  • Persistent below-NAV treasury unit buybacks (over half of all units now held in treasury, with continued purchases through July 2026) are mechanically NAV-per-unit accretive to remaining holders
  • Structural long-run ETS design (a falling unit cap and a rising Auction Reserve Price floor through 2030) provides a policy-engineered scarcity tailwind for NZU prices if the government maintains scheme credibility
  • The fund is the only NZX-listed, easily tradeable vehicle for NZU exposure, giving investors listed/brokerage-account access to an asset class otherwise requiring a dedicated EPA Holding Account
  • NZU prices are currently well below their 2022 peak (~$88) and below the government's own 2026 auction floor (~$71), which some investors may view as offering asymmetric upside if ETS settings are perceived as credible again

Bear case

  • NZU price — and therefore fund NAV — is fundamentally a government policy variable, not a market fundamental; the November 2025 NDC-delinking announcement alone erased ~20% of the NZU price in a single session, illustrating how a single policy decision can destroy a large fraction of unitholder value overnight with no diversification to cushion it
  • Extreme illiquidity (~4,000 units/day, ~NZ$6,000 notional traded) means the quoted NZX price can and does diverge materially from published daily NTA in either direction, creating real execution risk and making the fund unsuitable for any meaningful position size without accepting a wide effective spread
  • The fund pays no income and has essentially never distributed cash; total return depends entirely on achieving a favourable buy/sell price on a thinly-traded exchange, with no yield to cushion drawdowns
  • The 1.0% p.a. management fee is a certain, compounding drag against a zero-yield asset, permanently eroding NAV relative to the raw NZU price every year regardless of market conditions
  • The fund is shrinking rapidly via treasury buybacks (units on issue down from 47.2 million to ~20.9 million between 31 March 2026 and 30 July 2026, more than a 55% reduction) — if this continues, the fund risks becoming sub-scale, uneconomic to administer, or a candidate for wind-up/delisting, which is a structural overhang distinct from the NZU price itself
  • Repeated NZ government auction failures (all four 2023 auctions, and much of 2025-2026) show that policy-set floor prices are not always supported by secondary market demand, undermining the case that ETS design mechanically puts a floor under NZU (and hence NAV) prices

Valuation philosophy

Salt Carbon Fund is an NZX-listed unit trust whose only asset is New Zealand Units (NZUs), the compliance credits of the NZ Emissions Trading Scheme. It has no revenue, no margins, no operating cash flow and no earnings in any conventional sense — NZUs are a non-yielding commodity. A discounted-cash-flow model is therefore structurally inapplicable: there are no cash flows to discount. The fund is valued the only way such a vehicle can be: project the NZU spot price, carry NAV per unit forward at that price less the 1.0% p.a. management-fee drag, apply an exit discount to NTA reflecting the listed vehicle's persistent illiquidity, and discount back at a cost of equity.

Discount rate

8.5% = ~4.5% NZ risk-free + ~4.0% vehicle-specific premium for extreme illiquidity (~4,000 units/day, ~NZ$6k notional), fee drag, manager-change and wind-up/sub-scale risk. The NZU PRICE risk is deliberately NOT loaded into the discount rate — it is expressed explicitly in the bull/base/bear NZU price paths. Loading a commodity's price risk into the discount rate as well as the scenario spread double-counts it.

Key caveat

NAV per unit tracks the NZU price roughly one-for-one, and the NZU price is set by government policy (unit supply settings, auction reserve prices, forestry and agriculture rules), not by market fundamentals. The November 2025 NDC-delinking decision cut the NZU price ~20% intraday. This valuation is a policy forecast wearing the clothes of a financial model, and should be read as such.

Sanity check

Conventional P/E, P/B and EV/EBITDA multiples are undefined for this entity — it has no earnings, no EBITDA and no revenue. The meaningful sanity anchor for a listed fund is price relative to NTA. Current price/NTA 0.901x; at the weighted IV, 0.660x. The fund's own repurchases through 2026 have been executed at 0.88-0.90x NTA, and the market quote has sat at a persistent discount to published daily NTA. A weighted IV below current NTA is therefore consistent with how this vehicle has actually traded, not an anomaly.

The base case is NEGATIVE versus the current market price. That is the honest output: at $1.546 the market is paying ~90% of an NTA that is itself a leveraged bet on a policy-determined carbon price, and the 1% fee plus a persistent listed discount consume much of the modelled NZU appreciation.
