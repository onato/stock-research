For the Sustainable Future




   Pursue quality in circularity
   The word "recycling" has been in use for decades in Japan, and the era of pursuing only partial circularity has come to an end.

   From now on, it is essential to protect the environment, effectively utilize limited resources, and simultaneously

   develop the economy.

   This is why DOWA aims for true circularity.

   We strive to return as many materials as possible to the cycle from waste and recyclable resources generated worldwide.

   For materials that cannot be returned to the cycle, we ensure they are handled properly.

   By diversifying product functions and enhancing durability, we aim to extend the use of limited resources.

   As the world and Japan now aim to transition to the circular economy,

   we will bring together the technologies we have accumulated over 140 years and continue to pursue higher quality in circularity.




    © 2025 DOWA HOLDINGS CO., LTD.                                                                                                    2




Under the theme “Pursue quality in circularity,” we reiterate the direction DOWA Group is
aiming for.
We strive to return as many materials as possible to the cycle from waste and recyclable
resources. For materials that cannot be returned to circulation, we ensure they are
handled safely and appropriately. By diversifying product functions or enhancing
durability, we contribute to a more sustainable society. We aim to leverage these features
and strengths to take the next step forward.

 DOWA’s Strength


Collect, Separate, Manufacture
DOWA's businesses leading the circularity.
The DOWA Group has established a unique recycling-oriented business model by combining five businesses.
We possess a global network to collect waste and recyclable resources and recycle a wide variety of metals, while also proper ly treating
non-recyclable waste to reduce environmental impact.
In addition, we produce materials that contribute to groundbreaking technological innovations and provide technologies and se rvices that
extend the lifespan of products.
By collaborating with external partners through our circular business model, we create new value, pursue higher quality in circularity, and
achieve true circularity.

  The DOWA Group’s greatest strength lies in having both the “arterial” businesses (from
  “manufacture” to “consume”) and the “venous” businesses (from “dispose” to “collect”
  and “separate”) coexisting within the group. This unique feature is a significant advantage
  that sets us apart from other companies.

   The Japanese government is positioning the transition from a linear economy to the
   circular economy as a national strategy. This transition aims to address six major societal
   challenges, as shown on the right-hand side.
   Particularly, strengthening the resource circulation and waste management infrastructure
   is a key national strategy. Policies such as the 5th Fundamental Plan for Establishing a
   Sound Material-Cycle Society and the Act Concerning Sophistication of Recycling
   Business, etc. to Promote Resource Circulation are being advanced.
   For our group, which integrates both arterial and venous functions, this represents a
   major opportunity to strengthen our business foundation and competitiveness further.

  We believe “Circularity has entered an era in which quality matters.” Until now, the focus
  has been primarily on the simple 3Rs (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle). Going forward, we
  anticipate a shift toward an era of qualitative innovation in resource circulation. For
  example, in “Integrated circularity,” we see significant opportunities in returning materials,
  including those previously discarded, to the cycle. In “Long-term circularity,” we aim to
  contribute to society by offering new products and services that are more durable and
  prevent functional degradation.
  Among DOWA Group’s five businesses, the Environment & Recycling and Smelting
  segments are positioned to handle “Integrated circularity,” while the Electronic Materials,
  Metal Processing, and Heat Treatment segments are positioned to handle “Long-term
  circularity.”

 First, regarding changes in the management environment, the darker green areas in the
 diagram represent the conditions assumed when formulating the Medium-term Plan
 2024, while the white areas indicate changes that emerged during the three years of the
 plan.
 Unfortunately, most of these changes had a negative impact on our profitability,
 although there were some positive changes.
 For example, the tightening of export regulations on various metals in China positively
 impacted our business because we do not import raw materials from China. The resulting
 surge in rare and minor metal prices benefited us.
 On the other hand, we faced negative factors such as declining PGM prices, rising
 electricity costs, and temporary drops in automobile production volumes due to improper
 vehicle certifications. Experiencing these many changes was unprecedented, and we felt
 the management environment was becoming increasingly unpredictable over the past
 three years.
