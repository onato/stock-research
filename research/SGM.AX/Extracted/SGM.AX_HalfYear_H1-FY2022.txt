                        PRINCIPAL ACTIVITIES

                        The principal activities of the Group during the financial year comprised (1) the buying, processing, and selling of ferrous
                        and non-ferrous recycled metals, and (2) supporting businesses and data centres in managing end of life physical IT assets
                        through reuse, redeployment, and recycling. This includes IT asset disposition (ITAD) and e-waste recycling solutions.
                        The Group’s principal activities remained unchanged from the previous financial year.

                        Sensitivity to movements in foreign exchange rates

                        The principal currencies in which the Group’s subsidiaries conduct business are United States (“US”) dollars, Australian
                        dollars (“A$”), and British pounds sterling. Although the Group’s reporting currency is the Australian dollar, a significant
                        portion of the Group’s sales and purchases are in currencies other than the Australian dollar. In addition, significant
                        portions of the Group’s net assets are denominated in currencies other than the Australian dollar.

                        The Group’s consolidated financial position, results of operations, and cash flows may be materially affected by
                        movements in the exchange rate between the Australian dollar and the respective local currencies to which its subsidiaries
                        are exposed.

                        Some of the results discussed below are presented on a “constant currency” basis, which means that the current period
                        results are translated into Australian dollars using applicable exchange rates in the prior year corresponding period. This
                        allows for a relative performance comparison between the two periods before the translation impact of currency
                        fluctuations.

                        higher sales volumes during HY22.




For personal use only
                        Trading margin increased by 45.0% through disciplined management of the buy / sell spread as prices increased.
                        Operating costs increased due to higher activity across the business, new business acquisitions and greenfield facilities,
                        increased Group performance incentive provisioning, and inflationary pressures.

                        Statutory EBIT in HY22 was A$341.4 million compared to A$78.5 million in HY21. At constant currency, statutory
                        EBIT during HY22 was A$348.0 million. HY22 Underlying EBIT of A$361.7 million was A$305.3 million higher than
                        HY21. The group experienced strong earnings growth across all operating segments. Higher sales volumes and higher
                        material prices, combined with effective margin management, resulted in improved trading margins. In addition, the share
                        of results from the Group’s investment in the SA Recycling business increased from A$24.4 million in HY21 to A$128.7
                        million in HY22, a A$104.3 million improvement.

                        Statutory NPAT in HY22 was A$253.2 million compared to A$53.0 million in HY21. Underlying NPAT was A$269.3
                        million in HY22 compared to A$37.3 million in HY21. Underlying income tax expense of A$83.3 million during HY22
                        was A$69.6 million higher than HY21 due primarily to higher profit before tax and resulted in an underlying effective tax

                        borrowings through the period to fund working capital needs. Statutory EBITDA in HY22 was A$442.0 million compared
                        to A$177.1 million in HY21. See the Reconciliation of Statutory Results to Underlying Results included herein for more

                        term incentive plans (“LTIP”). The Company also leveraged its employee share ownership programme trust to reduce the
                        dilution effect of certain shares issued under the LTIP.

                        External Operating Environment

                        The recycled ferrous market continued to recover throughout HY22, with strong steel demand in domestic and export
                        markets. During HY22, the average Turkey heavy melting steel (“HMS”) price was US$471 per tonne1, an increase from




For personal use only
                        HY21 average on US$312 per tonne1. Zorba prices remained consistently above US$1,500 per tonne1 during the half year
                        compared to an average of US$1,0791 per tonne over the prior corresponding period. Non-ferrous metal traded at
                        historically high prices with the average copper price at US$9,8485 in HY22 or US$2,644 above the prior corresponding
                        period This upward price movement in ferrous and non-ferrous metal resulted from increased demand from global
                        manufacturing and infrastructure spending. Additionally, the urgency for global economies and corporates to transition to
                        a low-carbon economy continued to support demand for recycled ferrous and non-ferrous metals.

                        COVID lockdowns, labour shortages, increased commodity prices, strains on logistics networks, and scarcity of supply in
                        many parts and consumables led to significant macro-economic inflationary pressures across the globe.

                        Despite these issues, steel industry profitability was strong, particularly in the United States where strong consumer
                        demand and government stimulus (actual and prospective) underpinned a rebound in activity. Proprietary intake volumes
                        in North America were up on both the prior corresponding period and the FY19 monthly average, as the US economy
                        continued its recovery trajectory. Gross Domestic Product (GDP) increased at an annual rate of 6.9%2 in the fourth quarter
                        of 2021, following an increase of 2.3% in the third quarter. Despite the evident recovery in the US economic activity, the
                        resurgence of COVID cases resulted in continued restrictions and disruptions in some parts of the country.

                        The pace of scrap market consolidation in the US accelerated in the half, driven by the increased demand for scrap from
                        steel mills. Sims participated in the market consolidation with NAM acquiring Atlantic Recycling Group located in
                        Baltimore, Maryland (transaction completed in January 2022) and its Joint Venture, SA Recycling, acquiring four metal
                        recycling companies, including PSC Metals (transaction completed in December 2021) for US$290.0 million.

                        In Australia & New Zealand, proprietary intake volumes were near to pre-COVID levels. This was an excellent result
                        given that the Australian economy contracted 1.9%3 in the September quarter 2021, reflecting reduced activity due to
                        extended lockdowns across NSW, Victoria, and ACT. This fall followed four consecutive rises since the 6.8% fall
                        recorded in June quarter 2020, when the entire country was in lockdown. GDP in September quarter 2021 was 0.2%
                        below December 2019 pre-pandemic levels.

                        In the UK, proprietary intake volumes were substantially in line with the prior comparable period despite yard closures
                        and COVID impacts. UK GDP is estimated to have increased by 1.1%4 in September quarter 2021. However, this level of
                        GDP was still below the pre-pandemic December quarter.

                        Sims Lifecycle Services (“SLS”) grew repurposed units and market share in HY22, as customers continued to focus on
                        the circular economy and ESG outcomes. This was a strong result against a backdrop of challenging market conditions
                        posed by supply chain constraints which coupled with the surge in demand for cloud material limited the release of cloud
                        material.

                        Momentum has, to date, continued into 2H FY22 in the metal business and our Joint Venture, SA Recycling. Intake

                        Market Drivers

                        The ongoing or announced stimulus spending is increasing demand for steel-intensive infrastructure spending and driving
                        retail consumption. Post consumption scrap is also increasing, and that is positive for both ferrous and non-ferrous metal
                        recycling.

                        The global decarbonisation of steel making, and electricity generation industries is driving demand for recycled metal.

                        The fundamental drivers of the cloud infrastructure recycling remain positive over the medium term. It is expected that
                        cloud material shortage will ease in early 2023 and cloud repurposing will return to rapid growth.

                        It is worth noting that there are risks to the materialisation of these positive drivers, particularly as it relates to global
                        uncertainty from geopolitical risks, macro-economic factors, and the unpredictability of how COVID may evolve.

                        CLIMATE CHANGE RAMIFICATIONS

                        The Company recognises that climate change could have meaningful impacts on the financial performance of the Group
                        over time. Based on scenarios planning, Sims has refined its understanding of key physical and transition risks and




For personal use only
                        opportunities related to climate change, consistent with the Task Force on Climate-Related Financial Disclosures TCFD
                        recommendations. The Company has commenced actions to mitigate their impact, where possible. The increased
                        frequency and severity of climate change-related natural disasters could affect not only our operations but also our value
                        chain. The transition to lower carbon economies presents both risks and opportunities for Sims.

                        Some of the key risks identified relate to increased, and more extreme, weather events and the uncertainties linked to
                        climate-related policies, availability and cost of technologies impacting:
                             health and safety issues for employees operating on sites (extreme temperatures and extreme rain);
                             docks, material handling and the transportation of products not only on site but also in our upstream and
                                 downstream value chain (extreme temperatures, extreme rain and rising sea levels);
                             damage to assets and relocation of infrastructure (flooding, cyclones, rising sea levels)
                             access to a reliable supply of electricity (extended heat waves);
                             market competitiveness (climate change policies)
                             operating costs and capital investitures (investments to decarbonize operations)

                        These risks are currently not expected to have a material impact on the Company’s financial performance. However,
                        effective from July 2019, all capex approvals over a threshold value require consideration of the impact of climate change
                        as standard practice.

                        (d) Global Enterprise Resource Planning (“ERP”) software implementation costs

                        During the six-month period ended 31 December 2021, the Group continued to progress with its global ERP
                        implementation and accounted for the costs associated with incurred in accordance with the revised accounting policy
                        adopted on 30 June 2021. The revised policy was adopted in response to the IFRS Interpretations Committee
                        (”IFRIC”) agenda decision published in April 2021 in relation to configuration and customization costs in a software
                        as a service (“SaaS”) arrangement.

                        The impact on the Group’s Half Year financial statements are as follows:

                                During the six-month period to 31 December 2021, A$23.7 million of costs incurred to date relating to the
                                 Global ERP implementation were charged to profit or loss. This was composed of A$2.8 million of employee
                                 benefits expense and A$20.9 million of other expenses, which primarily include professional fees incurred
                                 during development of the software.

                                Costs incurred in the prior corresponding period (six months ending 31 December 2020) totaled A$19.3 million
                                 and were recognised in accordance with the Group’s previous accounting policy (e.g., capitalised and
                                 recognised as an intangible asset). The costs were recognised in the Income Statement in accordance with the
                                 Group’s revised accounting policy in the Group’s annual financial report for the year ended 30 June 2021.

                                The consolidated statement of cash flows includes payments to suppliers and employees relating to the Global
                                 ERP implementation within cash flows from operating activities. Amounts recognised in the prior
                                 corresponding period were recognised as investing activities.
