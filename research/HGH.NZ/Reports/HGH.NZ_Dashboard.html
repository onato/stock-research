DCF Valuation — Book-Value / ROE Model

⚠

Methodology Note: Book-Value/ROE Model, Not an FCF DCF

Heartland is a deposit-funded bank. Reported operating cash flow is dominated by loan-book and deposit-flow timing (FY2024 OCF was +$928.4m on deposit inflows outpacing loan growth; FY2021 OCF was −$339.5m, the mirror image) and has no relationship to distributable owner earnings. There is also no meaningful net-debt bridge for a bank — customer deposits fund the lending book and are not financing debt to net against equity. For these reasons this valuation uses a book-value compounding model: intrinsic value = PV of projected dividends (Years 1-5, discounted at Cost of Equity) + PV of a Year-5 exit value equal to projected book value per share × an assumed exit Price-to-Book multiple.

Sanity check: PASSED. The base case's implied P/B of intrinsic value (0.741x) sits below both the current traded multiple (0.879x) and the stock's own FY2021-FY2025 historical P/B range (0.62x–1.58x) — the model does not require multiple expansion beyond current trading levels to produce its output.

Framing: this is an income/recovery story whose value hinges on whether ROE can climb back toward Heartland's ~13.1% cost of equity — not a multi-bagger growth story. The base case here is a modest, honest downside (−15.7%) reflecting ROE (7.3% at 1H2026, underlying) still sitting well below cost of equity even after the post-FY2025 recovery.
