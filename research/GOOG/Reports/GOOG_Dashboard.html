Company Overview

Alphabet Inc. is the parent company of Google, the world's dominant search engine, and a diversified technology conglomerate operating across digital advertising, cloud computing, hardware, and autonomous vehicles. Its Google Services segment generated $342.7B of FY2025 revenue (85% of total), while Google Cloud contributed $58.7B (15%) and is accelerating rapidly. Alphabet crossed $400B in annual revenue for the first time in FY2025 ($402.8B), with FY2025 operating income of $129.0B (32% margin).

Q1 2026 revenue was $109.9B (+22% YoY) with a 36.1% operating margin, demonstrating continued operating leverage even as AI infrastructure capex ramps dramatically toward the guided $180-190B in FY2026.

Bull Case

  • Google Cloud at inflection: Q1 2026 +63% YoY, $240B contracted backlog, Vertex AI/TPU differentiation — Cloud is the most visible growth catalyst and is barely in its third year of profitability.
  • AI Overviews proving bear case wrong: Search grew 19% YoY in Q1 2026 despite ChatGPT's rise; AI Mode monetizes at parity with traditional Search; 2.5B monthly users expand the market.
  • $180-190B capex builds an infrastructure moat that benefits Search, Cloud, YouTube, and Gemini simultaneously — competitors face a multi-year lag to match compute scale.
  • Antitrust outcomes were less severe than feared: Chrome and Android not ordered divested; no structural Search breakup.
  • Waymo optionality: ~$110B standalone valuation, 400K weekly rides, global expansion underway — not in the DCF, ~$9/share embedded option value.

Bear Case

  • $180-190B FY2026 capex is historically unprecedented for Alphabet (44%+ of revenue); if AI demand disappoints, the capital cycle will take years to unwind and will suppress FCF and ROIC.
  • AI is structurally disintermediating the link-based web: AI Overviews reduced click-through rates to publishers 58%; OpenAI and Perplexity are training the next generation to bypass Google.
  • Antitrust overhang remains: Search remedy under appeal; Ad Tech remedy pending; EU DMA enforcement ongoing; private publisher litigation escalating.
  • AI talent attrition (Shazeer to OpenAI, Jumper to Anthropic) signals frontier labs increasingly outcompete Alphabet for the best researchers.
  • Advertising concentration: 84% of revenue; Amazon retail media ($50B+/year) now directly competes for performance advertising dollars.

The AI CapEx Super-Cycle — Central Investment Thesis

CapEx vs. Operating Cash Flow vs. Free Cash Flow

AI CapEx Super-Cycle: CapEx jumped from $32B (FY2023) to $52B (FY2024) to $91B (FY2025), with $180-190B guided for FY2026 and FY2027 expected higher still. Operating Cash Flow continues to grow strongly (+62% from FY2023 to FY2025), but FCF is compressed as CapEx outpaces OCF growth. FCF is expected to trough in FY2026-27 before recovering as AI infrastructure begins monetizing across Search, Cloud, and Gemini.

Model approach & sanity-check correction: The initial DCF (pre-fix) produced a base intrinsic value of $193.85/share implying a P/E of 17.7x — below Alphabet's 10-year trough P/E of 19.0x (the 2022 ad-recession low). No plausible non-catastrophic scenario justifies a sub-trough multiple for a business growing revenue 10-14% with a net-cash balance sheet. The sanity check tripped on two compounding errors: (1) the FCF path front-loaded full $185-200B capex with insufficient OCF growth, yielding Year 1 FCF of only $8B — mathematically inconsistent with Q1 2026 OCF of $45.8B already annualizing to ~$183B; (2) WACC of 9.5% was too punitive for a net-cash mega-cap with ~1.0 beta. The corrected model smooths the FCF trough (Year 1 FCF $29B, grounded in the Q1 2026 OCF run-rate), lowers WACC to 8.5% (retaining a 0.5% antitrust/execution risk premium), and adds an 18x EV/EBITDA exit-multiple cap anchored to the 10-year historical average. The corrected base IV of $267/share implies P/E 24.5x and EV/EBITDA 19.2x — squarely within Alphabet's historical range and appropriate for a wide-moat franchise. The model still shows ~23% downside from the current $346 price, with the gap attributable to the market pricing in faster AI monetization (Cloud, AI Mode ads, Gemini subscriptions) and option value in Waymo (~$9/share at $110B standalone valuation) and Gemini as a platform not yet captured in near-term FCF.
