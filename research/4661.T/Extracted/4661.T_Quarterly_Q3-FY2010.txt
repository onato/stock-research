Qualitative Information, Financial Statements and Other Data
 1. Qualitative Information on Consolidated Operating Results
          The challenging operating environment remained in force during the nine months ended December 31, 2009, as
  the Japanese government made an official “declaration of deflation,” reflecting slack consumer spending due to the
  deterioration of employment conditions and decreased income. In addition, the numbers of domestic travelers and
  overseas visitors to Japan decreased as a result, in large part, of an outbreak of novel influenza A (H1N1).
          Under these circumstances, net sales, operating income and ordinary income for the OLC Group decreased for a
  number of reasons, including the fact that the previous year was Tokyo Disney Resort 25th Anniversary, among others.
  Net sales stood at ¥286,095 million, a 4.8% decrease compared with the same period of the previous fiscal year, while
  operating income and ordinary income was ¥39,739 million (down 6.0%) and ¥39,018 million (down 6.7%) respectively.
           On the other hand, net income stood at a record high level of ¥25,296 million (up 5.8%) due to various factors,
  including a decrease in income taxes resulting from an expected decrease in taxable income following the transfer of the
  retail business segment. This figure was achieved despite a posting of extraordinary loss resulting from the transfer.

   [Theme Park Segment] Tokyo Disneyland, Tokyo DisneySea and others
   Net sales and operating income decreased as theme park attendance fell below the level of the same period of the
   previous fiscal year due to various reasons, including the fact that Tokyo Disney Resort 25th Anniversary was
   celebrated in the previous year.

   Net Sales   ¥221,554 million (down 6.1% from the same period of the previous fiscal year)
           During the period under review, we opened new attractions in each of our two theme parks. “Monsters, Inc. ‘Ride
   & Go Seek!’” was opened in April at Tokyo Disneyland and “Turtle Talk” was opened in October at Tokyo DisneySea,
   with both proving extremely popular, especially among families. Furthermore, as part of our special events program for
   the two theme parks, in addition to “Disney’s Halloween,” which was this year also held at Tokyo DisneySea for the
   first time ever, and other events, we held special Christmas-themed events starting in November.
          Total attendance at the two theme parks for this period fell below the level of the same period of the previous
   fiscal year for reasons that, among others, included the fact that Tokyo Disney Resort 25th Anniversary was celebrated
   in the previous year.
          In addition, net sales per guest at theme parks remained strong, maintaining almost the same level as the previous
   fiscal year when net sales per guest were strong due to the 25th Anniversary. Ticket receipts remained essentially
   unchanged from the same period of the previous fiscal year. While sales of “Duffy” products sold exclusively at Tokyo
   DisneySea remained strong, overall merchandise sales were slightly below the level of the same period of the previous
   fiscal year due to the previous year having been the 25th Anniversary, among other reasons. Food and beverage sales
   were slightly above the level of the same period of the previous fiscal year due to various factors, including strong
   wagon sales.
         Owing to the factors described above, net sales for the theme park segment decreased as a whole.

  Operating Income      ¥32,031 million (down 13.2%)
         Operating income decreased due to various factors, including a decrease in net sales, despite the decrease in the
  cost of merchandise ratio and in depreciation and amortization and others.

  [Hotel Business Segment] Tokyo Disneyland Hotel, Tokyo DisneySea Hotel MiraCosta and others
  Despite a decrease in net sales, operating income increased as a result of various factors, including a decrease in
  preparation expenses prior to the opening of Tokyo Disneyland Hotel.

  Net Sales   ¥34,894 million (down 0.4.%)
        As a standard practice for all hotel guests, we have been offering the “Tokyo Disney Resort Multi-day Passport
  Special” at the three Disney hotels for a year now, with sales starting on April 1, 2009. In addition, we have been
  implementing the “Tokyo Disneyland ‘Happy 15’ Entry” program, under which hotel guests are allowed to enter Tokyo
  Disneyland 15 minutes earlier during the period from September 1, 2009 to March 31, 2010.
         However, occupancy rates for each hotel fell below those of the same period of the previous fiscal year and
  overall net sales declined for a number of reasons, including external factors, such as the outbreak of novel influenza A
  (H1N1), and the fact that the previous year was the 25th Anniversary.


  Operating Income      ¥6,887 million (up 23.6%)
        Despite a decrease in net sales, operating income increased due to various factors, including a decrease in
  preparation expenses prior to the opening of Tokyo Disneyland Hotel.

  [Retail Business Segment] The Disney Store
  Despite a decrease in net sales, operating income increased as a result of various factors, including efforts to reduce
  fixed expenses.

  Net Sales   ¥11,241 million (down 6.2%)
        At the Disney Store, we created appealing sales outlets that reflected seasonal demands with “The Resort,” a
  program featuring a wide variety of items useful at resorts and other tourist destinations, as well as the rollout of
  Halloween- and Christmas-themed products. However, net sales dropped amid a further decline in the economic
  environment.
        Regarding the number of stores, we opened stores at Gotemba Premium Outlets and the AEON Tsuchiura
  shopping center and closed four stores, leaving us with a total of 55 stores as of December 31, 2009.


  Operating Income      ¥170 million (up 401.5％)
         Despite a decrease in net sales, operating income increased as a result of a reduction in fixed expenses, including
  store personnel expenses.

  [Other Business Segment] Ikspiari, Cirque du Soleil Theatre Tokyo and others
  Net sales and operating income increased due to various factors, including full-year operation of Cirque du Soleil
  Theatre Tokyo and a decrease in preparation expenses prior to the opening of the theatre.

  Net Sales   ¥18,405 million (up 5.8%)
         At Ikspiari, the Company held events including “Ikspiari Halloween 2009” and “Ikspiari Pure White Christmas”
  in addition to “ZED SUMMER FESTA @ IKSPIARI,” an event linked to Cirque du Soleil Theatre Tokyo.
        Furthermore, overall net sales increased as a result of full-year operation of Cirque du Soleil Theatre Tokyo,
  which was opened to the public on October 1, 2008.


  Operating Income      ¥423 million (an improvement of ¥755 million)
        Operating income improved due to various factors, including a decrease in preparation expenses prior to the
  opening of Cirque du Soleil Theatre Tokyo.

3. Qualitative Information on Projections for Consolidated Business Results
         Compared with the projections for consolidated business results announced on November 5, 2009, the actual
  results for the nine months ended December 31, 2009 showed an increase in net sales and operating income primarily
  around the theme park segment. This was due to various factors, including the fact that net sales per guest in the theme
  park segment continued to remain at the same high level as the previous year, exceeding our projection.
         However, taking into account the impact of factors such as weather risk on attendance at the theme parks during
  the fourth quarter, we are not presently changing our projections for the consolidated full-year business results.
          In the theme park segment, net sales tend to be lower and fixed expenses tend to be higher during the fourth
  quarter. This is the result of various factors, including the fact that attendance at the theme parks during the fourth
  quarter is usually lower than other quarters for seasonal reasons and that we conduct intensive maintenance of our
  attractions during this period to take advantage of the shorter opening hours of the theme parks.
         Actual results could differ materially from projections due to the influence of various factors, including economic
  conditions, changes in consumer spending habits, the weather, and natural or other disasters. For further information on
  business risks, please refer to the Company’s “Consolidated Financial Statements for the Fiscal Year Ended March 31,
  2009,” released on June 26, 2009.
