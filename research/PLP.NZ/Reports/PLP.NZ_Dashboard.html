Booster Private Land and Property Fund (PLP.NZ)

NZX-listed unit trust holding 100% of the unlisted Private Land & Property Portfolio (rural/industrial NZ real estate) · FY end 31 March · NZD

⚠ Why This Fund Is Structurally Overvalued at the Current Price

The units trade at roughly parity to (slightly above) NAV ($1.304 vs an $1.3010 unit price at 18-Jun-2026) — but that NAV has declined for three consecutive fiscal years since its FY2023 peak of $1.4002. The usual listed-fund thesis of "buy the discount to NAV and capture convergence" simply does not exist here: there is no discount to close.

The on-market price is not doing genuine price discovery. Approximately 95.5% of units are held by four related Booster vehicles — Booster KiwiSaver Scheme 56.33%, Asset Custodian Nominees 21.67%, Booster SuperScheme 11.18%, Booster Investment Scheme 6.32% — leaving a genuine independent free float of only ~4.5%. A price set by that sliver of thin trading is not a market test of the manager's own NAV.

This is not a discount-rate artifact. Even at a bare 9% cost of equity — risk-free rate plus a property equity risk premium with every structural premium (illiquidity, valuation opacity, concentration, agricultural risk) stripped out — the base-case value is only about $1.04, still roughly 20% below the $1.304 market price. The negative verdict comes from the gap between the fund's delivered return stream and any defensible required return, not from premium build-up.

Live governance risk: the FMA has filed civil proceedings against the manager (BIML) and five senior executives/directors — 75 causes of action over historical related-party dealings (2017-2023) feeding the Booster Wine Group — with trial set for February 2027. NAV itself is a Level 2 fair value: PLPP's holdings are valued at a redemption price quoted, assessed and ultimately adopted by the Manager, informed by (but not equal to) independent property valuations — flagged as a key audit matter every year.
