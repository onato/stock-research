Read this before the numbers below BIF trades essentially AT NAV (~1.00x) — unlike listed VC/PE trusts elsewhere, which typically trade at a 0.6–0.9x discount to NAV — because units are struck by the Manager on application/redemption rather than priced by an order book, so there is no discount-to-NAV margin of safety to exploit. Separately, the Manager's own most recent unit issue (3-Jul-2026 at $1.4759) priced 2.3% below the audited 31-Mar-2026 NAV of $1.5114, while the market quote sits at $1.508 — i.e. the market quote is now roughly 2.2% above the Manager's own latest mark.

Capital Deployed vs Cash Returned — The Core Bear Point

Cumulative Capital Deployed vs Distributions Received ($m)

Across five full years the Fund has deployed roughly $20.3m of cumulative acquisitions into its portfolio, against a single $568k distribution received — in FY2022 only, and nil every year since. The entire reported NAV gain since is unrealised, manager-marked appreciation.

DCF-Equivalent Valuation: NAV Compounding Model

⚠
Model approach — why this is NOT an FCF DCF Standard P/E, EV/EBITDA and P/Sales multiples are meaningless for a fund with no revenue or earnings from operations, so a conventional free-cash-flow DCF was rejected outright. Instead this section uses a NAV-per-unit compounding model with an exit price-to-NAV multiple, discounted at a 20% cost of equity built from a 4.5% NZ risk-free rate + 6.0% equity risk premium + 2.5% Level-3 manager-marking premium + 2.0% zero-realisations premium + 2.5% illiquidity/tiny-free-float premium + 2.5% early-stage venture total-loss-risk premium. The sanity check below tripped deliberately: the base-case intrinsic value of $0.691 is 54% below the $1.508 market price — that gap is the finding, not a modelling error. Read this as: BIF is an illiquid, income-free, exit-dependent venture exposure currently priced at ~1.0x NAV, not a discounted asset play.
