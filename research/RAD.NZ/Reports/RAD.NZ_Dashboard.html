Leverage & Lease Load

The defining balance-sheet feature: roughly half the portfolio is leased, so total debt is bank borrowings PLUS a large IFRS-16 lease liability

Total Debt vs Shareholders' Equity

TotalDebt is bank borrowings + IFRS-16 lease liabilities combined (FY2026: NZ$71.6m bank + NZ$141.5m lease = $213.1m). FY2020 is the IFRS-16 adoption year -- lease liabilities jumped from $0 to $185m, not a real 4x acquisition of debt.

Reported FCF vs Owner FCF (DCF basis)

Reported FreeCashFlow (OCF less capex) EXCLUDES lease principal and double-excludes lease interest handling; Owner FCF (from the DCF JSON) is the corrected basis: FY2026 $9.05m reported vs $10.34m owner FCF. Owner FCF history spans two hugely negative acquisition-capex years (FY2022, FY2023) -- shown for audit, not trend-fitting.

Operating Cash Flow vs CapEx

FY2022/FY2023 capex spikes are acquisition-heavy (facility purchases), not maintenance capex; the DCF normalizes base-case capex to ~3% of revenue.

Balance Sheet & Capital

IFRS-16 lease adoption (FY2020) makes the raw asset/equity history discontinuous -- read FY2021 onward as the clean base

Shareholders' Equity

FY2022 jump ($24.1m to $70.1m) is a capital raise, not organic retained-earnings growth; equity CAGRs are not used as a DCF growth anchor for this reason.

Cash & Equivalents

Cash is run lean (leverage-funded growth model); watch alongside the ASB facility headroom, not in isolation.

Shares Outstanding & Dividend per Share

14:1 share split on 8 Dec 2020: FY2019/FY2020 carry the pre-split 12.5m count. Do not read a continuous per-share trend across the FY2020-FY2021 boundary -- the FY2021 jump to 175m is the split, not dilution.
