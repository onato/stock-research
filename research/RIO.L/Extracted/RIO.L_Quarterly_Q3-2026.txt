Rio Tinto releases third quarter 2026 production results

14 October 2026
Strong operational performance at Oyu Tolgoi copper and Pilbara iron ore
Rio Tinto Chief Executive Simon Trott said: “We delivered strong operational performance in the third quarter.
Oyu Tolgoi copper production increased 29% year-to-date, with the operation running at a ~450 kt annualised
rate in the quarter, and global iron ore sales are up 3% year-to-date. The Simandou iron ore mine hit an
annualised production run-rate of ~10 Mtpa (100% basis) in the quarter as the project continues to ramp up.
“We are finishing the year with momentum, with significant opportunities to accelerate improvements in safety,
productivity and reliability through implementation of our operating system. We remain on track for a $1.8 billion
annualised productivity benefit run-rate by the end of 2026. Our major projects remain on schedule and budget,
underwriting our growth profile to 2030.
“We made further significant steps in decarbonising our business in the third quarter, with our Pacific Aluminium
smelter repowering arrangements now well progressed.
“Today, we announce that we have achieved more than $2 billion1 of capital release year-to-date, as we actively
manage our portfolio with discipline to maximise long-term value, as part of the $5 to 10 billion capital release
program.”
1. Executive Summary
     • Operational excellence: ~1% YTD increase in copper equivalent (CuEq2) production, driven by growth
       from Oyu Tolgoi copper and Pilbara iron ore.
     • Copper: Oyu Tolgoi production increased by 27% YoY in Q3 and 29% YTD. At Kennecott, we successfully
       completed the rebuild of the flash converting furnace at end-August ahead of plan.
     • Iron ore: Q3 global iron ore sales were 90.8 Mt, up 3% YoY, while Pilbara sales increased 2% YoY.
       Simandou hit an annualised production run-rate of ~10 Mtpa (100% basis) in Q3 as the project ramps up
       and remains on track to reach a 60 Mtpa production run-rate during H2 20283.
     • Aluminium: Consistent, stable performance across the full operating system. Pacific smelter repowering
       arrangements are now well progressed, following the Tomago and Bell Bay power agreements in Q3.
     • Lithium: Production up 33% YTD despite adverse weather impacting Q3 operations. Fenix 1B ramp-up
       continues to progress well.

3. Group update
Expenditure on exploration and evaluation
     •     YTD pre-tax and pre-divestment expenditure on exploration and evaluation charged to the Income
           Statement was $692 million compared with $537 million in 2025. Approximately 50% of the spend was
           by the Copper product group, 28% by central exploration and other operations, and 22% by the Iron
           Ore product group.
     •     Copper accounts for the majority of total expenditure on exploration and evaluation (included in the
           Copper product group and central exploration).
Capital release
We are systematically redeploying capital into higher-returning opportunities. To date, we have entered
arrangements to release more than US$2 billion1 for redeployment through our capital allocation framework.
This comprises:
     ◦     In July, we signed an agreement with Yindjibarndi WaterCo for the proposed sale of the Dampier
           Seawater Desalination Plant in Western Australia.
     ◦     We have agreed the sale of land in Kitimat, British Columbia, Canada, to LNG Canada for their Phase II
           project. Completion expected in Q4.
     ◦     We have entered into a non-binding arrangement for a lithium sales contract with a pre-payment
           component.
     ◦     We have completed a number of smaller non-core asset disposals as part of our ongoing portfolio
           optimisation.
We are progressing other opportunities, with further announcements expected in 2026 as we deliver the initial
~$5 billion of the $5–10 billion capital release program announced at Capital Markets Day 2025.

Middle East conflict
Ongoing disruptions in the Middle East continued to drive volatility in energy and freight markets. Operational
impacts to Rio Tinto remained limited, with no material disruption to production or outbound supply chains. Rio
Tinto’s fuel availability remained adequate, supported by inventory buffers and alternative sourcing.
Conditions in the Strait of Hormuz remain highly volatile, and we continue to monitor developments and
maintain contingency plans for further disruption.

4. Our markets
Global economy: global growth moderated slightly through Q3 due to persistent geopolitical and energy
market uncertainty. Developed market activity remained strong, supported by ongoing AI-linked investment
and resilient manufacturing output.

Chinese economy: domestic economic activity moderated in Q3, with strong export demand and
manufacturing activity partly offset by weaker domestic consumption and investment. August’s industrial
production grew 5% YoY, driven by a 17% YoY increase in high-tech manufacturing. Policy support remained
focused on infrastructure, including grid and AI development.

US economy: growth remained resilient, supported by AI-related investment, broader business spending
and consumer demand. Inflation remained elevated, prompting the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates by
25 basis points in September.

Copper
    •    London Metal Exchange (LME) copper reached a record high of $6.74/lb ($14,850/t) in mid-August,
         supported by constrained mine supply, strong investor flows and optimism around AI-driven
         electricity demand. Prices averaged $6.40/lb ($14,110/t) for the quarter.
    •    Copper mine supply remained constrained during Q3, with major storms in Chile adding to existing
         supply challenges. DRC production remained relatively resilient despite concerns around sulphur
         and sulphuric acid availability.
    •    Uncertainty around potential US tariffs on refined copper cathode imports persisted through Q3,
         sustaining a premium between Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME) and LME prices and driving
         continued US imports above underlying demand.
    •    The copper concentrate market remained extremely tight in Q3, with spot treatment and refining
         charges ending the quarter at a record low of -$267/t. High sulphuric acid prices are helping to offset
         pressure on smelter economics.
Iron ore
    •    During Q3, iron ore prices were sustained in a narrow band around their $97/dmt 61% delivered
         (CFR) average. Higher seaborne freight costs compressed producer netbacks, particularly for longer
         haul Atlantic supply. Seaborne lump premiums rebounded by 33% QoQ, realigning to long-term
         historical average levels and the marginal cost of sintering in China.
    •    China's finished steel consumption is estimated to have declined ~4% QoQ and ~2% YoY in Q3,
         while steel exports increased 3% YoY to a 122 Mt annualised rate, partly offsetting weaker domestic
         demand and supporting crude steel and pig iron production.
    •    Seaborne iron ore supply declined 1% QoQ in Q3 in response to elevated operating and freight
         costs putting pressure on marginal supply. Of note, long-haul shipments outside Australia and
         Guinea declined by an estimated 8% MoM in September.
    •    China's domestic iron ore production declined 8% YoY in Q3, affected by flooding, safety inspections
         and scheduled maintenance.

Aluminium
    •    LME aluminium averaged $3,226/t in Q3, 23% above the 2025 average but below the four-year high
         reached in Q2.
    •    Middle East supply saw some recovery through Q3, with most of the smelting capacity curtailed
         earlier in the year remaining offline, while Chinese production remained close to its 45 Mt capacity
         cap.
    •    The global aluminium market remained in deficit as ex-China demand showed early signs of
         recovery and reported inventories continued to decline. Regional physical premiums remained
         elevated, reflecting constrained metal availability and higher freight costs.
