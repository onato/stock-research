                The MTU Group




Interim Group management report

The MTU Group
                                                                                                                MTU’s portfolio covers the entire lifecycle of commercial and military aircraft engines and
                                                                                                                aero-derivative industrial gas turbines. The company’s activities range from develop-
                                                                                                                ment, manufacturing and marketing to maintenance.

                                                                                                                The company possesses technical expertise in low-pressure turbines, high-pressure
                                                                                                                compressors and turbine center frames, as well as the relevant repair and manufacturing
                                                                                                                techniques. Within the scope of risk and revenue sharing partnerships, MTU is involved
                                                                                                                in key technology programs at both national and international level, cooperating with
                                                                                                                some of the industry’s biggest names, such as GE Aerospace, Pratt & Whitney and Rolls-
                                                                                                                Royce.

                                                                                                                The Group also performs maintenance services for commercial aircraft engines. In the
                                                                                                                military business, MTU has been a system partner to the German armed forces for many
                                                                                                                decades.

                                                                                                                MTU is active in two segments: the OEM business (original equipment manufacturing)
                                                                                                                and the MRO business (maintenance, repair and overhaul). The OEM segment consoli-
                                                                                                                dates the commercial new and spare parts business and the entire military business. The
                                                                                                                MRO segment covers the commercial repair and maintenance business.

                                                                                                                Research and development
                                                                                                                Technology in the aviation industry advances at a very rapid pace, requiring constant
                                                                                                                innovation. In addition to ongoing developments in the commercial and military engine
                                                                                                                programs currently overseen by MTU, the Group is also involved in development pro-
                                                                                                                grams for future engine programs and technology.

Economic report                                                                                                 Macroeconomic conditions
                                                                                                                The conflict in the Middle East had a significant impact on global economic develop-
                                                                                                                ment during the first half of 2026. While the global economy proved to be stronger than
                                                                                                                anticipated early in the year, the closure of the Strait of Hormuz between March and June
                                                                                                                restricted almost one fifth of the world’s oil supplies and has since resulted in a signifi-
                                                                                                                cant rise in energy prices. The resulting increase in oil prices has heightened inflationary
                                                                                                                pressure and dampened economic activity across the globe. However, shipping traffic
                                                                                                                through the Persian Golf has begun to recover following a ceasefire and the signing of
                                                                                                                a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) between the USA and Iran on June 17. At the
                                                                                                                same time, the price for Brent Crude dropped to close to U.S.$ 70 per barrel at the end
                                                                                                                of June after rising temporarily to over U.S.$ 130 per barrel due to fears over supply
                                                                                                                shortages.

                                                                                                                The impact of the conflict in the Middle East was not fully apparent to begin with, though
                                                                                                                its effects became significantly clearer as the conflict progressed. Economic development
                                                                                                                in the eurozone weakened during the first quarter of 2026. Gross domestic product
                                                                                                                (GDP) fell by 0.2% compared to the previous quarter (source: Eurostat). Causing a rise in
                                                                                                                energy prices, lower demand and a decline in corporate sentiment, the ongoing conflict
                                                                                                                put a strain on the economy. The European Central Bank responded and increased its key
                                                                                                                interest rate for the eurozone for the first time since September 2023, raising it to the
                                                                                                                current rate of 2.25%. Despite the German economy achieving 0.3% growth in the first
                                                                                                                quarter compared to the previous quarter, the ongoing escalation of the conflict, rising
                                                                                                                energy prices and higher inflation in April had a negative impact on both business and
                                                                                                                consumer confidence (source: Eurostat).

                                                                                                                The US and Chinese economic areas proved to be more resilient. In the USA, gross
                                                                                                                domestic product increased by 0.4% compared to the previous quarter (source: Eu-
                                                                                                                rostat). Bolstered by higher investments, a rise in exports and greater public and private
                                                                                                                demand, the economy was able to more or less offset the effects of the war. The US
                                                                                                                Federal Reserve (FED) kept its base rate between 3.5% and 3.75% throughout the entire
                                                                                                                first half of 2026 (source: FED). In the first quarter of the year, China’s GDP grew by 1.3%
                                                                                                                compared to the previous quarter, supported in particular by industrial production. High-
                                                                                                                tech production grew at a rate in the double digits (source: National Bureau of Statistics
                                                                                                                of China). This put the growth rate within the expected range.

                                                                                                                       Macroeconomic conditions
                                                                                                                       In this challenging and unstable environment, both the IMF and OECD have recently low-
                                                                                                                       ered their global economic growth forecasts for 2026. According to its forecast published
                                                                                                                       in April, the IMF anticipates global growth to fall to 3.1% in 2026 after reaching 3.5% in
                                                                                                                       2025. Prior to its correction, the IMF had assumed robust growth of 3.3.%. The conflict
                                                                                                                       in the Middle East is having a significant dampening effect on the positive impetus
                                                                                                                       being generated by technology-related investments. In June, the OECD revised its global
                                                                                                                       growth forecasts even further downwards to 2.8% for 2026.

                                                                                                                       According to the OECD’s latest forecasts, growth in the USA has slowed to 2.0%, which
                                                                                                                       0.1% lower than its 2025 figure. In the eurozone, growth may slow to 0.8% after reaching
                                                                                                                       1.4% in the previous year. As a result of this, Europe has tightened its monetary policy.
                                                                                                                       After reaching 0.5% in 2025, growth of 0.7% is expected in Germany in 2026, bolstered by
                                                                                                                       an expansive fiscal policy. China’s growth rate may slow from 2025’s rate of 5.0% to 4.5%
                                                                                                                       this year. The Chinese economy is proving to be more resilient than anticipated. Howev-
                                                                                                                       er, its recovery remains unsteady with weak domestic demand, particularly in the real
                                                                                                                       estate sector, still lagging behind its export-driven growth.

                                                                                                                       Crude oil prices are expected to remain high and prone to fluctuation. This will contin-
                                                                                                                       ue until a permanent peace settlement has been found for the conflict in Iran. The US
                                                                                                                       energy authority EIA assumes that the oil supply, which has been negatively impacted
                                                                                                                       by the conflict at the Strait of Hormuz, will begin to ramp up again in the third quarter,
                                                                                                                       normalizing by early 2027. As such, the price for Brent is expected to be around U.S.$95
                                                                                                                       per barrel in 2026.

    non-speculative financial investments as part of liquidity management.

    est-bearing sales financing.

    aperiodic capital flows are eliminated in the calculation of free cash flow (adjusted).
