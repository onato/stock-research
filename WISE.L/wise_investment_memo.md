## 1) Executive Summary

Wise is evolving into a **global payments utility with an embedded float base**, executing a **Costco-style strategy**: *intentionally cutting take rate to accelerate volume and balances, while expanding margins via operating leverage.*

Two engines are increasingly visible:

1) **Retail flywheel (Costco-like):** price leadership → customer growth → higher holdings → lower unit costs → further price cuts (with margin expansion).  
2) **Wise Platform (infrastructure-like):** B2B rails for banks/partners to replace expensive legacy cross-border infrastructure (SWIFT/correspondent banking), with higher switching costs and potentially higher margins.

**Key conclusion:** Wise should be valued primarily as a **long-duration compounder**. Interest income above 1% is not the core thesis, but it is **not negligible** and should be treated as **temporary cash-flow uplift / option value** rather than ignored.

## 2) What Wise Does (in one paragraph)

Wise provides low-cost cross-border money movement and multi-currency accounts. Its edge is operational: scale-driven netting, local rails, and a compliance infrastructure that lowers per-transaction costs. Wise is increasingly becoming a **primary account** for customers (holdings growing faster than customers), which creates a durable float base. Wise Platform extends the same rails to banks and institutions.

## 3) The Core Thesis (Why this is a Costco analogue)

### 3.1 The Costco playbook

Wise deliberately lowers take rate (headline price) to:

- increase customer acquisition
- increase usage frequency
- increase customer holdings (float)
- improve netting efficiency and unit economics

**Signal of real moat:** *take rate down while margins up.*

This is the Costco signature: low visible margin, high economic strength, durable compounding.

### 3.2 What makes Wise stronger than a pure Costco analogue

Wise has a second engine:

- **Platform** monetizes cost savings captured from banks (SWIFT replacement economics)
- switching costs and integration lock-in increase durability
- platform mix can raise blended margins over time

## 6) Interest Income: Correct Treatment (not ignored, not capitalized)

Wise earns interest on customer balances. Management frames this using APMs:

- **Underlying interest income:** first ~1% yield retained  
- **Excess interest income:** interest above 1% (intended to be competed away / shared)  

### 6.1 Key insight

Balance growth can offset yield compression.

If holdings grow ~30%+ while yield falls ~20–25%, total interest income can remain flat.

### 6.2 Regulatory nuance

Wise cannot always pay interest directly to customers in all jurisdictions (non-bank status). That can create **temporary retention** above 1% in some regions. However, competitive pressure and product evolution still push Wise toward pass-through over time.

### 6.3 Valuation rule

- Treat first 1% as **structural** (capitalized)  
- Treat >1% as **temporary uplift** (partially valued, discounted)

## 7) Platform Thesis (the convexity)

### 7.1 Why Platform matters

Banks pay large cross-border spreads due to:

- SWIFT fees and intermediaries
- compliance duplication
- slow settlement and poor FX pricing

Wise Platform can reduce total cost, creating margin surplus to share.

### 7.2 What to watch

- Platform revenue disclosure (if improved)
- volume growth via partners
- evidence of retention and pricing stability

### 7.3 Long-term implication

If Platform reaches ~50% of business with higher margins:

- blended margins expand
- intrinsic CAGR increases by ~1–3% over long horizon
